## Execution contract

One row is a bounded unit for a fresh context, ending in the listed output and
an explicit answer to its question. Read the brief and constraints, the row's
accepted dependencies, relevant repository instructions, then the necessary
sources. Broad surveys are bounded by the first-wave decisions they inform;
additional interesting topics become proposed follow-up rows.

Only the coordinator changes central state, the Q register, row status and the
outline trace. Workers own their assigned note or isolated spike path. Before
dispatch, the coordinator records an assignment in the change log: unit,
agent/session identifier, baseline, allowed writes, inputs, dependencies, output
and stop conditions. An assignment authorizes only its recorded scope; a worker does not infer
additional scope from this file.
Workers report conflicts and new questions instead of silently changing scope.
A reviewer checks evidence and completion before the coordinator accepts a row.

A and B permit parallel research after the current review. After C1, E1 informs
C2 before the portfolio checkpoint. D, E, F and G may overlap only when their
listed prerequisites are accepted. H2 follows the complete research/design set.
The note numbers are reserved identifiers, not a serial execution order.

A row is complete when its output answers the question, records limitations,
emits candidate requirements or explicitly emits none, and identifies unresolved
questions with owners. A negative or inaccessible finding can complete an audit
row; it cannot discharge dependent implementation or scientific claims. If it
blocks a later row, record that dependency explicitly. Do not mark a row complete
merely because a worker ran out of time.

Keep source citations and measured results separate from design proposals.
Acceptance evidence for research is not release evidence. Preserve existing
`DF-*`, `BL-*`, requirement and ADR identities; do not create implementation
obligations in the old registers during this upstream phase.
