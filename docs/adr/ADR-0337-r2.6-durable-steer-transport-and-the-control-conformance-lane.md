# ADR-0337: R2.6 durable steer transport — `steer` rides the wakeup seam, the `manual{principal}` admission arm, and the staged-interpreter conformance lane

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-06
- **Ticket:** R2.6
- **Requirement ids:** DF-S2.11-1 · DF-S1.20-1 · DF-S2.8-1 (member a's
  steer leg) · BL-14 · OQ-316 · R-2.6.1 · R-2.6.2
- **Spec:** §5e.1 (`control_strategy`, `SteerMode`/`ConcurrentInput`,
  staged variants) · §5e.2 (the stop protocol) · §5d.4/ADR-0131
  (wakeup subscriptions, occurrences, `deliver_after`) · ADR-0130
  (restart/takeover recovery) · ADR-0273 (the `pending_steer` loss
  window) · ADR-0181 (the `plugin_abi/1` conformance fixture) ·
  ADR-0132 OQ-316 (the `delivery_mode` admission cut)
- **Rung (ADR-0024 obligation):** C0 — every landed member is
  deterministic machinery over the landed C0 wakeup seam; no C1+
  interpreter semantics are claimed (`workflow`/`program` stay staged;
  `plan_execute`'s nudge loop is S3.10's).

## Context

ADR-0273 identified the residual this ticket owns: a `steer` admitted
under `steer_mode = queue_next_turn` parked the cue in session memory
(`SessionState::pending_steer`). The `context.artefact.delivered` row
was durable, but the pending *delivery* was not — a process restart
between `steer` and the next turn's first decision point silently lost
the cue (DF-S2.11-1). The durable transport was deliberately deferred:
the wakeup/subscription machinery (ADR-0131) is the one durable cue
channel, and its `steer` admission arm was cut to OQ-316 so the turn-loop
chain could own it once (DF-S2.8-1 member a's residual).

Three further residuals share the seam: the staged `decide` interpreters
(`react/steerable`'s durable-steer arm, `plan_execute`'s stop-protocol
legs, `workflow`/`program` bindings), the out-of-process
`control_strategy` conformance harness (AC-R-2.6.1-1's document leg),
and the env-owned hanging-tool kill (the driver records `unknown`; the
environment owns the kill — DF-S1.20-1).

The build surfaced two latent defects the durable-resume and kill cells
proved out: `Driver::resume_from` never re-seeded the `{tag}-{n}` alloc
watermark from the durable prefix (the first post-takeover decision
re-minted `d-1` over the committed row — in-process takeovers masked it
by carrying the live driver), and `LocalExecutor::drain_child` polled a
*blocking* pipe read, starving the deadline check — a silent child ran
to natural exit and the env-owned kill never fired.

## Decision

**D1 — the durable steer record is a wakeup occurrence, not a new kind.**
`steer{mode: next_turn}` keeps minting `context.artefact.delivered`
(durable-before-visible) and then rides the one durable cue channel: a
lazily-created `manual{principal}` wakeup subscription per run/principal
with `delivery_mode = steer`, `coalesce = none`, `max_pending = 16`, no
expiry; each queued steer mints `control.wakeup.occurred` keyed
`steer:<payload_ref>` (dedupe by occurrence key — a replayed steer id
audits rather than double-fires); `deliver_wakeup` fires
`control.wakeup.fired`; the driver drain submits
`Cue::Woken{delivery_mode: steer, payload_ref}`. `SessionState::
pending_steer` is deleted — the durable prefix, not session memory, is
the source the restart owes. No second durable cue channel exists
(CC1/CC10 — minting one would pre-decide a seam another ticket owns).

**D2 — the OQ-316 admission arm.** `delivery_mode = steer` is admitted
only on `trigger = manual{principal}` subscriptions; every other trigger
form (`timer`, `schedule`, `effect_terminal`, `retry_due`, …) answers
typed `WakeupPolicyUnsupported`. The pre-existing fleet boundary is
unchanged (`manual + follow_up` stays fleet-bound; ordinary non-manual
triggers keep their rules). `deliver_after` now covers both `follow_up`
and `steer`: a fired delivery whose run holds a committed non-terminal
effect is withheld until the effect settles. `deliver_due_wakeups`
deliberately excludes `steer` — steer deliveries enter the control
driver through the `deliver_wakeup`/drain path, never a surface poll.

**D3 — steering is declaration-driven; unknown variants refuse typed.**
`steering_for` reads the registered `VariantRecord`'s
`capability_declaration` (`steer_mode`/`concurrent_input`), falling back
to the impl's declared `steering` capability only when no record exists.
`hh/react-steerable-queue` is seeded as the `queue_next_turn` variant —
a registry alias binding the same `ReactSteerable` impl with the
declaration's mode (`variant_ref` stays `hh/react-steerable@1`; the mode
is declaration data, not impl state). `strategy_for`/`variant_for`
return `Option`: an unregistered ref is a typed refusal at `open`,
replay and plugin `bind` (`unknown_control_variant`/`NotInstalled`) —
the silent `react/minimal` substitute is dead on both sides of the
process line.

**D4 — the staged interpreters and the canonical wire.** `ReactSteerable`
interprets `Woken{steer}` as the same `propose{steer_ref}` decision as
`HumanInput::Steer`, and `Woken{follow_up}` as `{follow_up_ref}` —
joined interpretation, one contract. `PlanExecute` answers the
stop-protocol cues (`interrupt`/`cancel_requested` → `stop{cancelled}`)
and parks unsequenced cues rather than improvising a re-plan.
`StagedVariant` (`hh/workflow`/`hh/program`) parks every cue on
`human_input` but still answers the stop protocol — a parked run stays
cancellable, code-owned. `hh-control/src/wire.rs` is the canonical
document codec for `ControlContext`/events/`Cue`/`ControlDecision`/
`FinalReport`/`ControlError`/`RestoreError`/state-checkpoint docs — one
spelling on the wire, in tests, and at the ABI edge (CC1).
`ControlStrategy` carries `Send`: a binding is a pure fold over plain
data — the out-of-process host leg moves bound variants onto the
protocol thread, so thread-affinity would be a defect.

**D5 — the OOP conformance lane and the env-owned kill.**
`hh-plugin-fixture --mode control` hosts registered `hh-control`
variants over `plugin_abi/1` `invoke` (`open`/`observe`/`decide`/
`terminate`/`checkpoint`/`restore` — canonical docs in, canonical docs
out; caller-carried state makes the purity assertions hold by
construction; malformed input is `schema_violation`, unknown op
`unhandled_operation`, unknown variant `not_installed` at `bind`).
`hh-varhost/tests/control_strategy.rs` drives it through the real
`hh-helper` boundary — the durable-steer interpretation, the
declaration boundary, the stop protocol, decide determinism,
checkpoint/restore round-trip, and the typed refusals, all asserted on
the canonical record.

**D6 — the env-owned kill is a real kill.** `drain_child` pumps
stdout/stderr on reader threads through a bounded poll, so the deadline
check runs every iteration even for a silent child; on expiry the
executor SIGKILLs and reaps, reporting `tool_error{timeout}` with
`outcome_hint = "unknown"` and no exit status. The dispatcher settles
the compensable effect `unknown{executor_error}` — durable
`action.effect.unknown`, never `applied`/`not_applied` guesses over a
process that provably ran (the declared-`timeout` class keeps its
`tool`-origin rule per AC-R-2.5.5-4).

**D7 — resume seeds the alloc watermark.** `Driver::resume_from` scans
the durable prefix for the max `{tag}-{n}` suffix exactly as
`Driver::open`/`replay_from` do, so a resumed driver's first decision
continues numbering instead of re-minting `d-1` (`decision_events`/
`last_decision_ref` rebuild the same way — the checkpoint restores
semantic state; the prefix restores alloc state).

## Consequences

- `queue_next_turn` steer survives a process restart: durable
  `context.artefact.delivered` → `control.wakeup.scheduled` →
  `control.wakeup.occurred` → (restart, lease-expiry takeover) →
  `control.wakeup.fired` → drained `Woken{steer}` →
  `propose{steer_ref}` at the next decision point —
  `crates/hh-embed/tests/r2_6.rs` proves the occurred-before-fired
  ordering across a real store reopen.
- One durable cue channel only: steer, follow-up, retry-due and
  effect-terminal deliveries share the subscription/occurrence/fired
  spine; the steer leg's distinctiveness lives entirely in the
  `delivery_mode` member and the admission rule.
- The interpreter surface is now conformance-drivable out of process —
  a packaged `control_strategy` variant can ride `plugin_abi/1` with
  the same six ops the driver calls in-process.
- The `manual{principal}`-only admission means non-manual steer
  sources (scheduled, peer) remain typed-refused; a future widening is
  additive in `wakeup.rs`'s admission table.

## Alternatives considered

- **A dedicated `pending_steer` durable record kind** (the ticket text's
  starting shape): rejected — it mints a second durable cue channel
  with its own dedupe/drain/restart rules, duplicating machinery the
  wakeup seam already owns (CC1/CC10). The row text named a record kind
  to be ADR'd; the seam choice is this record.
- **`steer` admitted on any trigger**: rejected — OQ-316's question was
  specifically whether steer is admissible at all; admitting it broadly
  before the ownership/interaction analysis (WS-F1 × WS-D1) would
  foreclose the narrower answer. `manual{principal}` is the leg the
  boundary op actually needs.
- **Deliver steer through `deliver_due_wakeups`**: rejected — surface
  polls must not consume control cues; the driver drain owns ordering
  against `decide`.
- **Retry-once on blocking pipe reads** (no threads): rejected — a
  blocking `read` on a silent child's pipe sleeps until EOF; no amount
  of loop restructuring fixes starvation without a wake source. Reader
  threads + `recv_timeout` is the pure-std answer.

## Revisit trigger

Reopen if: (a) a non-manual steer source needs admission (the register
revalidation at R2.22–24 re-checks OQ-316 against the landed arm); (b)
`queue_next_turn` gains a second durable channel for ordering or
priority the occurrence key/`max_pending` bound cannot express; (c) a
`control_strategy` variant needs non-`Send` state — the trait bound then
moves to the binding layer, not the impl set.
