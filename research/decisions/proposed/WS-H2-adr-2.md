# ADR-NNNN — The C2 declassification/endorsement contract: per-basis effects on `taint`/`readers`, capacity-bounded shape endorsement, bounded sanitizers as `policy_rule`, robust declassification (D-ROBUST), and recoverable denial with a closed remedy set

**Status:** proposed
**Owner workstream(s):** WS-H2 (consumers: WS-H1, WS-H3, WS-H4, WS-H7, WS-G1, WS-D4, WS-F3, WS-B1, WS-I2) · **Phase:** 2 · **Related:** ADR-0035 (closed basis list — unchanged), ADR-0033 (I3 `taint ≠ ∅ ⇒ authority ≤ external`), ADR-0034 (P1, P6), ADR-0016 (`Validator` kinds), ADR-0031 (kernel decision record), ADR-0039/ADR-0040 (approvals budgeted), ADR-0002 (iii), ADR-0007 (T-LCD-12/-13/-14); CF-WS-H2-02/-05/-07; OQ-101 (shape of taint rows; unattended runs), OQ-103 (partial), OQ-106 (answered), OQ-WS-H2-01/-04/-05/-07; R-2.8.2, R-2.8.3, R-2.8.7

## Context

ADR-0035 fixed *who* may endorse (class ≥ target; never `delegate`) and *which* six bases exist, and left to H2 the enforcement of the two set-valued components and "declassification policy at C2 without introducing a second label type". Three things are missing for that to be a contract: **what** each basis may do to `taint` and `readers` (ADR-0035 says `declassified` widens readers only, and that `validator` raises `external → environment` for closed schemas — but not which schemas, nor whether taint clears); the **robustness** precondition without which a `policy_rule` executed by the kernel can be steered by injected recipients (Fides' `policy_no_untrusted_declassification` denies exactly this; Zdancewic–Myers state the property); and what the monitor offers **instead of aborting** — the 2026 ablation evidence (`provisional`) says recovery, not blocking, is where utility comes from, and ~93% approval rates (S-053) say `ask` alone is not that recovery. Sabelfeld & Sands' four dimensions (what/who/where/when) and prudent principles supply the checklist.

## Options considered

1. **Open sanitizer/declassifier hooks (any registered function may lower a label).** *Rejected:* an escape hatch on authority (T-LCD-01 in policy form; ADR-0035 option 3).
2. **A new endorsement basis `sanitize`.** *Rejected:* a dialect bump is unnecessary — a bounded sanitizer is a `policy_rule` executed by the kernel from a `definition`-issued rule, with the sanitizer's `(from, to)` bounds as the rule's declared effect.
3. **Shape endorsement for any schema-valid value (including strings).** *Rejected:* "unconstrained strings revert to full labels" (Fides); a URL or identifier carries a payload; capacity must be finite and declared.
4. **Abort on violation (Fides `PolicyViolation`, CaMeL `Denied`).** *Rejected as the only behaviour:* utility; remedies exist that never widen authority, so denial can carry them.
5. **Approval as a general declassifier.** *Rejected:* ADR-0035 basis 2 — approval endorses one effect; under fatigue it would launder recipients; D-ROBUST applies before `ask`.
6. **Per-basis effect table + capacity-bounded shape endorsement + bounded sanitizers as `policy_rule` + D-ROBUST + closed remedy set (chosen).**

## Decision

1. **Per-basis effects (binding; dossier §6.3 table).** `seal`/`pin`/`promotion`: raise authority per ADR-0035, clear taint, readers as declared. `approval`: never changes any component (endorses one `(effect_id, args_canonical_hash)`). `validator` (**shape endorsement**): `external → environment` and taint → ∅ **iff** the validating schema is *capacity-bounded* — booleans, closed enums, bounded integers/decimals with declared range, dates in a declared window, fixed-shape records and bounded arrays of those — with `capacity_bits = ⌈log₂|values|⌉ ≤ cap_max(definition)` (default 8); unbounded strings, free text, URLs, paths and open-alphabet identifiers never qualify (`BasisNotAllowed`). This answers OQ-106; a Q-LLM-style constrained extractor is a `Validator{kind: judge}` whose verdict earns `environment` only through the deterministic schema check that follows it. `policy_rule` has two sub-kinds: **(a) recipient declassification** — widens `readers` to the *named recipient set of one effect*, never touches authority or taint; **(b) bounded sanitizer** — a deterministic `Validator`/`Procedure` registered in the sealed definition with `bounds{from, to, removes}`; the output is a new item (`derived_from.kind = projection`, `deriver = kernel`) endorsed in the same append; `SanitizerBoundExceeded` if the realized output is not `⊑ to`. This reconciles ADR-0034 P1 ("a projection never lowers") with sanitization: lowering is the endorsement, not the projection (CF-WS-H2-02).
2. **D-ROBUST.** An endorsement/declassification event is refused (`RobustnessViolated`) if any *input to the decision* — recipient parameters of `policy_rule (a)`, selector arguments of a sanitizer, the `subject_ref` of an `approval` — has `effective_authority ≤ external` or `taint ≠ ∅` and was not itself shape-endorsed. The decision to declassify and the choice of what is declassified are thereby never influenced by content at the attacker's level.
3. **Prudent principles as acceptance checks.** Conservativity (no endorsements ⇒ ADR-1's guarantees), monotonicity of release (declassify/allow rules never turn an allowed flow into a denied one; `deny` rules are policies evaluated first), non-occlusion (`derived_from` from every declassified item to its source; immutability I1–I5), semantic consistency (decisions over records).
4. **Check 3 (flow) full form** (CF-WS-H2-02): for effects with `world = open` or domain ∈ `{net_egress, message_human, spend}`, plus `fs_write ∧ world = open` and `memory_write` to scope ≥ `project`: `recipients(p) ⊆ readers(x)` for every `content_param` `x`, with `recipients` resolved from `recipient_params` via the capability's `resolve_recipients`; a `Public` reader set always passes; otherwise `deny` with remedies. **Taint component of check 4:** Π's rows are keyed on `(EffectClass, effective_authority, taint ≠ ∅)`; a `taint ≠ ∅` row may be `allow` only for `read_only ∧ closed` classes or by explicit `definition`-issued rule.
5. **Recoverable denial.** `authorize(p) → allow | ask(effect_id, remedies[]) | deny(reason, remedies[])`; `Remedy = approval(effect_id) | sanitize(sanitizer_ref, param) | shape_endorse(validator_ref, param) | branch(spec) | substitute(param, min_authority = principal) | prerequisite(EffectClass)`. Invariants: R1 every remedy is a pure narrowing or an existing basis; R2 enumeration is bounded search over the finite `(Label, effect projection)` state and deterministic given the records; R3 a consumed remedy is a ledger event conferring nothing beyond the one proposal/value; R4 `ask` counts against `approvals.requested` (ADR-0040) and remedies are ledger-derived views unless WS-L2 admits a counter (OQ-WS-H2-02); R5 unattended runs omit `approval` from the remedy set. The model chooses among remedies; it cannot mint one.
6. **PrincipalRef (partial answer to OQ-103, to be confirmed by H3/J6):** `user(id) | recipient(channel_kind, address) | tenant(id) | public`.

**T-LCD statement (ADR-0007 decision 5).** Satisfies T-LCD-12 (operations over records; out-of-process), T-LCD-13 (every endorsement, sanitization and remedy is an observable event with a basis), T-LCD-14 (approvals and remedies are counted; AC-H2-10 compares under `MatchSpec`), T-LCD-05 (a profile-conditioned sanitizer or rule carries a debt record), T-LCD-06/-15 (hosted: `n/a` rows). Not applicable: T-LCD-01/-02/-03/-04/-07/-08/-09/-10/-11. ADR-0024 rung: bases, bounds, `cap_max` and rules are MUST-data; the endorsement validator, D-ROBUST check and remedy enumerator are MUST-code.

## Evidence (doc 3 §3.3 synthesis contract — all five mandatory)

- **(a) Mechanism evidence:** dimensions what/who/where/when and prudent principles — Sabelfeld & Sands (S-WS-H2-07, Tier A); "attackers can influence neither the decision to disclose information nor the choice of what information is disclosed" — Zdancewic & Myers (S-WS-H2-08, Tier A); only the owner declassifies — DLM (S-218); declassification at trusted endpoints — Flume (S-219); output-type lattice `bool ⊑ enum ⊑ string`, "Low capacity outputs are less useful to deliver prompt injection payloads or exfiltrate information", `policy_no_untrusted_declassification` — Fides (S-101/S-102, 2025); `is_trusted(recipient)` short-circuit, reader coverage per content parameter, Q-LLM refusal path — CaMeL (S-099/S-100, 2025, code); sanitizers with `(from → to)` bounds, call-scoped rulings "conferring no lingering authority", finite recovery graph, remedies ablation — APPA (S-WS-H2-01, `provisional`); attest-schema "confines shape and bandwidth rather than semantics" — APPA; typed releases binding to one action manifest — CXI (S-WS-H2-16, `provisional`); authorization derived in a clean context — AuthGraph (S-WS-H2-15, `provisional`); approval fatigue bounds `ask` — S-053 (Tier B); permission elicitation as a protocol verb — ACP (S-035).
- **(b) Source-code precedent:** `camel-prompt-injection/src/camel/pipeline_elements/security_policies/slack.py` L52–76 (recipient/channel reader coverage), `banking.py` L52–85 (`is_trusted(recipient)` gate, per-field coverage), `agentdojo_security_policies.py` L47–54 (`make_trusted_fields_policy`), `capabilities/utils.py` L33–37 (`can_readers_read_value`), `quarantined_llm.py` L25–99 (refusal); `fides/Tutorial.ipynb` cells 34–36 (untrusted declassification denied; content predicate). Capacity-bounded endorsement, bounded sanitizers as `policy_rule`, D-ROBUST as an append check, and the remedy enumerator: `no-precedent / novel` as running code.
- **(c) Disconfirming evidence considered:** approval is a weak endorsement (S-053) — accepted; approval never changes a label and D-ROBUST runs before `ask`; recovery's benefit rests on one provisional ablation (S-WS-H2-01) — the contract is safe regardless (remedies never widen), the benefit is measured (AC-H2-10); a bounded value may still be *false* (APPA) — a validity/evidence question (ADR-0014, WS-G2), not a label question; "in-the-loop" tasks reintroduce targetable human judgement (S-WS-H2-02) — bounded by D-ROBUST (an injected recipient cannot reach the approval prompt) and by H7's budget. None overturns.
- **(d) Conditionality:** independent of model tier and task class. Assumes a `definition`-issued rule set (unattended runs: R5); assumes recipient parameters are declared per capability (E1); `cap_max` is a per-definition parameter whose default (8 bits) is a conditioned choice with a Stage 3 removal test.
- **(e) Build-stage assignment:** **C2 / Stage 2** (per-basis table; check 3 on egress domains; D-ROBUST); **C2 / Stage 3** (shape endorsement with G1; sanitizers; remedies; AC-H2-3/-4/-7/-10); **C2 / Stage 4** (`branch` remedy with F3).

## Consequences

- WS-H1's `authorize` returns remedies and evaluates D-ROBUST before `ask`; Π's taint rows take the shape in decision 4. WS-H7 budgets `ask` and may rank remedies. WS-G1 registers capacity-bounded Validators and sanitizers with bounds. WS-H3 uses `readers` on credentials and the `secret:*` taint tag family; WS-H4 keeps destination policy (H2 decides value-to-destination). WS-D4's `promotion` clears taint per the table. WS-B1/H1 extend the decision and endorsement payloads (CF-WS-H2-05).
- Any later dossier that wants a new way to lower a label must map it onto one of the six bases with declared bounds, or propose a dialect bump.

## Reversibility

**Low-to-medium.** The per-basis table and `cap_max` are data and can be tightened by ADR; removing D-ROBUST after Stage 2 reopens injected-recipient exfiltration through approval prompts (the Fides cells 34–35 scenario), and admitting unbounded schemas reopens the payload channel — those two rules are load-bearing. Remedies can be removed by returning `[]` without any record change.
