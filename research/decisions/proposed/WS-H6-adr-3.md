# ADR-NNNN — Redaction, garbage collection and retention over an audited ledger (envelopes never leave; content leaves only by endorsed tombstone; ordered retention floors), and `audit_completeness` as a component-vector veto metric

**Status:** proposed
**Owner workstream(s):** WS-H6 (with WS-B1, WS-I1, WS-I2, WS-H3, WS-L4, WS-L6, WS-J5, WS-B3, WS-F2) · **Phase:** 2 · **Related:** ADR-0029 (redaction-safe hashing; `redact`), ADR-0026 (history never rewritten; `lifecycle.ledger.redacted`), ADR-0037 (I3 "deletion is only ever redaction"; S8 audit retention), ADR-0038 (bundle levels; `missing[]`), ADR-0044 (`audit_completeness` three-term definition — **amended by this ADR**; sink policy), ADR-0045/0047 (`MetricDeclaration`, veto invariants, `n/a{reason}`), ADR-0035 (endorsement bases; redaction endorser), ADR-0007 (T-LCD-15); CF-WS-H6-03, CF-WS-H6-04; OQ-WS-H6-04/-05/-06, OQ-085, OQ-112, OQ-119, OQ-128; R-2.8.6, R-2.8.3, R-2.9.2, R-2.12.1

## Context

Three ratified rules pull against each other. ADR-0026/0029 make the ledger append-only with redaction as "tombstone + blob deletion; the hash covers the address"; WS-B1 §6.5 lets cold-tier blobs be garbage-collected ("hashes survive, bytes may not"); ADR-0044 defines `audit_completeness = chain_ok ∧ no open scope past deadline ∧ no missing blob without tombstone`, so a policy-driven GC with no record would silently mark runs incomplete. Meanwhile secrets and personal data must be able to leave (WS-H3; erasure obligations), retention durations are unowned (OQ-085/OQ-112), and the scorecard needs audit completeness as a class-scoped, veto-capable metric (doc 2 §8 "audit completeness"; ADR-0047 veto invariants; OQ-128 asks which vetoes are C0). The tamper-evident-logging literature resolves the tension by separating integrity structures from payload custody: Crosby & Wallach's "selectively delete old events, in an agreed-upon fashion, while generating efficient proofs that no inappropriate events were deleted" (S-WS-H6-02); the IETF agent-audit draft's tombstones with a `content_fingerprint` proving prior existence after erasure (S-WS-H6-09, `provisional`); Agent Flight Recorder's encrypted payloads with hashes only in the integrity layer (S-WS-H6-08, `provisional`); CloudTrail's digest files kept apart from log files so log processing and deletion policies can differ (S-WS-H6-07).

## Options considered

1. **Retain everything forever; no redaction.** *Rejected:* violates WS-H3's secret-isolation obligations and erasure duties; storage cost of blobs; contradicts WS-B1 §6.5.
2. **Allow redaction/GC of any event including envelopes, rewriting the chain.** *Rejected:* ADR-0026 forbids rewriting; the chain and every citation would break; audit-grade facts (who authorized what) would be erasable by whoever holds storage.
3. **GC without a record; completeness ignores missing blobs.** *Rejected:* cannot distinguish accident from GC from tampering (Schneier–Kelsey's crash-vs-truncation problem in blob form).
4. **Audit completeness stays a single boolean over three terms.** *Rejected:* cannot render partial `n/a` for hosted rows (T-LCD-15) or tell the reader *which* obligation failed; no coverage or producer term.
5. **Envelopes never leave; content leaves only by endorsed tombstone (`redacted`) or policy record (`gc`); retention floors ordered audit ≥ experiment ≥ blob tier; `audit_completeness` as a component vector with `n/a` per component and `veto: true` (chosen).**

## Decision

1. **R1 — Envelopes never leave.** The `audit_fields` of every audit-grade event (WS-H6-adr-1 Rule C), every checkpoint and every cross-run anchor are retained for at least `AuditPolicy.retention.audit_envelope_floor` — a run-manifest field; proposed default: no automatic deletion for the life of the instrument installation (durations and legal-hold semantics: OQ-WS-H6-05 for WS-L6). `redact` on an `audit_field` is `NotRedactable`; `gc` never targets events.
2. **R2 — Content leaves by tombstone only.** Two audit-grade tombstone classes: `lifecycle.ledger.redacted{targets[], reason_code, endorser, basis, content_fingerprints[]}` (endorser `authority ≥ principal` with `basis = approval`, or the kernel's scanner with `basis = policy_rule` and a rule ref — closed list, ADR-0035; otherwise `IllegitimateEndorsement`) and `lifecycle.ledger.gc{addresses[], policy_ref, tier, retained_until?}` (refused with `Pinned` for addresses referenced by a retained bundle or a live fork prefix). Both precede byte deletion (durable-before-delete). Because leaf hashes cover `ContentAddress`es (ADR-0029), chain, checkpoints and proofs remain valid; `content_fingerprints[]` (the addresses) prove prior existence.
3. **R3 — Secrets.** Secret values never enter the ledger (ADR-0026 exclusion); credential events carry references only (WS-H3); an accidental leak is handled by R2 with `policy_rule` (scanner) or `approval` (principal). Blob encryption at rest (WS-H3's design) is compatible: hashes are over plaintext bytes at `put_blob`; a decryption failure reports `missing{reason = key_unavailable}`, never `tampered`.
4. **R4 — Retention order.** `audit_envelope_floor ≥ experiment/bundle retention ≥ blob tier policy`. A run whose blobs were GC'd with records keeps `blobs_accounted = true`; its bundle reports reproducibility R0 with `missing[]` (OQ-112 shape for WS-L4/L6). Referenced fork prefixes and executed/published versions follow ADR-0037 S8 and WS-B1 §6.5.
5. **R5 — Redaction is audited.** Every tombstone is an audit-grade event under Rule P (producer = ledger) with the endorser's provenance; `audit_view.redactions[]` lists them; `redaction_count` is a declared metric.
6. **`audit_completeness` (amends ADR-0044 decision 4).** `AuditCompleteness{chain_ok, checkpoints_ok, scopes_closed, blobs_accounted, producers_ok, coverage_ok, cross_run_ok | n/a, headline = ∧ of applicable components, n_a[{component, reason ∈ {class, observability, not_run}}], evidence: view_hash}` computed by `audit_view`; ADR-0044's three terms map to `chain_ok`, `scopes_closed`, `blobs_accounted` (the last now reads "no referenced blob missing without a `redacted` **or `gc`** record"). Declared as `MetricDeclaration{name: audit_completeness, dimension: security, level: run, value_type: bool + components, requires_observability: {events} (components needing `ledger` render `n/a`), applies_to_classes: {native, hosted}, detector_classes_allowed: {deterministic}, oracle_classes_allowed: {trace_predicate}, outcome_class_policy: computed for every outcome class, headline: false, veto: true, charged_to: instrument, computed_from: audit_view}`. A run with `headline = false` is *success-with-veto* (ADR-0047); this is WS-H6's answer to OQ-128: audit completeness is a **C0 / Stage 3 veto invariant**. Companion declarations: `coverage_unmet_count`, `redaction_count`, `checkpoint_interval_events`, `unsigned_tail_events`, `permission_decisions_by_decider_and_scope`.
7. **Scope deadline (input to OQ-119).** Default `scopes_closed` deadline = the scope's budget wall-time ceiling (ADR-0040) plus a manifest grace; a run that ends with `lifecycle.run.finished` closes all deadlines; a crash without `resumed` leaves scopes open and `scopes_closed = false` until recovery appends `lifecycle.run.resumed{recovery_decision}` and the terminal events — this is the crash-vs-truncation discriminator (S-WS-H6-01's abnormal-shutdown marker realized as an event). Final owner WS-B3/F2.

**T-LCD statement.** Satisfies T-LCD-15 (per-component `n/a{reason}`; never 0/false by absence), T-LCD-12 (record-level rules; out-of-process), T-LCD-14 (redaction and completeness are reported per arm so policies compare under matched budgets), T-LCD-11 (tombstones survive export as records; a sink that cannot carry them lists the loss). Not applicable: T-01…T-10, T-13. **ADR-0024 rung:** tombstone mechanics and the completeness computation are MUST-code; floors, redaction authority and deadlines are MUST-data.

## Evidence (doc 3 §3.3 synthesis contract — all five mandatory)

- **(a) Mechanism evidence:** Crosby & Wallach S-WS-H6-02 (Tier A: agreed-upon deletion with proofs that nothing inappropriate was deleted); Schneier & Kelsey S-WS-H6-01 (Tier A: crash vs truncation must be distinguishable; permission masks for selective disclosure); ADR-0029 decision 4 (hash covers the address — the property that makes R2 sound); ADR-0037 I3/S8 and WS-B1 §6.5 (tiers; hashes survive); ADR-0044/0047/0045 (`audit_completeness`, veto, `n/a`); CloudTrail S-WS-H6-07 (integrity structures separated from log custody); IETF draft S-WS-H6-09 (`provisional`: tombstone + `content_fingerprint`; erasure with proof of prior existence) and Agent Flight Recorder S-WS-H6-08 (`provisional`: hashes only in the integrity layer; encrypted payloads; "commitment proves a log existed but content is unrecoverable") as fresh corroboration only.
- **(b) Source-code precedent:** OpenHands S-110 @3fc7b22 `openhands-sdk/openhands/sdk/utils/truncate.py` L28–46 (content-named offload with write-once dedup — the blob side, via ADR-0036) and `event/condenser.py` (`Condensation{forgotten_event_ids}` — the projection forgets, the file keeps); Codex S-107 `codex-rs/rollout/src/compression.rs` (compression at rest preserving decode — via ADR-0029); transparency-dev/merkle @a09734c `proof/verify.go` (proofs over leaf hashes independent of payload custody). Tombstone families with endorser/basis, `gc` records, ordered floors and the component-vector veto metric are `no-precedent / novel` as an integrated contract.
- **(c) Disconfirming evidence considered:** "keep everything" simplicity vs erasure duties; forensic loss after redaction; Kiehl S-195 on machinery; OTel/goose/Codex default-off content capture (ADR-0044 (c)) suggesting content should never have been stored. *Why not overturning:* the floors distinguish small structural envelopes from large content; every redaction is endorsed and recorded, so erasure is compatible with audit; the `gc` record removes the ambiguity ADR-0044's definition would otherwise create; content capture stays ADR-0044's L2 rule (research instrument; consent per WS-L6). Residual risk — an over-broad `policy_rule` scanner redacting legitimate content — is bounded by R1 (envelopes) and the `redaction_count` metric.
- **(d) Conditionality:** independent of model tier and task class. Assumes ADR-0035's endorsement bases and the `principal` class are available at Stage 2; assumes WS-L6 sets durations (defaults here are placeholders); for hosted rows `producers_ok`/`coverage_ok`/`cross_run_ok` are `n/a{observability}` unless the adapter supplies `ledger`-level rows.
- **(e) Build-stage assignment:** C0 / Stage 2 (`lifecycle.ledger.gc`, redaction endorsement preconditions, `audit_view.content_refs` accounting, component vector); C0 / Stage 3 (`audit_completeness` in the registry as veto; AC-H6-2/-5/-10/-11); C1 / Stage 4 (bundle `missing[]` interaction with WS-I3/L4; lab-wide retention policy with WS-L6).

## Consequences

- ADR-0044 decision 4 is amended (definition extended; "tombstone" reads "`redacted` or `gc` record"); WS-I1's `audit_view` gains the component sections; WS-I2 registers the veto metric and companions (OQ-128 input).
- WS-B1's `redact` gains H6's preconditions and the `gc` operation; WS-L6 owns durations; WS-H3 owns scanner rules (`policy_rule` basis) and blob encryption; WS-J5 rows carry `audit_completeness` beside `audit_ref`.
- WS-B3/F2 adopt the scope-deadline default or replace it under OQ-119.

## Reversibility

**High for floors and deadlines (MUST-data); medium for the tombstone rules** (relaxing R1 after Stage 2 would allow erasure of audit facts and break AC-H6-2); **medium for the metric shape** (consumers read the component vector; collapsing it to a boolean loses `n/a` semantics required by T-LCD-15).
