# ADR-0192 — The merge contract: a closed `MergePolicy` with no last-writer-wins; belief merge as claim reconciliation plus memory conflict sets; workspace merge as the parent's own effects; conflicts surfaced as records and validators run on the merged state

**Status:** ratified, amended (Phase 4 synthesis)
**Owner workstream(s):** WS-F5 · **Phase:** 4 · **Related:** ADR-0026, ADR-0033/0034, ADR-0037, ADR-0082, ADR-0083, ADR-0109, ADR-0112, ADR-0113, ADR-0133, ADR-0138 (D5), ADR-0046; OQ-013, OQ-020, OQ-092; R-2.6.5; CF-406, CF-407, CF-412, CF-414; OQ-427/04/06

## Context

"Belief merge" in the brief covers three different things: what children *claim*, what they *remembered*, and what they *changed*. The field merges all three by text — the lead agent "synthesizes" returned lists (S-036), RAH's parent reads a shared JSON file, Prime Agent's harness file is last-writer-wins — and MAST documents the consequences: reasoning–action mismatch (13.2 %), premature termination (6.2 %), incomplete verification (8.2 %). Two ratified results already constrain the answer: a child's report is an `Observation{source: model_claim}` at `delegate` that is never evidence (ADR-0112), and memory conflicts resolve by supersession under the authority rule with *no* recency tie-break (ADR-0082). The remaining design question is the workspace and named artifacts, where the honest precedents are LangGraph's typed channels — a single-valued channel receiving two updates in one step raises `InvalidUpdateError` and tells the author to declare a reducer — and CRDT theory, which says exactly which merges are conflict-free without coordination (unions, counters, add/remove sets) and which are not (registers, text).

## Options considered

1. **Last-writer-wins by `seq` or completion order.** Rejected: unsound (Lamport — arrival order is not causal order), loses writes silently (Prime Agent), contradicts ADR-0082 decision 4.
2. **Model-synthesised merge** (the parent reads every child's output and rewrites the state). Rejected as the default: the parent is `delegate`; its synthesis is a claim; workspace changes would become un-attributed rewrites with no baseline (violates ADR-0138 D5 and G2's evidence discipline). Admitted as `parent_decides` for *resolution* of surfaced conflicts only.
3. **Always three-way textual merge, git-style, auto-resolving non-overlapping hunks and asking on overlaps.** Partially adopted as `three_way_text` — but only as one declared policy, because semantic conflicts survive it (S-584) and named artifacts/keyed memories are not text.
4. **Typed per-kind merge under a closed policy sum with no LWW value; conflicts are records; belief = reconciliation; validators on the merged state.** Chosen.
5. **Write-time conflict detection over a shared workspace (STORM, S-582).** Not rejected — expressible as ADR-0191's `share` mode with `single_writer` per key, where the conflict surfaces at `prepare`; a Lab factor, not the default.

## Decision

1. **Closed policy sum.** `MergePolicy = single_writer | append{dedup_by} | conflict_set | three_way_text{base, tokenizer ∈ {line, ast(grammar_ref)}} | validator_selected{validator_ref} | parent_decides`. There is no value meaning "newest wins"; `seq`, wall-clock, arrival order and child index never select a side.
2. **Defaults per object kind** (a definition's `CoordinationPolicy.merge_policies` may choose another admissible one): child result records, claims, cost rows ⇒ `append` (CRDT-safe union; claims then `reconcile` per ADR-0112, `ledger_only` default, `probe` read-only); memory versions written by children into a scope the parent owns ⇒ `conflict_set` at the parent's `write` (ADR-0082 detection; the parent at equal `delegate` authority may assert `supersedes` with a ledgered reason — OQ-432 asks whether a stronger basis is needed); workspace `FsChangeSet`s vs the fork-point snapshot ⇒ `three_way_text{line}` for disjoint hunks and `MergeConflict` for overlaps (alternatives `single_writer`, `validator_selected`, `parent_decides`); named artifacts ⇒ `single_writer` (the owner) with `MergeProposal`s from others queued as `append`; external resources ⇒ not merged (released; state re-read through the mediated capability).
3. **Records.** `MergeConflict{conflict_id, object, base_ref?, sides: [{child_run_id, version_ref, label}], detector ∈ {deterministic{policy}, judged{validator_ref}}, resolution?, resolved_by?}`; `MergeResolution = choose{side} | supersede{new_version, reason} | coexist | escalate{principal_ref} | abandon{side}`; `MergeReport{merge_id, parent_run_id, children[{child_run_id, outcome, result_ref?}], merged[{object, policy, inputs[], output_ref, label}], conflicts[], absent[], lost_write_count, provenance, derived_from: [child heads]}` — a derived view (`merge_report = project(...)`) with rebuild-equality (ADR-0026), cited by the parent's completion claim.
4. **G-1 No lost writes.** `lost_write_count = |committed child writes with observed{applied} to owned objects| − |merged[].inputs ∪ conflicts[].sides|` is kernel-computed and must be 0; otherwise `MergeIncomplete` and no completion.
5. **G-2 No silent overwrite.** Every resolution of a `single_writer`/keyed conflict is a ledgered `control.merge.resolved{conflict_id, resolution, basis, resolver}`; `choose`/`supersede` by the parent are `delegate` decisions whose output label is `⊔(chosen side, ctx(parent))`; `escalate` routes through WS-H7; `supersede` obeys ADR-0082's authority rule; a judged detector may create a conflict and never resolve one (ADR-0082 decision 4c).
6. **G-3 Belief is reconciled, never merged.** A child's `effected`/`verified`/`achieved` claim becomes a parent belief only through a `ReconciliationRecord` against authoritative handles (the child's ledger, effect states, validator verdicts, world state); a parent claim citing a child's text rather than its reconciliation is `unverifiable`; incompatible sibling claims are two records, not a conflict to pick from.
7. **G-4 Validators run on the merged state.** `merge` ends at the parent's `verify` decision point over the merged workspace and artifacts; the completion gate (ADR-0113) refuses a `finish` whose criteria cite only per-child verdicts when a `three_way_text` or `parent_decides` merge touched the criterion's evidence handles — the only defence against "compiles but disagrees at runtime".
8. **G-5 Absent is not empty.** A child with `outcome ≠ result` appears in `absent[]`, contributes nothing to `merged[]`, and any completion claim whose `evidence_refs` include its expected artifact is `unachievable`-class (never `achieved`).
9. **G-6 Determinism.** `merge` under `{append, conflict_set, three_way_text, validator_selected}` is a pure function of `(child heads, fork-point snapshot, policy)`; `merge_hash` is order-independent over child completion; only `parent_decides` involves a model call, charged to the parent (`subject`).
10. **Provenance.** Every merged item carries `derived_from = sides` with `Derivation.kind = subagent_result`, label = join of inputs (`kernel` deriver for deterministic policies; `delegate` join for `parent_decides`), and workspace changes are applied as the parent's own `fs_write` effects with `baseline_ref` (ADR-0138 D5) so B4 rollback and G2 attribution see ordinary effects.
11. **Metrics (ADR-0045 form; native only).** `coordination.lost_write_count` (**veto**), `coordination.silent_overwrite_count` (**veto**; any merged output equal to one side of an unresolved `single_writer`/keyed conflict), `coordination.conflicts_open_at_completion`, `coordination.absent_children`, `coordination.merge_cost` (`parent_decides` tokens, `charged_to = subject`).

**T-LCD statement.** Satisfies T-LCD-02 (merged text and rewind/absent notices are typed leaves with provenance), T-LCD-08 (`parent_decides` runs through the control strategy with `(ModelProfile, ResourceAccount)`), T-LCD-10 (objects keyed by `version_id`/content address), T-LCD-12 (pure operations, out-of-process), T-LCD-13 (resolutions and reports are ledger events), T-LCD-14 (merge cost attributed; policy comparisons under `MatchSpec`), T-LCD-15 (metrics class-scoped; hosted `n/a{class}`). Not applicable: T-LCD-01/-03/-04/-05/-06/-07/-09/-11. ADR-0024 rungs: `merge` for deterministic policies, G-1/G-2 and the vetoes are MUST-code; `merge_policies` defaults are MUST-data.

## Evidence (doc 3 §3.3 synthesis contract — all five mandatory)

- (a) Mechanism evidence: S-578 (CRDTs: only commutative/associative/idempotent merges are conflict-free without coordination); S-581 (arrival order ≠ causal order); S-403 (MAST FM-2.6/3.1/3.2 as observed consequences of text-level merging and unverified child output; 2025 single-team, mechanism only); S-584 (semantic conflicts survive textual merge; "logic that compiles but disagrees at runtime"); S-036 (lead synthesises returned lists — the model-merge baseline); S-064 (shared output file; no failure handling — the `absent` gap); ratified ADR-0082 (no recency tie-break; authority rule), ADR-0112 (claims never evidence), ADR-0113 (completion gate), ADR-0138 D5 (merge never implicit).
- (b) Source-code precedent: langchain-ai/langgraph @ e539ac1 `libs/langgraph/langgraph/channels/last_value.py` L56–66 (`InvalidUpdateError` "Can receive only one value per step. Use an Annotated key to handle multiple values."), `channels/binop.py` L123–142 (declared reducer; two `Overwrite`s refused), `channels/topic.py` L77–86 (accumulate), `types.py` L704–745 (`Send` map-reduce); getzep/graphiti and mem0 negatives via ADR-0082's evidence (recency/model-decided deletion); openai/codex @ 0735c51 `core/src/tools/handlers/multi_agents/wait.rs` L120–160 (`AgentStatus::NotFound` as a distinct outcome). The integrated contract (typed per-kind merge, `lost_write_count`, reconciliation-not-merge, validators-on-merged-state) is `no-precedent / novel`.
- (c) Disconfirming evidence considered: git-style automatic three-way merge resolves most overlaps and surfacing every conflict costs parent tokens — mitigated by admitting `three_way_text` as a deterministic policy whose output is validator-checked (G-4) while refusing only *silent* overwrite; STORM (S-582, `provisional`) — write-time detection is a Lab factor via ADR-0191 `share`; D4's own objection that `coexist` sets accumulate — measured by `conflicts_open_at_completion` and resolvable by the parent's ledgered supersession; the cost of G-4 (re-running validators after merge) — bounded to criteria whose evidence handles the merge touched.
- (d) Conditionality: C3; assumes ADR-0133 fork-point snapshots exist for workspace merges (`ForkPointMissing` otherwise) and ADR-0082's conflict detection is available in the parent's memory scope; `three_way_text{ast}` requires a registered grammar; `parent_decides` quality is model-conditioned and is measured, never assumed (T-LCD-05 if a profile conditions the default).
- (e) Build-stage assignment: C3 / Stage 4 first slice (`append`, `conflict_set`, `single_writer`, `MergeReport`, G-1/G-2/G-5 vetoes, AC-F5-01 isolated form, AC-F5-02, AC-F5-09); second slice (`three_way_text{line}`, `validator_selected`, `MergeProposal`, G-4 gate integration); Stage 4–5 Lab (`lab/coordination-topology-v1`, `three_way_text{ast}`).

## Consequences

WS-F3's join step calls `merge` and consumes `MergeReport`; WS-G2's completion gate reads `absent[]` and `conflicts[]`; WS-D4 gains the parent as an equal-authority resolver; WS-B1 adds `control.merge.*` classes; WS-J4's analysis treats `merge_policy` as a component-level factor; the results store keys merged runs by the parent run with `MergeReport` as an annotation. Parents can no longer "synthesise" child work into state without a record of what each child wrote.

## Reversibility

**Medium.** The policy sum and records are additive over existing ledgers; dropping G-4 or the vetoes is a policy change; reintroducing a recency default would contradict ADR-0082 and require a superseding ADR there first.
