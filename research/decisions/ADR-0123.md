# ADR-0123 — Ensembles are budgeted control-plane procedures, not router features: `EnsembleProcedure{sample_k_vote, mixture_of_agents, cascade}` at the `delegate` decision point, k ledgered model calls each routed individually, deterministic or governed-judge reducers, matched-total comparison only

**Status:** **ratified** (Phase 2 synthesis, 2026-09-10)
**Owner workstream(s):** WS-C2 (with WS-F3 for delegation, WS-G1/G3 for reducers/judges, WS-L2 for slices, WS-I2/J3 for admissibility, WS-A3 for the `Procedure` entity) · **Phase:** 2 · **Related:** ADR-0012 (β; `delegate` decision point), ADR-0016 (`Procedure`, `Validator`, `Budget`), ADR-0027 (structural id chain), ADR-0039/0040 (charges; `slice` mode; fan-out gauge), ADR-0041/0046 (matched-total; benefit kinds), ADR-0045 (outcome classes), ADR-0047 (judge governance; veto), ADR-0007 (T-LCD-09/-13/-14/-15); OQ-013 (answered by ADR-0040 reservations); R-2.3.2, R-2.6.3

## Context

Doc 3 §2.3 lists "ensembling" under the router. The evidence says ensembles are a different kind of object: self-consistency samples k reasoning paths and marginalises by vote (ICLR 2023, +3.9…+17.9 pp, cost linear in k); Mixture-of-Agents layers proposers under an aggregator (ICLR 2025); FrugalGPT's cascade routes *after* generation through a learned scorer (TMLR 2024). Against them, Chen, Zaharia & Zou (NeurIPS 2024) prove Vote/Filter-Vote performance is *non-monotone* in the number of calls — "more LM calls lead to higher performance on 'easy' queries, but lower performance on 'hard' queries" — and LLMRouterBench (`provisional`) reports "larger ensembles exhibit diminishing returns compared to careful model curation"; Anthropic's production system spends ~15× chat tokens and states multi-agent "require[s] tasks where the value of the task is high enough" (S-036). No audited harness implements an ensemble inside its model router; every one that fans out does so through subagent delegation with a budget slice (S-036; codex session-tree pool; Inspect nested scopes — WS-L2 §4c). The ratified contracts already provide the parts: `Procedure` and `Validator` entities (ADR-0016), the `delegate` decision point (ADR-0012), budget `slice` delegation with reserve-before-spend and the `fan_out` gauge (ADR-0040), judge governance with independence and `charged_to` (ADR-0047), and the matched-total comparison mode (ADR-0041).

## Options considered

1. **Ensembles as a router policy kind (`select` returns k targets; the router aggregates).** *Rejected:* the router is a per-call boundary component and must stay pure and cheap; aggregation is a verification decision (P4) or a control decision (P3), not a model-boundary one; k-fold fan-out inside `select` would bypass `delegate`, the budget slice and the fan-out gauge (ADR-0040 decision 5).
2. **Ensembles as provider-side features (e.g., a gateway "best-of-n").** *Rejected:* unattributable — one `model_call_id` would hide k charges and k model identities (ADR-0039 R-ACC-2 nothing unaccounted).
3. **No ensembles in scope.** *Rejected:* the sponsor named ensembling; the Lab must be able to *compare* sample-k, MoA and cascade against single calls at matched budget — that comparison is the point.
4. **Ensembles as explicit `Procedure`s at the `delegate` decision point with k individually routed, individually ledgered model calls, a typed reducer, a budget slice, and matched-total-only comparison (chosen).**

## Decision

1. **Representation.** `EnsembleProcedure` is a HIR `Procedure` (ADR-0016) with `kind ∈ {sample_k_vote, mixture_of_agents, cascade}` and fields `members: [{role: ModelRole, count, model_binding}]`, `reducer ∈ {majority_deterministic{key: canonical output hash | validator-normalised answer}, validator_ref (a `Validator{kind: judge}` under ADR-0047: independent of every member snapshot, `charged_to = subject` when bound in the definition), first_passing_validator (cascade: cheapest member first, escalate on `verification.validator.verdict = fail`)}`, `budget: Ref<Budget>` (allocated in `slice` mode, ADR-0040 decision 2), `stop_rule` (early exit on unanimity or on a cascade pass), and an `AssumptionDebtRecord` when any member binding is family-keyed (T-LCD-05).
2. **Execution.** The control strategy reaches the ensemble only through the `delegate` decision point (β may place it in code or model — ADR-0012 decision 6); each member is its own logical `model_call_id` routed by the router's `select` with `role = member.role` (ADR-1) — the router never fans out on its own; `fan_out` is metered as a gauge and refused at the cap (`SpawnRefused`); the reducer's output is a `verification.validator.verdict` (or a kernel `derived` observation for `majority_deterministic`) with `authority ≤ delegate` (ADR-0033 — an ensemble never endorses); a `cascade` is the only kind that may stop early *by construction*, and its scorer/validator is charged like any validator.
3. **Accounting and factors.** The slice's consumption equals the sum of member charges plus the reducer's; `cost_view.by_attribution` shows the ensemble under `harness_overhead.verification` (judge reducers) or `task` (member calls) — never hidden in `routing`. `k`, `kind` and `reducer` are component-level factors; an ensemble arm is comparable with a single-call arm **only** under `MatchSpec.mode = matched_total` (ADR-0041) with `benefit_kind = artifact_benefit` (ADR-0046); the engine refuses `matched_cap` for k > 1 vs k = 1 (utilisation would be incomparable) — `IncommensurableMatch`.
4. **Hosted participants.** An ensemble of hosted participants is a Lab-side `Procedure` over `OpaqueProcess` members at `configuration-level`/`product-level` granularity; member-level attribution metrics are `n/a` (T-LCD-15).
5. **Tiering.** C2 / Stage 4 (after WS-F3 delegation and the reservation contract) for `sample_k_vote` and `cascade`; `mixture_of_agents` at C2 / Stage 4+ once a judge reducer with `calibration_ref` exists (WS-G3). No ensemble is C0/C1.

## Evidence (doc 3 §3.3 synthesis contract — all five mandatory)

- (a) Mechanism evidence: vote-based aggregation improves reasoning at linear cost in k (S-452, Tier A); layered proposer/aggregator ensembles are implementable with open models (S-451, Tier A); cascades route post-generation through a scorer (S-446); returns are non-monotone in k on mixed-difficulty tasks (S-453, Tier A — the reason k is a factor and the comparison is matched-total); production fan-out costs ~15× and pays only on high-value tasks (S-036); fan-out without reservation overshoots budgets (S-209 `provisional (mech)`, adopted by ADR-0040).
- (b) Source-code precedent: delegation-with-slice patterns — codex `codex-rs/core/src/rollout_budget.rs` (shared pool per session tree), inspect_ai `src/inspect_ai/util/_limit.py` (nested exclusive scopes) — both already cited by ADR-0040; judge-as-validator governance ADR-0047; no audited harness router implements ensembles (negative finding, WS-C2 §5 R6). The `EnsembleProcedure` as a typed, budget-sliced, individually routed, reducer-governed procedure is `no-precedent / novel as integrated`.
- (c) Disconfirming evidence considered: ensembles may hurt (S-453; S-449 `provisional`) — this is why they are never a default and are compared only under matched-total; MoA's latency/TTFT cost is unreported in its abstract — the procedure carries `turn_phase_profile` (ADR-0043) so latency is always beside quality; "provider best-of-n is cheaper to build" — rejected on attribution (R-ACC-2). None overturns.
- (d) Conditionality: assumes outputs are reducible (a canonical answer form or a validator) — free-form outputs require a judge reducer under ADR-0047 governance; assumes budgets large enough for k ≥ 2 members under a slice (the guard refuses otherwise); task-class dependent by construction (S-453), which is what the Lab measures.
- (e) Build-stage assignment: **C2 / Stage 4** (`sample_k_vote`, `cascade`, slice accounting, `IncommensurableMatch` refusal) → **C2 / Stage 4+** (`mixture_of_agents` with calibrated judge reducers, WS-G3) → **C4** (ensemble shape as an evolution target under ADR-0046 gates). ADR-0024 rung: ensemble kinds are *variants* of the `Procedure` class; the matched-total refusal is *Core* (already ADR-0041).

**T-LCD statement.** Satisfies T-LCD-09 (`k`, `kind`, `reducer` are factors), T-LCD-14 (matched-total only; `matched_cap` refused across k), T-LCD-15 (hosted member metrics `n/a`), T-LCD-05 (family-keyed member bindings carry debt records), T-LCD-12. T-LCD-13 applies through the members' own artifacts, not the ensemble.

## Consequences

- Doc 3 §2.3's "ensembling" moves, in the spec, from the router subsystem to the control/orchestration subsystem (§7.2-5, F3) with a cross-reference; the router's responsibility line reads "routing and fallback".
- WS-F3 gains `EnsembleProcedure` as a delegation shape with a `slice` budget; WS-G1/G3 supply the reducer kinds; WS-J3's engine gains the `IncommensurableMatch` case for k-vs-1 under `matched_cap`.
- WS-I1's `cost_view` attributes member calls to `task` and reducers to `harness_overhead.verification`.

## Reversibility

**High.** Ensembles are variants of an existing entity behind existing accounting; removing them loses no ledger data. Moving them *into* the router later would be the expensive direction (bypassing slices, gauges and attribution), which this ADR forecloses.
