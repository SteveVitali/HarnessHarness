# ADR-0037 — Immutability, supersession and revocation over versioned records; names as append-only histories; the sameness ladder (typed diff ↔ version)

**Status:** **ratified, amended** (Phase 2 synthesis, 2026-09-10); previously: **ratified** (Phase 1 synthesis, 2026-09-10)
**Owner workstream(s):** WS-L4 (consumers WS-D4, WS-H6, WS-J2, WS-J5, WS-I5, WS-I6, WS-I7, WS-C3, WS-H5) · **Phase:** 1 · **Related:** ADR-0002 (reversibility; no self-widening of authority), ADR-0007 (T-LCD-05/-10/-15), ADR-0008; ADR-0016 (`supersedes`, `derived-from`), ADR-0017 (`HirDiff`), ADR-0026/ADR-0027 (append-only, redaction, lineage), ADR-0025 (`verify_resume`); OQ-020, OQ-034, OQ-110, OQ-114; CF-008, CF-084, CF-087; R-2.12.1, R-2.4.4, R-2.8.6, R-2.10.2

## Context

Doc 2 §10 asks what semantics apply "when a skill, memory, policy, tool wrapper, or optimizer-generated rule is superseded or revoked … how should downstream artifacts inherit invalidation, and what should be immutable for audit" (OQ-020). WS-A3 fixed `supersedes` as an edge with `reason ∈ {edit, revocation, expiry, migration}` and the rule that revocation is "a new version + `supersedes`, never deletion" for `Memory`; ADR-0017 fixed `HirDiff` with a classification (`semantic_ops`, `surface_ops`, `authority_delta`, `budget_delta`) and left the registry storage form to WS-L4; WS-B1 fixed append-only ledgers with tombstone redaction and fork-by-reference. Two fresh mechanism papers show the failure when revocation is a label rather than an enforced record: in five memory systems "the revoked fact is returned … outranks its replacement, and leads agents to the unsafe action" (S-106), while version stamps enforced at the protocol layer produce zero validity failures (S-077). SemVer forbids modifying a released version; git and every audited harness keep immutable objects behind mutable names; Harbor's lock keys equality by digest and records paths for humans. The lab additionally needs a rule for *when two definitions are "the same harness"* so results are pooled, compared as lineage, or treated as different harnesses.

## Options considered

1. **Mutable records with a `revoked` flag** (soft revocation). *Rejected:* the S-106 failure signature; contradicts I1 immutability and WS-B1's append-only ledger.
2. **Hard deletion on revocation.** *Rejected:* destroys the audit record (R-2.8.6); opencode/goose destructive paths are WS-B1's rejected counter-examples.
3. **Supersession as an edge on immutable versions, enforced by the resolver/retrieval layer; names as append-only histories; sameness computed from `HirDiff` classification (chosen).**
4. For registry lineage storage: **diffs only** (git-packfile style, rebuild snapshots on read) or **snapshots only**. *Rejected in favour of* snapshots authoritative with cached diffs, because `apply(base, diff) = target` is a checkable invariant and lookups dominate.

## Decision

1. **Immutability I1–I5.** Anything with a `version_id`/`ContentAddress` never changes; a logical name is an append-only history of `NameHistoryEntry{namespace, name, version_id, semantic_id, label?, published_at, publisher, status ∈ {active, deprecated, yanked}, supersedes_entry?, diff_ref?, display_selector?}`; deletion is only redaction (hash survives); sealed definitions, run/bundle manifests, registry records and name entries are immutable; run inputs are fixed at `open_run`.
2. **Supersession S1–S8.** Supersession is an edge carried on the newer record and mirrored in name history, never a flag on the older one; revocation without replacement is a `RevocationRecord` (a new version with `validity.until`, empty body, authority, provenance) so that "revoked" is itself hash-chained; the graph is acyclic and per-name a DAG (forks are branches, head chosen by status and authority, never by timestamp alone); `resolve(mode = execute)` never returns revoked/yanked/stale heads, `mode = audit | reproduce` always resolves a pinned `version_id` with its status — the guard is in the resolver and in D4's retrieval, not in the model; dependants of a revoked member are `stale-by-dependency` in a derived `StaleIndex` and their results are annotated `depends_on_revoked = true` (never silently excluded — OQ-034 shape); memory corrections, assumption-debt expiry and dialect migration are supersessions with their reasons; every executed or published version is retained at least as canonical bytes, and name histories/edges are never GC'd.
3. **Sameness ladder** (dossier §6.4): L0 equal `version_id`; L1 equal `semantic_id` (surface/ext/provenance-only diff — results pool, the surface diff is a profile-version experiment, T-LCD-10); L2 same lineage compatible (`semantic_ops ≠ ∅`, `authority_delta ∈ {none, narrowing}`, `budget_delta ∈ {none, tightening}`, `supersedes{edit}` under one name — adjacent points on one lineage, resumable per WS-A5 rules, never pooled); L3 same lineage incompatible (`authority_delta = widening`, `budget_delta = loosening`, or dialect change — human origin with attestation, MAJOR label bump, never resumable); L4 different harness. Hosted participants have only L0/L4; their `participant_version` is a claim and DRIFT is the observation that it failed.
4. **Registry lineage storage:** sealed snapshots are authoritative; `HirDiff`s between consecutive versions are derived, cached and referenced from name-history entries (`diff_ref`); `apply(base, diff) = target` byte-for-byte is checked and a failing diff is regenerated, never trusted. Ablation successors live in experiment-scoped namespaces.
5. **Operations:** `publish`, `resolve`, `supersede`, `revoke`, `lineage`, `sameness` with the failure modes in dossier §6.7 (`LabelReused`, `KindMismatch`, `AuthorityWideningRequiresHuman`, `CycleDetected`, `NotAncestorOrSibling`, `AuthorityInsufficient`, `Revoked{…}`).

**T-LCD statement.** Satisfies T-LCD-10 (L1 is the rename test), T-LCD-05 (assumption-debt expiry is a recorded supersession), T-LCD-15 (hosted sameness reports only L0/L4; component-level lineage `n/a`), T-LCD-12 (operations, out-of-process). Not addressed: executable tests.

## Evidence (doc 3 §3.3 synthesis contract — all five mandatory)

- **(a) Mechanism evidence:** immutable objects behind mutable refs — git (S-224); released versions must not change — SemVer (S-227); revocation must be enforced below the model — S-106 (`provisional (mech)`), corroborated by doc 2 §5.1 Tier-B synthesis ("revocation must be enforced by the retrieval/control layer") and by the mature cache-invalidation principle S-077 instantiates (`provisional (mech)`); supersession as an appended record the projection enforces — OpenHands `Condensation`, Codex rollback-as-event (S-110, S-107 via WS-B1 F2); digest-anchored equality with paths for humans — Harbor `SourceTrialLock` (S-132); renames are behaviourally significant and must be separable — S-049 (Tier B), doc 2 §9; reversibility and non-widening — ADR-0002.
- **(b) Source-code precedent:** harbor `src/harbor/models/job/lock.py` @7d5285b L91–121 (content-anchored equality; `SourceTrialLock{action: regrade, trial_id, task}` as lineage), software-agent-sdk `openhands-sdk/openhands/sdk/event/condenser.py` (`Condensation{forgotten_event_ids}`), `profiles/agent_profile.py` (stable `id` + renameable `name` + `revision`) @3fc7b22; codex `codex-rs/protocol/src/protocol.rs` `ThreadRolledBackEvent`, `SessionMeta{forked_from_id}` @0735c51; Pro Git §10.2 refs over objects. **`no-precedent / novel`** for: a `RevocationRecord` as a hash-chained version; `StaleIndex` propagation to dependants with annotated (not excluded) results; the L0–L4 sameness ladder computed from a classified typed diff.
- **(c) Disconfirming evidence considered:** S-077/S-106 are 2026 single-team preprints and their numbers may not replicate; append-only name histories and stale indexes add storage and a resolver hop on every load; the OpenHands authors chose deny-lists for skills because catalogs drift, suggesting strict supersession may make definitions brittle. *Why none overturns:* the record shape is standard truth-maintenance/cache-invalidation practice independent of the preprints' numbers (only the failure signature is borrowed); the resolver hop is one indexed lookup and the index is derived/rebuildable (WS-B1 view discipline); brittleness is confined to the *sealed* form — authored selectors and deny-lists remain legal (WS-A5) and resolution is recorded, so drift is visible rather than silent.
- **(d) Conditionality:** model- and task-independent; assumes WS-A3's `HirDiff` classification is available (Stage 1) and that D4 implements validity/authority-before-relevance retrieval (CF-008); the authority model for `publish`/`revoke` in shared registries is OQ-110 (WS-H1/L6/J2).
- **(e) Build-stage assignment:** **C0 / Stage 1** (I-rules, `publish`/`resolve`/`supersede`/`revoke` records, single namespace); **C0 / Stage 2** (`StaleIndex`, `lineage`/`sameness`, revocation enforcement in `resolve` and D4 retrieval — AC-L4-7); **C1 / Stage 4** (multi-namespace registry service, WS-J2); **C4 / Stage 6** consumes lineage for evolution/attribution (WS-I5/I7).

## Consequences

- WS-D4's retrieval filter operates on this dossier's `supersedes`/`RevocationRecord`/`validity` records; the S-106 scenario becomes a regression test in D4's suite (AC-L4-7).
- WS-J2 stores snapshots plus cached diffs and serves name histories; WS-J5 annotates rather than hides `depends_on_revoked` rows; WS-J4 pools results only at L1.
- WS-C3 receives surface-only diffs as profile versions; WS-I6's "retire rule R" produces `supersedes{expiry}`.
- WS-H6 retains every executed/published version's canonical bytes; yank/revoke/redact are audited events.
- Ontology: `capability declaration` "immutable for the life of a run" is restated as "a versioned record whose history is append-only" (CF-084, wording only).

## Reversibility

**Low** for I1–I4 and S1/S3 (append-only history and resolver-enforced revocation are load-bearing for audit and for D4's safety property); **medium** for the sameness ladder thresholds (L2/L3 boundaries can be re-cut without touching stored records); **high** for the lineage storage form (snapshots vs diffs is an internal registry choice).
