## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream decides **what the audit trail of HarnessHarness is, what makes it trustworthy, and how it is checked**: (i) the **audit-event catalogue** — which run-ledger event classes are *audit-grade* (decisions, effects, permission grants/denials, delegations, credential uses, extension loads, evolution edits, human approvals, endorsements, redactions, recoveries) and the mandatory fields each must carry inline; (ii) the **tamper-evidence contract** — how the per-run hash chain WS-B1 already supplies (ADR-0029) is turned into checkable, signed, cross-run-anchored commitments (tree heads, checkpoints, inclusion/consistency proofs) and what threat model that contract does and does not cover; (iii) the **correlation rules** by which every audit fact is addressable from the trace model (WS-I1), the results store (WS-J5), the bundle (WS-L4/I3) and across delegation trees; (iv) the **redaction-vs-integrity-vs-retention resolution** — how secrets and personal data leave the record without breaking the chain, and what may never leave; (v) **audit completeness** as a computable, class-scoped scorecard metric with veto semantics (WS-I2); (vi) acceptance criteria (tamper battery, coverage fixtures) and build-stage placement.

The binding position, taken up front and consistent with ADR-0026/0042/0044: **there is no second audit log.** The audit trail *is* the run ledger, read through `audit_view`, plus a small set of commitments (checkpoints) that are themselves ledger events. WS-H6 adds no store, no identity kind and no event that a consumer could obtain elsewhere; it adds *obligations* (which events must exist, with which fields, produced by whom), *commitments* (signed tree heads) and *checks* (coverage, chain, proofs).

Left to neighbours: the ledger envelope, ID model, canonical form, chain leaf hash and `redact`/blob store (WS-B1 — adopted verbatim); the effect lifecycle and `abandoned` semantics (WS-B2 — adopted; H6 fixes only that every terminal is audit-grade); the trace projection and `SinkPolicy` (WS-I1 — adopted; H6 extends `audit_view`); identity profile, rotation checkpoints and bundle attestation subject (WS-L4 — adopted); the provenance record, endorsement bases and the seven-check monitor (WS-L3/H1 — adopted; H6 audits them, never re-decides); approval cadence and UX (WS-H7); what a credential *use* is (WS-H3 — H6 fixes the audit record it must emit); extension pin attestations (WS-H5); egress decisions (WS-H4); the viewer (WS-K2); consent/retention *durations* (WS-L6, OQ-085/OQ-112); every ecosystem choice (ADR-0050).

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence (a failure mode exists / an architecture is implementable)

**F1 — Tamper evidence is a property of *commitments plus auditing*, not of a hash chain.** Schneier & Kelsey's construction gives forward security (an attacker controlling the machine after *t* cannot alter or forge entries before *t* without detection *when the trusted verifier next reads the log*), and it says explicitly that deletion of the tail is possible and only *detectable*, that a closing record and an abnormal-shutdown marker are needed to distinguish "crashed at 12:10" from "truncated after 12:10", and that its primary limitation is an attacker who simply keeps writing plausible entries (S-WS-H6-01). Crosby & Wallach define the semantics in terms of an *auditor* who can demand two proofs — membership ("this event is still present") and consistency ("the log as seen now is consistent with how it was seen in the past") — and note that hash-chain designs make both proofs linear in the log length, whereas a history tree makes them logarithmic (S-WS-H6-02). Certificate Transparency operationalizes exactly these two proofs with domain-separated Merkle hashing (S-190, S-WS-H6-03 `proof/verify.go`), and both production mechanisms read here commit periodically rather than per entry: CloudTrail signs an hourly digest that chains to the previous digest (S-WS-H6-07); Rekor signs a three-line checkpoint per tree size and lets witnesses cosign it (S-WS-H6-04, S-WS-H6-05/06). **Implication:** WS-B1's per-event chain (ADR-0029) is the *leaf layer*; H6 must add (a) periodic **checkpoints** = signed tree heads over the run's leaf hashes, (b) an auditor role that stores heads *outside the writer* (the results store, the bundle, the parent run), and (c) the two proofs as operations. Without (b) a chain proves nothing to anyone but its author — Notarized Agents' "the entity producing the log is the entity being logged" (S-WS-H6-10) is the same point.

**F2 — The audit-grade catalogue is convergent across independent designs, and it is the *structural* events, not content.** Agent Flight Recorder binds intent → policy evaluation → human approval → execution → effects → context provenance → code provenance → delegation provenance (S-WS-H6-08); the IETF draft's action types are `tool_call, tool_response, decision, delegation, escalation, error, lifecycle` with `record_phase ∈ {pre, post, concurrent}` (S-WS-H6-09); doc 2 §5.6 lists what a monitor must know (provenance, capability scope, policy, target effect, approval points) and §11 requires "every side effect attributable to a run and tool call" and that high-risk effects be "auditable even if the model or extension layer is compromised". Every item already exists in WS-B1's taxonomy as amended (ADR-0026 log): `action.effect.*` phases (B2), `security.permission.requested/decided/granted/revoked`, `security.label.*`, `security.credential.mediated`, `security.egress.*`, `security.extension.loaded`, `control.subagent.*`, `control.decision`, `measurement.harness_edit.*`, `lifecycle.run.resumed{recovery_decision}`, `lifecycle.lease.fenced`, `lifecycle.ledger.redacted`, `security.audit.checkpoint`. **Implication:** H6 does not add a vocabulary; it *classifies* existing classes as audit-grade and fixes, per class, the fields that must be **inline in the envelope/payload (never offloaded, never redactable)** so that an audit-grade event remains interpretable after every blob it references is gone. This is the *content-free envelope rule* (§6.1) — the same separation Codex makes between `log_only` and `trace_safe` fields (WS-I1 F3), applied to durability instead of export.

**F3 — Per-event chains are necessary but insufficient for a *branching, forking, multi-run* record.** VCT (S-WS-H6-11) builds branch chains → session roots → account root precisely because transcripts branch and are selectively deleted; Crosby & Wallach's "forking-inconsistent views" and C2SP's split-view protection are the same problem for a logger that can present different histories to different verifiers. HarnessHarness has three such seams: forks (`lifecycle.run.forked{forked_from{run_id, at_seq, source_head_hash}}`, ADR-0027), subagent delegation (`control.subagent.spawned` ↔ child `lifecycle.run.created{spawn_event}`), and export/import of hosted rows. **Implication (§6.3 anchoring rules):** every cross-run edge carries the *other* run's head hash at the moment of the edge, in *both* directions where both runs are live (child records the parent's head at spawn; parent records the child's terminal head at `result`), so a run tree forms one connected commitment graph and a re-written child cannot be spliced under an untouched parent.

**F4 — Redaction and retention are solvable without weakening integrity if the hash covers the address, not the bytes — and if garbage collection leaves a record.** ADR-0029 already hashes offloaded payloads by `ContentAddress`; ADR-0037 I3 makes deletion "only ever redaction"; Crosby & Wallach describe "selectively delete old events, in an agreed-upon fashion, while generating efficient proofs that no inappropriate events were deleted"; the IETF draft uses tombstones plus a `content_fingerprint` so "proof of prior existence after erasure" survives GDPR-style erasure; Agent Flight Recorder keeps payloads encrypted and hashes only. WS-I1's `audit_completeness` flips to `false` on "any referenced blob missing without a tombstone" (ADR-0044) while WS-B1 §6.5 lets cold-tier blobs be GC'd with "hashes survive, bytes may not" — the two are consistent only if **GC also writes a record**. **Implication:** one `lifecycle.ledger.redacted` (endorsed) and one `lifecycle.ledger.gc` (policy-driven) tombstone family; both audit-grade; the *envelope* of an audit-grade event is never redactable; retention floors are defined per class (§6.4).

**F5 — Production harnesses persist the *outcome* of a permission decision but not the decision itself, and never the request.** Codex's rollout persistence policy lists `ExecApprovalRequest`, `RequestPermissions`, `ApplyPatchApprovalRequest`, `ElicitationRequest`, `HookStarted/Completed`, `GuardianAssessment` and `ExecCommandBegin/End` as transient (S-107 `policy.rs` L145–200); the human's `ReviewDecision` reaches only the optional `codex.tool_decision` telemetry event with `source ∈ {AutomatedReviewer, Config, User}` (`session_telemetry.rs` L1098). OpenHands persists a `UserRejectObservation{rejection_source ∈ {user, hook}, action_id}` but records an *acceptance* only implicitly (the action runs) and the `ConfirmationPolicy` in force only as state (S-110). WS-B1 followed Codex in making `security.permission.requested` `ephemeral` (AC-B1-12), and WS-I1 measures `approval_wait_ms` from the durable `action.tool.proposed` to `decided` (M10). **Implication:** the audit-grade record of a permission act is `security.permission.decided`, which must therefore carry *everything the request contained* that the decision depended on (`requested_at`, the proposal `EventRef`, the `effective_risk_class`, the options presented, the rendering the human saw by reference, the decider and its provenance, the decision *scope*), and a request that is never decided (timeout, abort, run end) must still close with a durable `decided{decision ∈ {timeout, abandoned}}` — Codex's own `ReviewDecision::TimedOut` exists but is not persisted. CF-WS-H6-01.

**F6 — Authority is widened outside the audit record in every audited harness.** Codex `ReviewDecision::{ApprovedExecpolicyAmendment, ApprovedMcpPolicyAmendment, NetworkPolicyAmendment, ApprovedForSession}` and the Claude SDK's `PermissionResultAllow.updated_permissions[] → {userSettings, projectSettings, localSettings, session}` turn a one-off approval into a persisted rule as a side effect of the approval (S-107 L4074–4109; S-109 L109–255). ADR-0035 already forbids exactly this at the label level ("approval endorses one Effect intent … never content, never a class"; persistence-scope ceilings) and requires every label increase to be a ledger event, but it does not say what the *permission-rule* write looks like in the ledger. **Implication:** every change to the permission rules in force during or after a run — cached session approvals, persisted amendments, hook-installed allowances — is an audit-grade `security.permission.granted{scope ∈ {once, session, persisted}, rule_ref, basis, endorser}` (or `revoked`), and a persisted change additionally appears as `lifecycle.definition.changed{diff_ref}` with `authority_delta` classified (ADR-0017); an approval with a persisted side effect therefore produces *two* audit-grade events with one `permission_id`. CF-WS-H6-02 (to WS-H1/H7).

**F7 — The producer of an audit-grade event must be the kernel, and the check is cheap because the ledger already stamps `producer` and provenance.** ADR-0035's minting rules give `kernel` authority only to the reference runtime; ADR-0026's envelope carries `producer{component_class, component_variant_ref, participant_ref}`; ADR-0024 lists "audit" among MUST-code kernel components. Agent Flight Recorder's completeness limitation ("if an action bypasses the instrumented gateway, the recorder never sees it") is exactly why the effect executor, the reference monitor and the ledger — not tools, hooks, extensions or the model — must be the only producers of audit-grade classes; a hook that could append `security.permission.decided` would be an escape hatch (T-LCD-01's failure signature applied to authority). **Implication (§6.1 rule P):** `append` refuses an audit-grade event whose `producer.component_class` is not in the class's declared producer set or whose provenance authority is below `kernel` (`AuditProducerInvalid`).

**F8 — What the audited runtimes lack (negative results, extending WS-B1 §5):** no chaining or signing (Codex, Pi, OpenHands, opencode, goose, Logos, Managed Agents); no persisted approval request or automated-reviewer verdict (Codex); no event for an *accepted* action or for the confirmation policy in force (OpenHands); no ledger record of policy amendments (Codex, Claude SDK); no cross-run head binding for subagents (all); no audit-coverage check anywhere. Everything in §6 that closes these gaps is `no-precedent / novel` as an integrated contract; each primitive has the standards/production precedent named in F1–F4.

### 4b. Performance evidence (benchmark lifts) — each tagged `provisional` if 2026 single-team/preprint

- Crosby & Wallach (S-WS-H6-02, Tier A, 2009): 1,750 events/s on one core with per-commitment signatures, 10,500/s with signing offloaded; 3 KB membership proof vs 800 MB chain walk at 80 M events. **Use:** the order-of-magnitude argument that tree heads (not chain walks) are what a viewer or results row should verify; WS-B1's Stage-1 target of "tens of events per second per run" is three orders below this, so cost is not a C0 concern.
- Agent Flight Recorder (S-WS-H6-08, `provisional`): ~48 µs median per event, 512 B overhead, 100 % detection of edit/delete/reorder/fork with 0 false positives, anchoring ≈ $2.30 per 100 k events. **Use:** none load-bearing; the detection battery is adopted as the *shape* of AC-H6-1 (which is a correctness test, not a performance claim).
- VCT (S-WS-H6-11, `provisional`): sub-ms cryptographic latency, 0.9 % storage overhead. **Use:** none.
- No benchmark measures "audited vs unaudited harness"; the recommendation rests on Tier-A cryptographic results, RFC 6962/C2SP specifications read in reference source, and two production mechanisms, not on any 2026 number.

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

**Against signed checkpoints and tree heads at C0 ("a hash chain is enough; nobody attacks a research ledger").** None of the seven audited harnesses chains, signs or checkpoints; Managed Agents and Logos ship append-only files with no integrity mechanism and are used at scale; Kiehl (S-195) warns that ledger machinery rarely pays for itself; a research lab's threat is mostly *accident*, not adversary. *Why it does not overturn:* (a) R-2.8.6 is C0/Must by sponsor emphasis and ADR-0029's chain was ratified *because* H6 would attest it; (b) a chain alone cannot answer the two questions an auditor asks (Crosby & Wallach) without walking the whole run, cannot prove to a results row or a bundle that a cited event is in the run it claims (F1), and cannot detect a rewritten *child* run under an untouched parent (F3); (c) accidents are the common case and are what completeness checks catch — truncated tails after crashes (Schneier & Kelsey's 12:10 vs 2:00 case), GC without record, unclosed scopes; (d) the cost is bounded: a compact range per run and one signature per checkpoint, at a cadence that is a MUST-data parameter, with the tree itself derived (a `project` view) rather than stored. What *is* deferred (C2) is everything that needs parties outside the instrument: witnesses, anchoring, receiver receipts.

**Against the kernel as sole signer ("the logger is the logged entity").** Notarized Agents (S-WS-H6-10) argues no self-signed log is evidence against its own author; Agent Flight Recorder concedes post-compromise entries are untrustworthy. *Why it does not overturn at C0:* the trust boundary of HarnessHarness is already the reference monitor's TCB (doc 2 §5.6; ADR-0035 minting rules); the model, tools, hooks, extensions and executors are *untrusted* relative to it and are exactly the parties audit-grade events must not be producible by (F7). A compromised kernel is outside the C0 threat model, stated explicitly (§6.5) rather than hidden. The C2 extension admits external witnesses (C2SP cosignatures) and receiver-side receipts for open-world effects as *hosting-mechanism capabilities*, never as a native-contract change (T-LCD-06).

**Against content-free envelopes ("audit needs the arguments").** A forensic reader wants the command line, not its hash. *Why it does not overturn:* the argument bytes remain available by `ContentAddress` until redacted or GC'd, and the envelope keeps `args_canonical_hash` (B2), the capability `semantic_id`, the `EffectClass`, the `effective_risk_class`, the decision and the decider — enough to answer *who authorized what class of effect on what, with what outcome* after erasure. The IETF draft and Agent Flight Recorder both separate integrity structures (hashes) from payload custody for the same reason; CloudTrail separates digest from log. The residual loss — a redacted argument cannot be re-read — is the intended effect of redaction.

**Against strict producer rules ("hooks and extensions legitimately decide").** Hooks *do* decide in Codex and the Claude SDK (`PreToolUse` → `permissionDecision`), and forbidding them to append audit-grade events looks like a loss of function. *Why it does not overturn:* the hook may *propose* a decision; the reference monitor records it as `security.permission.decided{decider = hook, decider_ref, provenance}` — the producer of the *event* is the kernel, the *decider* is the hook, and the two fields are separate. Nothing a hook can do today becomes impossible; what becomes impossible is a hook writing an approval the monitor never saw (X-CPE's assembly-bug shape, S-103).

**Against per-class retention floors ("keep everything forever is simpler; or: GDPR says delete").** Both extremes exist in practice. *Why it does not overturn:* the floors distinguish the *envelope* (small, structural, content-free, retained for the audit retention period) from *blobs* (large, content, GC-able with a record) — the IETF draft's tombstone + `content_fingerprint` shape satisfies erasure while keeping proof of prior existence, and WS-L4 S8 already requires executed/published versions to be retained. Durations are left to WS-L6 (OQ-085/OQ-112); H6 fixes only the ordering (audit floor ≥ experiment retention ≥ blob tier policy) and that the floor is a manifest field, not code.

**Negative results recorded:** no audited harness records an *accepted* permission as an event of its own; none binds child and parent run heads; none records what left the system (WS-I1 `measurement.export.delivered` remains `no-precedent`); the 2026 designs (S-WS-H6-08/09/11) all assume a single linear record and do not address forks, subagent trees or hosted black-box rows — those parts of §6.3 are novel relative to them.

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

### 6.1 Position and the audit-grade catalogue (ADR WS-H6-adr-1)

**Position.** (1) The audit trail is the run ledger read through `audit_view`; no second store, no sampling, no audit-only identity. (2) A closed set of ledger event classes is **audit-grade**: `durability = ledger` is forced, every mandatory audit field is **inline and content-free** (ids, hashes, classes, enums, `ContentAddress`es — never free text, never offloadable), the class declares its **producer set** (kernel components only) and its **audit obligation** (which other audit-grade events must exist for the record to be complete). (3) The kernel signs periodic **checkpoints** (tree heads over the run's leaf hashes) that are ordinary ledger events and are additionally stored outside the writer. (4) Redaction and GC are audit-grade tombstones that never touch envelopes. (5) **Audit completeness** is a class-scoped veto metric computed from `audit_view`.

**Rule C (content-free envelope).** For every audit-grade class, the schema partitions payload fields into `audit_fields` (inline, canonical, hashed, non-redactable, non-offloadable, no `Text` leaf) and `content_refs` (`ContentAddress`es, redactable by tombstone). A class whose `audit_fields` would exceed the offload threshold is a schema error (`AuditFieldsTooLarge`), never an offload.
**Rule P (producer).** `append` refuses an audit-grade event unless `producer.component_class ∈ class.producers` and the event's provenance `authority = kernel` (`AuditProducerInvalid`). Deciders, proposers and endorsers are *fields*, never producers.
**Rule O (obligation).** Each audit-grade class declares obligations of the form `∀ e:Class . ∃ e':Class' with e'.link = e.key` (e.g. every non-`read_only` `action.effect.intended` has exactly one `authorized | refused` with the same `effect_id` and a `permission_id`); `audit_view.coverage` evaluates them.

### 6.2 Interface-contract sketch (language-agnostic; operations · inputs → outputs · invariants · failure modes)

All operations are pure over the durable prefix or are appends through WS-B1's `append`; every one is implementable out-of-process over the canonical encoding (T-LCD-12). `H` is the identity profile's hash (ADR-0036); leaf hashes are ADR-0029's event `hash`; node hashes are `H(node_tag ∥ left ∥ right)` (RFC 6962 shape).

- `audit_view(run, from?, to?) → AuditView{events_seen: seq_range, chain_ok, checkpoints: [CheckpointStatus{ref, size, tree_head, signatures_ok, consistent_with_prev}], scopes_unclosed: [ScopeId], content_refs: {present, redacted, gc, missing}, producer_violations: [EventRef], coverage: CoverageReport{obligations_checked, unmet: [{obligation_id, subject: EventRef}]}, cross_run: [{edge: EventRef, other_run, anchor_ok}], redactions: [EventRef], sink_deliveries: [SinkDelivery], completeness: AuditCompleteness}` — extends WS-I1's `audit_view` (ADR-0044) with checkpoints, producer, coverage and cross-run sections; `project`-pure with `derived_from` and `view_hash`. *Failures:* `UnknownRun`, `UnknownIdentityProfile`.
- `checkpoint(run, lease, kind ∈ {periodic, effect_terminal, final, rotation, on_demand}) → EventRef` — appends `security.audit.checkpoint` (§6.3) computed from the writer's compact range; signs with the run's registered kernel key. *Invariants:* the signer never signs a head inconsistent with any head it previously signed for this run (C2SP rule) — enforced by requiring `prev_checkpoint.tree_head` to be a consistency-verified prefix; `tree_size = seq + 1`. *Failures:* `Fenced`, `SignerUnavailable` (checkpoint is retried; the run does not proceed past a `final` or `effect_terminal` checkpoint deadline without one — a policy field), `InconsistentHead` (never signed; run flagged).
- `prove_inclusion(run, event: EventRef, at: CheckpointRef) → InclusionProof{leaf_index, tree_size, path[]}`; `verify_inclusion(proof, leaf_hash, tree_head) → ok | RootMismatch` — O(log n); a results row, a bundle member or a viewer link cites `audit_ref{run_id, seq, hash, checkpoint_ref, inclusion_proof?}`.
- `prove_consistency(run, from: CheckpointRef, to: CheckpointRef) → ConsistencyProof{size1, size2, path[]}`; `verify_consistency(proof, head1, head2) → ok | Inconsistent` — what an auditor who stored `head1` runs on seeing `head2`.
- `verify(run, from?, to?) → ok | Tampered{at_seq, kind ∈ {edit, delete, reorder, truncate, late_insert, fork_equivocation, bad_signature, producer}}` — extends ADR-0029's `verify` with checkpoint signature checks, consistency across checkpoints, cross-run anchor checks and producer checks; recomputes under each id's own `idp` (ADR-0036).
- `audit(run, auditor_store) → AuditReceipt{heads_recorded[], verdict}` — the *auditor* role (Crosby & Wallach): a subscriber that recomputes heads independently, compares with signed checkpoints, and stores `(run_id, checkpoint_ref, tree_head, signatures)` in a store the writer cannot reach (results store row, bundle manifest, parent run, or a lab-wide audit index). Two auditors seeing different heads for one `(run, size)` is `Equivocation`.
- `redact(run, lease, targets[], reason_code, endorser, basis) → EventRef` — WS-B1's `redact` with H6's preconditions: `targets` may name only `content_refs` (never an envelope or an `audit_field`); `endorser.authority ≥ principal` with `basis ∈ {approval, policy_rule}` (a kernel secret scanner uses `policy_rule` with the rule ref); appends `lifecycle.ledger.redacted` then deletes bytes. *Failures:* `NotRedactable(field)`, `IllegitimateEndorsement`.
- `gc(run, addresses[], policy_ref) → EventRef` — appends `lifecycle.ledger.gc` before deleting; refused for any address referenced by a retained bundle or a live fork prefix (`Pinned`). *Invariant:* after `gc`, `audit_view.content_refs.missing = ∅`.
- `coverage(run) → CoverageReport` — evaluates every Rule-O obligation over the durable prefix; obligations are declared data (`AuditObligation{id, class, quantifier, target_class, link_field, terminal_only?}`) in the audit policy, versioned in the bundle.
- `audit_completeness(run) → AuditCompleteness` — §6.4.
- `export_audit(run, sink) → Delivery{loss_report}` — lowering of `audit_view` to an external audit-record convention (e.g. an IETF-draft-shaped record set, a `gen_ai.*` sink) is a WS-I1 export with a **telemetry loss report** that must list any dropped `audit_field`, decider, basis or proof (T-LCD-11); import of an external audit record yields events with `origin = import`, `authority = unverified` (ADR-0021/0033) and `participant_class = hosted`.

**Correlation rules (I1 ↔ H6 ↔ J5 ↔ L4).**
1. The unit of citation is the `EventRef` (ADR-0027); an `audit_ref` adds `hash` and a `checkpoint_ref` so a citation is verifiable without the run's full chain.
2. Every span in `trace_view` is a scope opened by a ledger event (ADR-0042); an audit-grade event inside a scope is reachable from the span by `scope{}`; the *permission* scope kind (M10) is the audit-grade `decided` event; there is no audit fact without a span-addressable event.
3. Cross-process: the propagation context's `tracestate` member `hh=run:<run_id>;ev:<event_id>` (ADR-0042) lifts into `causes[]`; an external receipt (MCP server, subprocess, receiver) that carries it is linked, never trusted (authority `external`/`unverified`).
4. Cross-run: `control.subagent.spawned.parent_head_at_spawn` ↔ child `lifecycle.run.created.parent_anchor`; `control.subagent.result.child_head` ↔ child `final` checkpoint; `lifecycle.run.forked.source_head_hash` ↔ source checkpoint at `at_seq`. `verify` follows these edges; a mismatch is `Tampered{fork_equivocation}`.
5. Results and bundles: every results row (WS-J5) carries `audit_ref` of the run's `final` checkpoint and the run's `audit_completeness`; the bundle manifest's `traces.audit_tree_head` (ADR-0038) is the `final` checkpoint's head; the bundle attestation (in-toto shape) has the bundle `version_id` as subject and the checkpoint refs in its predicate.
6. Metrics: every `MetricValue.computed_from[]` (ADR-0044) is a list of `EventRef`s; a scorecard number is therefore auditable to leaf hashes under a signed head.

### 6.3 Data-model sketch

`security.audit.checkpoint{ kind ∈ {periodic, effect_terminal, final, rotation, on_demand}, origin: RunId, tree_size: int (= seq of this event, exclusive), tree_head: bytes (RFC 6962 root over leaf hashes [0, tree_size)), chain_hash: bytes (= prev_hash of this event; the linear head, kept for O(1) checks), prev_checkpoint: {ref: EventRef, tree_size, tree_head}?, consistency_proof: path[]? (prev → this; omitted when the auditor can recompute), cross_run_anchors: [{edge: EventRef, other_run: RunId, other_head{tree_size, tree_head}}], idp, rehash?: {idp', chain_hash', tree_head', bridge_record_ref} (rotation kind only, ADR-0036 step 3), signatures: [{signer_name, key_id (H(pk) prefix), alg_ref (pinned by idp), sig}], witness_cosignatures: [{witness_name, key_id, timestamp, sig}] (C2), audit_policy_ref: version_id }`. `audit_fields` = all of the above; nothing is offloaded (the proof path is O(log n) and bounded by policy; if a path would exceed the threshold it is omitted and recomputed on demand).

`AuditPolicy{ version_id, cadence: {every_n_events?, every_effect_terminal_of_class ⊆ {irreversible, compensable}, at_finish: true (fixed), on_rotation: true (fixed), max_unsigned_ms?}, signer_key_ids[], witness_policy?: {required: int, witnesses[]}, retention: {audit_envelope_floor, checkpoint_floor, blob_tiers_ref}, obligations: [AuditObligation], redaction_authority: {min_authority = principal, allowed_bases = [approval, policy_rule]} }` — a MUST-data record referenced from the run manifest (`audit_policy_ref`) and pinned in the bundle; the *mechanism* (hashing, signing, checks) is MUST-code (ADR-0024 lists audit as kernel code).

`AuditCompleteness{ chain_ok: bool, checkpoints_ok: bool, scopes_closed: bool, blobs_accounted: bool, producers_ok: bool, coverage_ok: bool, cross_run_ok: bool | n/a, headline: bool = ∧ of the applicable components, n_a: [{component, reason ∈ {class, observability, not_run}}], evidence: view_hash }` — refines ADR-0044's three-term definition (the three terms are `chain_ok`, `scopes_closed`, `blobs_accounted`); for hosted rows the components that need `events`/`ledger` observability the participant lacks render `n/a` (T-LCD-15), never `false`.

`KernelSignerRecord{ key_id, public_key_ref (ContentAddress), alg_ref, custodian ∈ {kernel_process, helper_binary, external_kms}, valid_from, valid_until, superseded_by? }` — listed in `InstrumentRecord` (ADR-0038) and the run manifest; the private key is never readable by the model, executors, tools, hooks or extensions (an H3 credential with `readers = {kernel}`); rotation is a `supersedes` on the record (ADR-0037) plus a `rotation` checkpoint co-signed by old and new keys.

**Threat model (stated, per ADR §3.3 (d)).** T1 *post-hoc storage tamperer* (edit, delete, reorder, truncate, late insert, replace a fork prefix): detected by chain + checkpoints + auditor-held heads — C0. T2 *compromised in-run component below the kernel* (model, tool, hook, extension, executor, hosted participant): cannot produce or alter audit-grade events (Rule P), cannot widen authority silently (ADR-0035 + F6), and everything it *did* through the kernel is recorded — C0. T3 *a run's writer replaced by a stale/rogue writer*: fencing (`lease_generation`) plus checkpoints signed only by the kernel key — C0. T4 *split view / equivocation across forks and delegation*: cross-run anchors and two-auditor comparison — C0 within the instrument; external witnesses C2. T5 *compromised kernel or signer key*: **out of scope at C0** (stated; forward security in the Schneier–Kelsey sense applies only to entries before compromise, and only if an auditor holds an earlier head); mitigations are C2 (witness cosignatures, receiver receipts) and operational (key custody, OQ-WS-H6-02). T6 *omission by bypass* (an action that never passed the kernel): out of scope for the audit trail — this is what containment (WS-H4) and effect mediation (WS-E5/B5) exist for; `coverage` can only check what has a proposal.

### 6.4 Redaction, GC and retention — the resolution (ADR WS-H6-adr-3)

R1. **Envelopes are never redacted or collected.** Every audit-grade event's `audit_fields` are retained for at least `retention.audit_envelope_floor` (a manifest field; default proposed: the life of the instrument installation, i.e., no automatic deletion — durations are OQ-WS-H6-05 / OQ-085 for WS-L6); checkpoints and cross-run anchors likewise.
R2. **Content leaves by tombstone only** (`redacted` with an endorser and basis; `gc` with a policy ref). The chain and every proof remain valid because leaf hashes cover `ContentAddress`es (ADR-0029 d.4); the tombstone's `content_fingerprints[]` are those addresses, so prior existence remains provable (IETF-draft mechanism; Crosby & Wallach's "agreed-upon deletion" with proofs).
R3. **Secrets never enter the ledger** (ADR-0026 exclusion); an accidental leak is a `redacted` event with `basis = policy_rule` from the kernel's scanner or `approval` from a principal; H3's credential events carry only references.
R4. **Ordering of floors:** audit envelope floor ≥ bundle/experiment retention ≥ blob tier policy; a bundle whose blobs were GC'd keeps `audit_completeness.blobs_accounted = true` (records exist) and reports reproducibility level R0 with `missing[]` (OQ-112 for WS-L4/L6).
R5. **Redaction is itself audited and endorsed**; an unauthorized `redact` is `IllegitimateEndorsement`; a redaction of an `audit_field` is `NotRedactable`.
R6. **Encryption at rest of blobs** (Agent Flight Recorder's per-event keys) is compatible — the hash is over plaintext bytes at `put_blob` time — but is WS-H3's design; H6 requires only that decryption failure is reported as `missing` with reason, never as `tampered`.

### 6.5 Audit completeness as a scorecard metric (WS-I2 dimension *security*)

`MetricDeclaration{name: audit_completeness, dimension: security, level: run, value_type: bool + component vector, direction: true-is-better, requires_observability: {events} (components needing `ledger` render n/a), applies_to_classes: {native, hosted}, admissible_granularities: all, detector_classes_allowed: {deterministic}, oracle_classes_allowed: {trace_predicate}, replicate_reducer: all-true share with (c, n), outcome_class_policy: computed for every outcome class incl. infrastructure_failure, headline: false, veto: true, charged_to: instrument, computed_from: audit_view}`. Veto semantics (ADR-0047): a run with `headline = false` is *success-with-veto*, never headline success — this is H6's input to OQ-128 (audit completeness is a C0/Stage 3 veto invariant). Companion declared metrics: `coverage_unmet_count`, `redaction_count`, `checkpoint_interval_events`, `unsigned_tail_events` (events after the last checkpoint at `finished`), `permission_decisions_by_decider_and_scope` (the audit view of approval fatigue for WS-H7).

### 6.6 Acceptance-criteria sketch

- **AC-H6-1 Tamper battery.** For a Stage-2 reference run: (a) flip one byte in a stored event → `Tampered{edit, at_seq}`; (b) delete an interior event → `Tampered{delete}`; (c) swap two events → `Tampered{reorder}`; (d) truncate after the last checkpoint → `Tampered{truncate}` (unsigned tail detected by the auditor's stored head or by `finished` without `final`); (e) insert an event with a valid-looking `prev_hash` → `Tampered{late_insert}` (checkpoint consistency fails); (f) replace a fork's source prefix → `Tampered{fork_equivocation}`; (g) rewrite a child run under an untouched parent → `Tampered{fork_equivocation}` at the parent's `result` anchor; (h) re-sign a checkpoint with a non-registered key → `Tampered{bad_signature}`. Zero false positives on an untouched corpus of ≥ 100 runs incl. crashed and resumed runs.
- **AC-H6-2 Content-free envelopes.** Redact every `content_ref` in a run and GC every blob: `verify = ok`, `audit_completeness.headline` unchanged, and every audit-grade event still answers *who / what class / on what / decided by whom / outcome*; attempting to redact an `audit_field` is `NotRedactable`.
- **AC-H6-3 Producer rule.** A hook, tool, extension or hosted adapter attempting to append any audit-grade class (fixture: a `PreToolUse`-style hook appending `security.permission.decided`) is refused with `AuditProducerInvalid`; the same decision routed through the monitor appears with `decider = hook`.
- **AC-H6-4 Decision completeness.** For fixtures with allow / deny / timeout / abort / cached / policy-amending approvals (Codex `ReviewDecision` variants as the fixture set), every `action.tool.proposed` of non-`read_only` class has exactly one `security.permission.decided`; every `decision_scope ≠ once` has a `security.permission.granted`; every persisted widening has a `lifecycle.definition.changed` with human origin; `approval_wait_ms` (WS-I1 M10) is computable from `requested_at`.
- **AC-H6-5 Coverage.** A fixture that abandons an effect, exhausts a budget and leaves an `unknown` effect at `finished` yields `coverage.unmet = ∅` only when the three `lifecycle.escalation.raised` events exist; removing one flips `coverage_ok = false` and the run to success-with-veto in the scorecard.
- **AC-H6-6 Proofs.** For every event in a Stage-3 run, `prove_inclusion` against the `final` checkpoint verifies; `prove_consistency` between every consecutive checkpoint pair verifies; proof size is O(log n) (measured, reported).
- **AC-H6-7 Auditor independence.** Two auditors fed by `subscribe` and a third reading the results-store rows agree on every `(run, tree_size, tree_head)`; a deliberately equivocating writer (fixture) is detected by the comparison.
- **AC-H6-8 Rotation (with WS-L4 AC-L4-6).** After `idp/1 → idp/2`, every pre-rotation checkpoint verifies under `idp/1`, the `rotation` checkpoint carries a verified `rehash`, and post-rotation checkpoints chain to it.
- **AC-H6-9 Export loss (T-LCD-11).** Lowering `audit_view` to an IETF-draft-shaped record set and to a `gen_ai.*` sink produces loss reports naming dropped fields (decider provenance, basis, `effective_risk_class`, proofs); lifting the draft's `prev_hash` chain yields `unverified` rows linked by alias.
- **AC-H6-10 Class scoping (T-LCD-15).** A hosted run at `observability_level = {events}` renders `producers_ok`, `coverage_ok` and `cross_run_ok` as `n/a{observability}` and `chain_ok` over the adapter-appended rows as a real value; never 0/false by absence.
- **AC-H6-11 Crash vs truncation.** A run killed mid-batch and resumed (`lifecycle.run.resumed{recovery_decision}`) is `chain_ok`; the same prefix with the resume event removed is `Tampered{truncate}` when a later head exists, and `audit_completeness.scopes_closed = false` otherwise (Schneier–Kelsey's abnormal-shutdown distinction, achieved by the recovery event rather than a marker).

### 6.7 Build-stage suggestion

- **C0 / Stage 1:** audit-grade class list with `audit_fields`/`content_refs` partition and producer sets in the schema (Rule C, Rule P enforced at `append`); `verify` over the chain (ADR-0029); `decision` payload completion (F5/F6 fields); `audit_policy_ref` and `signer_key_ids` in the manifest; `AuditObligation` records (evaluated, not yet vetoing).
- **C0 / Stage 2:** compact-range tree heads, `security.audit.checkpoint` with kernel signatures at the policy cadence, `prove_*/verify_*`, cross-run anchors, `lifecycle.ledger.gc`, redaction endorsement rules, `lifecycle.escalation.*`, `audit_view` with coverage; H6 attests `audit_view` (ADR-0044 consequence).
- **C0 / Stage 3 (evaluation-first):** `audit_completeness` in the metric registry as a veto (OQ-128); AC-H6-1…11 in the suite; export loss reports; `audit_ref` on results rows (J5).
- **C1 / Stage 4:** hosted rows (`n/a` components), bundle attestation with checkpoint refs (I3/L4), `rotation` checkpoints end-to-end, lab-wide audit index (auditor store).
- **C2 / Stage 4+:** witness cosignatures (C2SP shape) from a process outside the host; receiver-attested receipts for open-world effects as a hosting-mechanism capability (T-LCD-07 vector entry `receipts`); blob encryption with per-event keys (H3).

### 6.8 T-LCD statement (ADR-0007 decision 5) and ADR-0024 rung

Satisfies **T-LCD-12** (every operation is a record-level function; auditors run out-of-process), **T-LCD-15** (`AuditCompleteness` components render `n/a{reason}` for hosted rows; declared `requires_observability`), **T-LCD-11** (audit export is a lowering with a loss report; lifted audit records are `unverified`), **T-LCD-06** (no field or class exists for hosted participants; hosted rows use the native envelope), **T-LCD-13** (the compliance chain events remain hashed leaves citable by `audit_ref`; not made audit-grade so the measurement/audit distinction stays visible), **T-LCD-14** (`permission_decisions_by_decider_and_scope` lets approval-policy arms be compared under matched approval budgets), **T-LCD-05** applied reflexively (cadence and obligations are MUST-data with expiry-free defaults; a model-conditioned audit rule would need a debt record — none is proposed). Not applicable: T-01/-02/-03/-04/-07/-08/-09/-10 (no model-facing surface). **ADR-0024 rung:** hashing, signing, proofs, producer/obligation checks and completeness are MUST-code (kernel, "audit" in the MUST-code list); `AuditPolicy` (cadence, obligations, retention floors, redaction authority, witness policy) is MUST-data.

### 6.9 Host-ecosystem requirements (properties only; ADR-0050 §8(a))

The ADR-0050 kernel ecosystem must provide, as properties: a streaming standardized 256-bit hash with domain-tag prefixing (ADR-0036 H2); a standardized deterministic signature scheme with key-id derivation and constant-time verification, usable from the kernel process and the sandbox helper but not from executors; an O(log n) compact-range implementation over the ledger's `seq` (a few hundred lines; reference semantics in S-WS-H6-03); a key store with `readers = {kernel}` custody (WS-H3); the out-of-process auditor as a subscriber over the WS-L1 §6.5 boundary (`stream_events`) with byte-identical *canonical* events (CF-058 wording). Nothing here fires an ADR-0009 gate; all are satisfied by the ADR-0050 kernel ecosystem per ADR-0050 §8(a).

## 10. Confidence

**High** for the position (no second store; audit = ledger + checkpoints + checks) and for the commitment shape (per-run chain → tree heads → signed checkpoints → auditor-held heads): Tier-A theory (Schneier–Kelsey, Crosby–Wallach, RFC 6962), reference source read at pinned commits (transparency-dev/merkle, Rekor), two production mechanisms (Rekor/C2SP, CloudTrail) and the ratified ADR-0026/0029/0042/0044 all converge; the 2026 designs (S-WS-H6-08/09/11) corroborate the catalogue and are not load-bearing. **High** for the content-free envelope rule and producer rule (direct consequences of ADR-0029, ADR-0035 and doc 2 §5.6; the Codex `log_only`/`trace_safe` split is the same idea). **Medium** for the catalogue's completeness on planes whose Phase 2 owners are concurrent (H1, H3, H4, H5, H7, F2) — fields may be renamed at synthesis; the obligation list is deliberately data so it can grow without an ADR. **Medium** for the threat-model boundary (T5 out of scope) — defensible for a research instrument, contested for an organizational deployment (OQ-027 territory). **Medium-low** for cadence/cost numbers (unmeasured until Stage 2) and for anything about witnesses/receipts (C2, precedented only in transparency-log ecosystems and one preprint).
