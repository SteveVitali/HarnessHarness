## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream decides **how a proposed effect that the reference monitor cannot decide deterministically gets decided — by whom, at what granularity, with what evidence, under what budget, and with what record** — and how the design keeps the human decision *informative* rather than reflexive. Concretely it fixes: (i) the **escalation decision inputs and policy model**: the closed input record the kernel hands the approval component, the default rows of the policy table Π(EffectClass, effective authority) → `allow | ask | deny` that WS-H1 owns (OQ-101) and that this dossier supplies as a *floor narrowed by containment and attendance*, and the **reviewer chain** that resolves `ask` (deterministic rule → lease → hook → automatic reviewer → human) with a never-auto set; (ii) the **approval granularity ladder** G0 per-effect / G1 per-pattern lease / G2 per-class grant, including the exact lease key (OQ-091); (iii) the **approval request/response contract** — synchronous, asynchronous (durable, lease-bound, decision-before-`prepared`) and ABI-facing (lowering to ACP `session/request_permission`, MCP elicitation and hook forms, with a lowering loss report); (iv) **fatigue-mitigation mechanisms** with the evidence for each; (v) **approvals as a budgeted resource** — what `approvals.requested`/`approvals.granted` count (OQ-096), what exhaustion does, and the safeguard around `ask → deny`; (vi) **delegation of approval authority** (approver grants) and the **audit obligations** every decision carries for WS-H6.

It explicitly leaves to neighbours: the reference-monitor kernel itself, the seven-check set, the authority-handle runtime and the *ownership* of Π (**WS-H1** — this dossier supplies Π's default rows, the `ask` resolution semantics and the reviewer chain; H1 arbitrates where Π lives in the kernel and answers OQ-086 on shell reversibility, consulting §6.2 here); taint/reader enforcement that would add C2 rows to Π (**WS-H2**); the audit chain, attestation and tamper evidence of approval records (**WS-H6** — this dossier fixes what an approval record *must contain*); the durable suspend/wakeup mechanics that make asynchronous approvals survive restarts (**WS-B3** — this dossier fixes the events and the resume invariant); the executor and sandbox that make `reversible` cheap and so lower the base rate of prompts (**WS-B5/E5**); the judge governance an automatic reviewer inherits (**WS-G3**, ADR-0047); the rendering of approval prompts in CLI/web and the ACP session artefact (**WS-K1/K2/E4/K4**); the organizational layer where approvals become tickets, reviewers and on-call rotations (**WS-L8**, OQ-017/OQ-027); sub-agent spawning and the bubbling of a child's `ask` to the root principal (**WS-F3**); the control envelope that stops a run when an approvals budget is exhausted (**WS-F2**).

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence (a failure mode exists / an architecture is implementable)

**M1 — Approval prompts habituate, and the habituation is a property of the prompt stream, not of agents.** Tier-A HCI results predate agents: repeated interception dialogs train blind acceptance (S-328; 80k users), install-time permission warnings are attended by 17% and understood by 3% (S-329), and the *design* of a warning moves adherence by a factor of four (S-327). Anthropic's telemetry re-observes the same curve inside an agent harness: ~93% approval, attention decaying with volume (S-053). The mechanism is therefore established at Tier A; the agent-specific number is Tier B corroboration. Consequence: any design that "adds a prompt" for safety must be evaluated by its effect on the *information content* of the remaining prompts, not by the prompt's existence.

**M2 — The base rate of prompts is set by containment, not by the approval component.** Deterministic sandboxing (writable roots + egress policy) removed 84% of prompts before any classifier ran (S-053); Codex's `assess_patch_safety` auto-approves writes inside writable roots (S-107 `safety.rs`, via ADR-0031); Claude Code auto-approves "read-only actions and file edits in your working directory" without consulting the classifier (S-330). Felt et al.'s guideline for "automatic grant" of low-severity/reversible permissions (S-326) is the same rule stated a decade earlier. Consequence: the `reversible ∧ workspace_local` row of Π is `allow`, and the approval component's job begins where containment ends — which is why ADR-0031's per-class table is the *floor* this dossier narrows from, never a ceiling it relaxes.

**M3 — Every audited harness resolves `ask` through an ordered reviewer chain, and the order is load-bearing.** Codex: hooks → automatic reviewer (if strict/required) → user (S-107 `approvals.rs` "Approval precedence is: 1. Hooks 2. … Guardian. Else, user."). Claude Code: explicit rules → containment auto-allow → classifier → deny-with-reason (S-330). Inspect: policy approvers in order, chaining on `escalate`, rejecting when none remains (S-136 `_policy.py` L61–82). goose: all inspectors run, `permission` inspector consolidates into `approved / needs_approval / denied` (S-112). The three failure modes visible in source: (a) a cheap earlier stage *shadowing* the human (claude-agent-sdk's `can_use_tool will not be invoked … auto-approves every tool call before the callback is consulted`, S-109 L1806–1862; Claude Code drops blanket `Bash(*)` allow rules on entering auto mode for the same reason); (b) a failing stage failing *open* (goose `ToolInspectionManager` "Continue with other inspectors even if one fails") versus failing *closed* (OpenHands ensemble "contributes HIGH"; Codex `denied("automatic approval review could not complete")`); (c) a stage that can *widen* (Codex `ApprovedExecpolicyAmendment`, `NetworkPolicyAmendment`) sitting in the same sum as stages that only narrow. Consequence: the chain must be a closed, ordered sum in which every stage but the human may only narrow or pass, failure is `deny` for anything above `reversible`, and rule pre-emption of the human is a *declared* property of the mode, not an accident.

**M4 — Automatic reviewers are implementable as attenuated, evidence-restricted processes — and they are model-conditioned components.** Codex's Guardian runs as a separate session with `approval_policy = Never`, a permission profile intersected with read-only, MCP servers cleared, memories/skills/hooks disabled, and its own token budget (`reviewer_config.rs` L29–120); its policy template scores `risk_level ∈ {low, medium, high, critical}` × `user_authorization ∈ {unknown, low, medium, high}` and derives `outcome`, treating only user/developer messages, `AGENTS.md` and `request_user_input` responses as trusted evidence (`assets/guardian/policy_template.md`). Claude Code's classifier sees "user messages, tool calls … and your CLAUDE.md content. Tool results are stripped" and reuses verdicts per host/port "until new content enters the conversation" (S-330). goose's judge identifies read-only requests "by id and arguments" and keeps untrusted request instructions out of its system prompt (S-112 `permission_judge.rs` tests). These are three independent instances of the same architecture: *the reviewer's evidence set is exactly the content whose authority class is ≥ `principal`, plus the proposal itself* — which maps one-to-one onto ADR-0033's classes. The reviewer's quality is model-conditioned (Claude Code pins the classifier model per session and validates it on first use; Codex `auto_review_required_for_model`), so under ADR-0020/T-LCD-05 it is a conditioned rule with an assumption-debt record, and its FN rate is a class-scoped metric, not a constant.

**M5 — Session approvals are pattern leases, and their key determines both the fatigue relief and the injection exposure.** Codex caches `ApprovedForSession` under `ApprovalCacheKey{ExecCommand(UnifiedExecApprovalKey) | ApplyPatch(ApplyPatchApprovalKey)}` *paired with the exec-policy fingerprint*, so a policy change invalidates every lease (`approvals.rs` `request_user_approval`, `sandboxing.rs` `with_cached_approval`). goose keys on `tool_name:H(arguments)` with an optional expiry and separates judge-derived grants (`smart_approve`) from user grants (`user`). opencode keys on `permission × wildcard pattern` with last-match-wins and an `always` reply that appends allow rules. ACP exposes exactly `allow_once | allow_always | reject_once | reject_always`. Claude Code invalidates network allows when new content enters the conversation and drops *all* cached verdicts on a mode or rule change. The exposure is documented adversarially: ATR-2026-00118's "embedding risky operations within batches of benign ones" and "just click approve for all" are attacks on lease and batch scope (`provisional`, threat catalogue). Consequence (OQ-091 answer, §6.3): the lease key is `H(ToolCapability semantic_id ∥ ActionPattern canonical ∥ scope ∥ policy_fingerprint)` where `ActionPattern` is a capability-declared *projection* of the arguments (command prefix, path-prefix set, host:port, server+tool) — never a bare `effect_id`/call id (too narrow to relieve anything) and never a bare tool name (too wide); leases on `external`-scope effects are re-validated when the per-call `context_label` drops.

**M6 — Unattended runs need a defined resolution of `ask`, and the audited defaults disagree.** Codex `AskForApproval::Never` returns failures to the model "and never escalated to the user"; Claude Code `dontAsk` auto-denies and `-p` runs have "no prompt to fall back to"; pi-mono's gate blocks "by default" without a UI; ACP lets clients "automatically allow or reject … according to the user settings"; OpenHands parks the conversation in `WAITING_FOR_CONFIRMATION` and exposes a REST decision endpoint (a *deferred* ask); MCP's `InputRequiredResult{requestState}` lets a server hand back opaque state and be retried later; LangGraph's `interrupt` persists the graph and resumes on `Command(resume=…)` — but "re-executing all logic" of the node. Cursor's production stance is that blocking is the expensive failure (S-052). Consequence (§6.4): `ask` under `attendance = unattended` resolves per configuration to `deny` (default), `defer` (park the *intent*, before `prepared`, and wake on decision — never inside an effect), or `auto_review` (with the never-auto set excluded); there is no fourth option, and "wait forever" is admissible only under `attendance = interactive` (Inspect's stance) with the wait accounted as `time.human_wait_ms`.

**M7 — Explanations are both the fatigue mitigation with the best Tier-A support and an attack surface.** Warning adherence is design-sensitive (S-327); Codex carries `justification`, `approval_reason` and `retry_reason` to the reviewer and user; Inspect attaches `explanation` to every decision. But ATR-2026-00118 lists "minimizing language" as an attack pattern, and Claude Code deliberately reduced the model-visible denial reason to the fixed text "Blocked by classifier" (S-330). Consequence: the *kernel-generated* explanation (rule id, risk factors, authority of the proposal, budget state) is a typed record rendered by the surface; the *model-authored* justification is a `Text` leaf with `authority = delegate`, rendered as such and never merged into the kernel text (§6.5).

**M8 — Approval requests are already an effect domain and an accountable resource in the ratified contracts.** HIR/1 has `EffectDomain = … permission_request …` and `DecisionPoint = … authorize, escalate …` (ADR-0016); the kernel decision is `{effective_risk_class, allow | ask | deny, reason, cache_key?}` (ADR-0031); `approval` endorses one `(effect_id, args_canonical_hash)` and never content (ADR-0035); `approvals.{requested, granted}` and `time.human_wait_ms` are kernel counters and "exhaustion of an approvals budget converts the next `ask` into `deny`, never into `allow`" (ADR-0039/0040); `security.permission.requested/decided` exist with requests `ephemeral` (ADR-0026, AC-B1-12); the run-suspend/wakeup events exist for WS-B3 (`control.wakeup.scheduled/fired`). Codex's own `RequestPermissions` action — the agent asking for a widened `RequestPermissionProfile` scoped `Turn | Session`, reviewed like any other action — is the source-code precedent for treating a permission request as an effect through Π. This dossier's job is to fill those slots, not to add a second vocabulary.

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

- **Against per-class floors (R1).** Reversibility of arbitrary shell commands is undecidable and every production classifier is heuristic (ADR-0031 (c); OQ-086 open); an approval keyed on a wrong class is worse than a prompt, because the human never sees it. *Does not overturn:* the floor is monotone (`effective_risk_class = max_by_danger`, `unknown ⇒ irreversible`) so mis-assessment errs toward asking; the residual cost is prompt volume, which §6.5 attacks with leases and containment rather than by lowering the class.
- **Against an automatic reviewer (R2).** ~17% of "overeager" actions pass Anthropic's classifier (S-053); the reviewer is a model and therefore manipulable, model-conditioned and non-stationary; it adds a model round-trip per non-exempt action (Claude Code documents the cost explicitly); and an approval policy that changes silently with the reviewer model is an uncontrolled factor in the Harness Lab. *Does not overturn:* (i) the reviewer only ever resolves `ask` *inside a sandbox* (S-053's own framing) and never touches the never-auto set; (ii) its verdict is an *input* to a `policy_rule`, so the endorser remains the kernel executing a sealed rule and ADR-0035's "a `delegate` origin never endorses" holds; (iii) it is a `Validator{kind: judge}` variant with `ProfileRef`, `calibration_ref` and a debt record (ADR-0047/0020), so its FN rate is measured per profile and can expire; (iv) fail-closed on error and a repeated-denial fallback (Claude Code's 3-consecutive/20-total precedent) bound the damage of a wrong reviewer.
- **Against pattern leases (R3).** Leases are the channel through which injected or drifting proposals ride an earlier human decision (ATR-2026-00118 "embedding", `provisional`); a lease can outlive the conditions that justified it (goose adds expiry precisely for this); `allow_always` in ACP cannot carry a narrowed pattern (declared loss). *Does not overturn:* the key includes the policy fingerprint (Codex) and a capability-declared pattern projection, `external`-scope leases are re-validated on a `context_label` drop, leases carry `max_uses`/expiry and are never granted for `irreversible ∧ external` by any automatic stage.
- **Against approvals budgets and `ask → deny` (R4).** Converting a mid-task `ask` into `deny` produces stuck or silently incomplete runs and pushes models to route around a denial — Codex re-runs a denied sandboxed command unsandboxed on approval (WS-B2 §4a), and Claude Code documents "If the classifier blocks, Claude receives the reason and tries an alternative": *denial-driven evasion* is a real trajectory pattern. *Does not overturn:* ADR-0040 already fixes the conversion direction (never `allow`); §6.6 adds that a conversion is a ledger event visible to the principal, that the model's refusal observation carries `reason = approvals_exhausted`, that alternatives cannot widen (attenuation), and that repeated denials suspend rather than loop.
- **Against asynchronous `defer` (R5).** LangGraph's interrupt re-executes the node on resume; Cursor reports blocking as the expensive failure; OpenHands' implicit confirmation on re-run is a replay hazard (CF-063). *Does not overturn:* `defer` is admitted only at the `intended → authorized` boundary (before `prepared`, ADR-0030 write-ahead), the parked record is the durable `security.permission.pending` event, and resume is by reference to `permission_id` — so nothing is re-executed. Whether to *use* `defer` rather than `deny`-and-continue is a per-configuration choice; the default for unattended runs is `deny`.
- **Against explain-why (R6).** Explanations are an attack channel (minimizing language, ATR) and a fatigue accelerant if verbose; Claude Code reduced them to a fixed string. *Does not overturn:* the kernel explanation is typed and record-derived; the model's justification is a labelled `delegate`-authority `Text` leaf; surfaces may collapse it.
- **Against batching (R7).** Batches hide risky items among benign ones (ATR); goose/OpenHands batch *inspection* but still decide per item. *Does not overturn:* a batch is one prompt over N per-item endorsements with a single risk-class ceiling and per-item rendering; `irreversible` is never batched.
- **Against prompts at all (R8).** Position-paper argument that oversight competencies atrophy (S-331, `provisional`, no data) would suggest reducing human decisions to near zero. *Does not overturn:* it is argument only; the Tier-A evidence says prompts are design-sensitive, not futile; and the never-auto set exists precisely because some decisions must remain human under any autonomy setting.

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

### 6.1 Escalation decision inputs (closed record the kernel hands the approval component)

`EscalationInput{effect_id, run_id, turn_id, tool_call_id, capability_ref: VersionedRef (semantic_id), effect_class: EffectClass, effective_risk_class (ADR-0031), args_canonical_hash, effective_authority: AuthorityClass (ADR-0034 `min(authority(proposer), context_label.authority)`), context_label: Label, proposal_provenance: ProvenanceRecord, grant: PermissionRef? (coverage result of check 2), compensation_plan_id?, dedup_support, baseline_ref?, containment: {inside_writable_roots: bool, egress_allowlisted: bool, sandboxed: bool}, attendance ∈ {interactive, async, unattended}, budget_state: {approvals_remaining?, human_wait_remaining_ms?, approvals_mode}, lease_hit: ApprovalLeaseRef?, pending_batch_id?, retry_of: permission_id?, delegation_depth}`. Every field is a record the monitor already reads (ADR-0035 "reads records only"); nothing is free text. Invariant: the input is derived from the ledger and the sealed definition; the model's `justification` is *not* an input to Π (it is rendered to the human only).

### 6.2 The policy model Π and the reviewer chain

**Π (owned by WS-H1, rows supplied here; OQ-101).** `Π: (effective_risk_class, EffectDomain, effective_authority, containment, attendance) → {allow, ask(target), deny}` is MUST-code in its floor and MUST-data in its narrowing (ADR-0024): the floor rows are the ADR-0031 delivery table's "approval default" column; a sealed definition may only *narrow* them via `authority_cap`-bounded policy leaves; the Lab may vary them as a `configuration-level` factor. Default floor rows (a `deny` row wins over `ask`, `ask` over `allow`):

| row | condition | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Π-1 | `read_only ∧ world = closed` | `allow` (ADR-0035 check-4 exemption) |
| Π-2 | `reversible ∧ workspace_local ∧ inside_writable_roots` | `allow` |
| Π-3 | `reversible ∧ ¬inside_writable_roots` | `ask` |
| Π-4 | `compensable ∧ compensation_plan registered ∧ grant covers compensator` | `allow` |
| Π-5 | `compensable` otherwise | `ask` |
| Π-6 | `irreversible` | `ask` (G0 only; lease only by explicit human `allow_lease`) |
| Π-7 | `effective_authority ≤ external` (proposal driven by untrusted content) | raise one step: `allow → ask`, `ask → deny` for `irreversible ∨ scope = external` |
| Π-8 | domain `permission_request` (widened grant, Codex `RequestPermissions` precedent) | `ask` to a human with authority `principal`; never automatic; result is a `Permission` with `HirDiff.authority_delta = widening` of human origin (ADR-0017) |
| Π-9 | domain `spawn_process` with child permissions ⊄ parent | `deny` (attenuation, ADR-0016 invariant) |
| Π-10 | domain `secret_access` reaching a model-visible sink, or `spend` above the budget's soft threshold | `ask`; `deny` if `effective_authority ≤ external` |
| Π-11 | `unknown` class | treated as `irreversible ∧ external` (ADR-0031) ⇒ Π-6/Π-7 |
| Π-12 | `attendance = unattended ∧ verdict = ask` | resolve by `UnattendedPolicy ∈ {deny (default), defer, auto_review}`; the never-auto set is excluded from `auto_review` |

**Never-auto set (constitutional, cannot be narrowed by any layer or mode):** Π-8; `irreversible ∧ external` when `effective_authority ≤ external`; any effect whose `Permission` was revoked (`StaleIndex`, ADR-0037); explicit `ask` rules in the sealed definition; `message_human` effects that *are* the consent step for another effect; endorsements of `scope = user` persistence (ADR-0035 ceiling). Precedent: Claude Code's "actions no mode auto-approves"; Codex `Granular.request_permissions`/`mcp_elicitations` flags.

**Reviewer chain (resolution of `ask`).** `ReviewerRef ∈ {policy_rule, lease, hook, auto_reviewer, human(principal), human(reviewer under ApproverGrant)}`, evaluated in that order; each stage returns `resolve(allow | deny) | pass`; stages other than a human may only *narrow* (a hook or auto-reviewer `allow` is admissible only where the never-auto set does not apply and only as an *input* to a `policy_rule` — see below); a stage failure (error, timeout, budget exhausted) is `pass` for `read_only/reversible` and `deny` otherwise (fail-closed above the sandbox line; OpenHands/Codex precedent); when the chain is exhausted with attendance ≠ interactive the result is Π-12. **Endorsement mechanics (answers Q10):** the only endorsement bases are ADR-0035's closed list. A human `allow` is an `approval` endorsement of `(effect_id, args_canonical_hash)`. An automatic `allow` is a `policy_rule` endorsement: the endorser is the kernel executing a sealed `HarnessRule{kind: auto_review}` whose predicate consumes the reviewer's `Validator{kind: judge}` verdict and the hook's decision as *inputs*; `basis_ref` names the rule, the verdict event and the hook event. Thus no `delegate`-origin object ever endorses, the rule's authority is `definition` (set at `seal` by the principal's configuration), and removing the rule removes automatic approval entirely. **Precedence declaration:** a sealed definition declares `approval_mode ∈ {manual, tiered, auto_review, pre_approved_only, unattended_deny, unattended_defer, bypass}` as a typed parameter (lowering to ACP session modes/config options, Codex `AskForApproval`, Claude Code modes, goose `GooseMode` — all surfaces); `bypass` is admissible only with `containment.sandboxed = true` recorded in the run manifest, prompts a one-time principal acknowledgement, and still never overrides the never-auto set.

**Automatic reviewer requirements (C1/Stage 4).** A `Validator{kind: judge}` component variant with: (a) its own `AgentProcess.native` whose `permissions` are the parent's intersected with read-only and `net_egress = ∅`, no extensions, no memory writes; (b) an evidence set restricted to items with `authority ≥ principal` plus the `EscalationInput` and a kernel-rendered proposal (tool *results* and other `external`/`unverified` content are stripped; Codex/Claude Code precedent) — the ADR-0034 role-placement invariant applies to it as to any model call; (c) a closed verdict `{risk_level ∈ {low, medium, high, critical}, authorization ∈ {unknown, low, medium, high}, outcome ∈ {allow, deny}, rationale: Text}` recorded as `security.permission.reviewed` with `review_reason`; (d) `charged_to = subject` under `harness_overhead.verification` with its own `BudgetNode` (Codex `request_budget`); (e) an assumption-debt record (its thresholds are conditioned on the reviewer profile) and a Stage-4 calibration set (WS-G3, OQ-123) whose held-out FN/FP rates are class-scoped metrics; (f) verdict reuse keyed like a lease (§6.3) and invalidated on new `external` content, mode change, or rule change; (g) the repeated-denial safeguard `{max_consecutive_denies (default 3), max_total_denies (default 20)}` ⇒ `security.permission.escalated{to: human}` when attended, `lifecycle.run.suspended{reason: approval_escalation}` when `async`, and `deny`-and-continue when `unattended`.

### 6.3 Approval granularity ladder and the lease key (OQ-091)

- **G0 per-effect approval:** endorses exactly one `(effect_id, args_canonical_hash)`; mandatory for `irreversible`, for Π-7/Π-8 and for any effect in the never-auto set; recorded as `security.permission.decided{decider = human}` + `security.label.endorsed{basis: approval}`.
- **G1 per-pattern lease:** `ApprovalLease{lease_id: LeaseId, key, capability_ref, pattern: ActionPattern, scope ∈ {turn, run, session}, holder: AgentProcessRef, granted_by: ReviewerRef (human or policy_rule), origin_permission_id, expires_at?, expires_with: writer lease, max_uses?, uses, policy_fingerprint, risk_ceiling: effective_risk_class, revoked_at?}` with `key = H(ToolCapability semantic_id ∥ canonical(pattern) ∥ scope ∥ policy_fingerprint)`. `ActionPattern` is declared per capability by WS-E1 as a *projection* of the argument space (`SurfaceArgMap`-aligned, ADR-0022): exec → normalized command prefix (WS-E5's canonicalization, Codex `canonicalize_command_for_approval`); `fs_*` → path-prefix set under declared roots; `net_egress` → `(host, port, protocol)`; MCP/tool → `(server, tool, argument-shape hash)`; `spend` → `(payee, ceiling)`. Invariants: a lease never covers a higher `effective_risk_class` than the one approved (`risk_ceiling`); a lease hit is `decider = cache` with `origin_permission_id` for audit (ADR-0031); `policy_fingerprint` = `H(sealed definition version_id ∥ approval_mode ∥ Π narrowing leaves)` so any policy or mode change invalidates every lease (Codex/Claude Code precedent); `scope = external` leases are re-validated when `context_label.authority` drops below the value at grant; lease grant, use, expiry and revocation are `security.permission.lease.{granted, used, expired, revoked}` ledger events; leases for `irreversible` require a human `allow_lease` option, `max_uses` set, and `scope ≤ run`. Sub-agents receive leases only by attenuated `delegated-to` copy (`risk_ceiling` and pattern ⊆ parent's) and never mint leases for the parent.
- **G2 per-class grant:** a `Permission` entity in the sealed definition (`authority = definition`), not an approval. Mid-run widening is Π-8. This is the "install-time" mechanism of Felt et al. — retained for configuration, disfavoured as a consent surface (S-329: 17%/3%).

### 6.4 Approval request/response contract (sync, async, ABI-facing)

Operations (language-neutral; implementable out-of-process, T-LCD-12):
- `decide(EscalationInput) → EscalationDecision{effective_risk_class, verdict ∈ {allow, ask, deny}, target?: ReviewerRef, reason: ReasonCode, cache_key?, lease_hit?}` — pure over records; failure modes `PolicyUndefined` (Π has no row: impossible by construction, test-guarded), `AttendanceUnknown`.
- `request_approval(ApprovalRequest) → permission_id` where `ApprovalRequest{permission_id (allocated), effect_id, subject: ApprovalSubject{capability_ref, effect_class, effective_risk_class, args_canonical_hash, preview: {command? | diff_ref? | host? | payee?} as kernel-rendered records}, why: Explanation (§6.5), options: [ApprovalOption{option_id, kind ∈ {allow_once, allow_lease{pattern, scope, max_uses?, expires_at?}, deny_once, deny_lease{pattern, scope}, modify (C2), abort_run, escalate{to}}, narrowing?}], requested_of: ReviewerRef, mode ∈ {sync, async}, deadline?, batch_id?, retry_of?, provenance}`. Emits durable `security.permission.pending{permission_id, effect_id, requested_of, deadline?, batch_id?}` and ephemeral `security.permission.requested{rendering}` (the split resolves the AC-B1-12 tension: the *fact* that a decision is owed is durable; the prompt rendering is not — CF-152).
- `respond(permission_id, ApprovalResponse) → ok | AlreadyDecided | Unknown | Expired` where `ApprovalResponse{permission_id, decision ∈ {allow, deny, abort, cancelled, timed_out}, option_id?, lease?: ApprovalLease, decider: {kind ∈ {policy, cache, hook, auto_reviewer, human}, ref, authority: AuthorityClass, grant_ref?: ApproverGrantRef}, rationale?: Text, decided_at, latency_ms, responder_provenance}`. Invariants: exactly one decision per `permission_id` (idempotent re-delivery returns the recorded decision — Inspect single-driver, ACP stale-card rationale); the decision precedes `action.effect.prepared` (ADR-0030 write-ahead; `authorized | refused` is the effect's next phase); `timed_out`, `cancelled` and `abort` are refusals (`action.effect.refused{reason}`) — never `unknown` (WS-B2 `ApprovalTimedOut`); a human `allow` by a responder whose authority < `principal` and without an `ApproverGrant` is `IllegitimateEndorsement`; every response is `security.permission.decided{…, effect_id, effective_risk_class, decider, lease_id?, latency_ms, reason}` (ADR-0031/CF-064 payload) and, for `allow`, `security.label.endorsed{basis ∈ {approval, policy_rule}}`.
- `withdraw(permission_id, reason)` — on turn cancellation, lease expiry of the writer, or effect abandonment; emits `security.permission.decided{decision: cancelled}`.
- **Asynchronous form (with WS-B3):** `mode = async` ⇒ after `security.permission.pending` the run emits `lifecycle.run.suspended{reason: awaiting_approval, permission_id}` and `control.wakeup.scheduled{trigger: permission_decided(permission_id)}`; the writer lease may lapse; on `respond`, `control.wakeup.fired` → `lifecycle.run.resumed{recovery_decision{last_durable_phase: intended, action: continue}}` and the effect proceeds to `authorized/refused` under a fresh `lease_generation`. Nothing after `intended` exists to replay (R5). `deadline` is optional; when absent the pending record is bounded by `time.human_wait_ms` budget only. The out-of-band notification (Inspect `notify`, OpenHands REST, Cursor PR) is a surface concern (K1/K2/L8) and is *not* the source of truth — the ledger record is.
- **Batching:** `batch_id` groups pending requests that share `effective_risk_class`, `requested_of` and `scope`; a surface may render one prompt; the response carries one `ApprovalResponse` per `permission_id`; `irreversible` requests are never batched; a batch response of `allow` for all items is recorded as N endorsements, never one (ATR/goose/OpenHands).
- **Delegation of approval authority:** `ApproverGrant{grant_id, approver: PrincipalRef, delegated_by: human principal, may_decide: {domains ⊆ EffectDomain, max_risk_class}, may_lease: bool, may_escalate_to: ReviewerRef?, expires_at, attestation?}` — recorded as `security.permission.granted{kind: approver_grant}`; a reviewer acting under a grant endorses with basis `approval` and the grant as `basis_ref`; grants attenuate (a delegated reviewer cannot re-delegate wider); this is the only route by which a human other than the run's principal decides, and the hook for WS-L8's rotations/on-call. Sub-agent processes never hold approver grants (ADR-0035: delegate never endorses); a child's `ask` bubbles to the root run's chain with `delegation_depth` in the input (Claude Code's three-point subagent review is the precedent for adding `spawn_process` and return-time review rows to Π).

**ABI-facing forms (lowering, ADR-0021 pattern; T-LCD-11).** ACP v2 `session/request_permission`: `title ← kernel rendering of (capability surface name, pattern)`, `description ← Explanation.summary`, `subject ← {tool_call | command{command, cwd}}` or `other` for non-tool effects (e.g. `permission_request`, `spend`), `options ← allow_once/allow_lease(scope=session)/deny_once/deny_lease` as `allow_once | allow_always | reject_once | reject_always`, `_meta.hh ← {permission_id, effect_id, effective_risk_class, reason_code, pattern, batch_id}`; `outcome.cancelled → decision cancelled`. Declared loss (`hint_only`/`no_slot`): narrowed lease patterns and `max_uses` (ACP `allow_always` is session-wide by name), `modify`, `escalate{to}`, `abort_run` (no option kind), `deadline`; the loss report is part of the bundle. MCP: an incoming `elicitation/create` from a tool server is a `message_human` effect through Π (Codex `mcp_elicitations` precedent; never auto-accepted; `decline` is the unattended default); an outgoing tool call that returns `input_required{requestState}` is the target-side form of `defer` — the opaque state is stored on the `Effect` record (`ext`) and the retry is the same `effect_id`. Hooks: the WS-H5-attested hook interface receives the `EscalationInput` projection and returns `{allow, deny, ask, defer}` (claude-agent-sdk sum) — `allow` is an input to `policy_rule`, `defer` sets `mode = async`. Hosted participants (J6): the WS-L1 §6.5 verb `request_permission` carries `ApprovalRequest` as the payload; a hosted participant declares `permission-elicitation ∈ {declared, probed, unknown}` (T-LCD-07); approval metrics on participants without it render `n/a{reason: observability}` (T-LCD-15). Codex app-server (`protocol/v2/permissions.rs`) and Omnigent presets are the evidence that only *modes* and *mirrored prompts* are portable to black boxes.

### 6.6 Approvals as resource accounting (OQ-096 answer)

- `approvals.requested` increments on every `security.permission.pending` whose `requested_of` is a **human** (`principal` or grant holder) and is *not* satisfied by a lease or batch coalescing; `approvals.granted` increments on each human `allow` (once or lease); automatic-reviewer invocations are `evaluator_calls` plus their `tokens.*` (`charged_to = subject`, `harness_overhead.verification`), *not* approvals; lease hits and `policy_rule` allows are neither (they cost no attention). Rationale: the scarce resource is human attention, which a `deny` consumes exactly as an `allow` does — so the **budget dimension is `approvals.requested`**; `approvals.granted` stays a counter for the autonomy dimension and for `approval_rate`. `time.human_wait_ms` accrues from `pending` to `decided` for human targets and is excluded from `time.working_ms` (ADR-0039); MatchSpec on `approvals.requested` is admissible cross-model (ADR-0041 M1).
- Exhaustion (ADR-0040): the next human-targeted `ask` converts to `deny` with `reason = approvals_exhausted`, recorded as `control.budget.consumed{dimension: approvals.requested, …}` + `security.permission.decided{decider: policy, reason: approvals_exhausted}`; the model's refusal observation carries the same reason (so it does not retry blindly); a lease may still satisfy the request (leases were paid for). **Fatigue safeguard around the conversion:** (i) the conversion is surfaced to the principal as `security.permission.escalated{to: principal, reason: approvals_exhausted}` in attended modes — a *single* summary prompt offering `amend(budget)` (ledgered, ADR-0040 §6) or `stop`; (ii) in unattended modes the run continues under Π-12 `deny` and, if the denied effect blocks completion (WS-G2 `abandoned`/blocking rule), ends with `stop_reason = approvals_exhausted`; (iii) the conversion never widens and never auto-leases; (iv) `max_consecutive_denies` applies to converted denials too, so an exhausted budget cannot produce a silent denial loop.
- The Lab treats `approval_mode`, `UnattendedPolicy`, the reviewer variant and the approvals budget as `configuration-level` factors; every comparison across them is a `ComparisonReport` under a `MatchSpec` that includes `approvals.requested` (T-LCD-14).

### 6.7 Data-model sketch (runtime records; HIR touch-points only where ratified)

`PermissionRequest` (runtime record keyed by `PermissionId`; fields of `ApprovalRequest` + `state ∈ {pending, decided, withdrawn}` + `decision?`), `ApprovalLease` (§6.3), `ApproverGrant` (§6.4), `EscalationInput`/`EscalationDecision` (§6.1/6.2), `Explanation` (§6.5), `ReviewVerdict{permission_id, reviewer_ref, risk_level, authorization, outcome, rationale: Text, review_reason, profile_ref, cost_ref}`. HIR/1 touch-points: `Permission` (holder, grants, `authorizes` edge to the effect on `allow`), `Effect.permission_id` + `decider` (ADR-0031 field list), `HarnessRule{kind: auto_review}` (conditioned, with debt record), `Validator{kind: judge}` for the reviewer, `Budget.dimensions ∋ approvals.requested`, the `approval_mode` and `UnattendedPolicy` parameters of the Harness Definition (`MUST-data`), and `ControlBoundary.assignments[authorize | escalate] ∈ {code, model, human}` — which is how the Lab records *who* owned the decision point (WS-A2 β). New ledger classes proposed to WS-B1 (CF-152/02): `security.permission.pending` (durable), `security.permission.reviewed`, `security.permission.escalated`, `security.permission.lease.{granted, used, expired, revoked}`; extend `security.permission.decided.decider` with `cache` and `auto_reviewer`; `lifecycle.run.suspended{reason: awaiting_approval | approval_escalation}`.

### 6.8 Acceptance-criteria sketch

- **AC-H7-01 Decision precedes preparation.** No `action.effect.prepared` exists without a preceding `security.permission.decided{decision: allow}` or a Π `allow` for the same `effect_id`; property test over the ledger.
- **AC-H7-02 Monotone chain.** For every `ask`, the sequence of stage outputs never contains `allow` after a `deny`, and no non-human stage produces `allow` for an effect in the never-auto set (static + trace predicate).
- **AC-H7-03 Endorser legitimacy.** Every `security.label.endorsed{basis: approval}` has an endorser with `origin = human(principal | reviewer-with-grant)`; every `{basis: policy_rule}` names a sealed `HarnessRule{kind: auto_review}` and a `ReviewVerdict`; a synthetic `delegate` endorser is rejected with `IllegitimateEndorsement`.
- **AC-H7-04 Lease key.** Two effects with the same `(ToolCapability, pattern, scope, fingerprint)` share a lease; changing `approval_mode` or any Π leaf invalidates it (`lease.revoked{reason: fingerprint}`); an `irreversible` effect never hits a lease granted by a non-human; `risk_ceiling` is enforced.
- **AC-H7-05 Unattended resolution.** With `attendance = unattended`, every `ask` resolves within one decision step to `deny`, `defer` or `auto_review`; no run blocks on a human; `defer` produces exactly one `lifecycle.run.suspended` and resumes without re-emitting any effect phase after `intended`.
- **AC-H7-06 Timeout/cancel are refusals.** `deadline` expiry and turn cancellation yield `action.effect.refused`, never `unknown`; ACP `cancelled` maps to the same.
- **AC-H7-07 Accounting.** `approvals.requested` equals the count of human-targeted `pending` events minus coalesced/lease-satisfied ones; exhaustion converts to `deny` with the reason visible in the model's observation and to the principal; no `allow` follows exhaustion without a ledgered `amend`.
- **AC-H7-08 Reviewer attenuation.** The auto-reviewer process's permissions ⊆ parent ∩ read-only, `net_egress = ∅`; its evidence set contains no item with `authority < principal` other than the proposal; its failure yields `deny` for classes above `reversible`.
- **AC-H7-09 Repeated-denial fallback.** `max_consecutive_denies`/`max_total_denies` produce escalation (attended) or a bounded stop (unattended); a denial loop is impossible by trace predicate.
- **AC-H7-10 Lowering round-trip.** `ApprovalRequest → ACP request_permission → lift` recovers `permission_id, effect_id, effective_risk_class, reason_code` and declares loss for `modify/escalate/abort_run/max_uses/deadline` (T-LCD-11).
- **AC-H7-11 Explanation typing.** `Explanation` contains no free text except `model_justification: Text{authority = delegate}`; surfaces render the two with distinct provenance (T-LCD-02).
- **AC-H7-12 Metrics.** `approval_rate{class}`, `denial_rate{class}`, `human_wait_ms`, `auto_review_fn_rate` (on the calibration set) are `MetricDeclaration`s with `applies_to_classes` and `requires_observability ∋ events`; hosted participants without `permission-elicitation` render `n/a` (T-LCD-15).
