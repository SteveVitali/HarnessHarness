## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream decides **what changes when the unit of work a human hands to HarnessHarness is no longer a chat turn but a ticket, a PR, a webhook, a schedule or a message — and what the human organization gets back: who owns the work, who is accountable for its external effects, how it escalates when the agent cannot proceed, and how a fleet of such work is observed and paid for.** Concretely it fixes: (i) the **work-item and trigger model** — a durable organizational object that binds a HIR `Goal` (ADR-0016) to an external source of intent, an accountable human owner, a state machine with declared human-gate states, and the chain of run activations that execute it (ADR-0131); (ii) the **control-plane reconciliation contract** — how a fleet reconciler reads desired state from sources, claims work, opens and stops activations, distinguishes *blocked-on-human* from *stalled*, and re-derives every timer from the ledger; (iii) the **ownership, accountability and escalation model** — single accountable owner with explicit handoff, an owner-reference graph for follow-up work, an escalation policy with an ordered target chain, and the accountability record every work item can produce; (iv) **fleet-level observability and cost** as projections of the ledger and of goal-scoped budget nodes; (v) acceptance criteria, build-stage placement, and an explicit **C4-vs-deferred** partition with rationale (ADR-L8-3).

The binding position, taken up front: **the organizational layer adds no runtime, no second store and no new authority path.** It is (a) one new run kind (`fleet`) whose ledger holds work-item records and reconciler decisions, (b) records and policies that are MUST-data of a Harness Definition, and (c) projections. Every mechanism it needs already exists: goals, activations, subscriptions, occurrences, inbox runs and ingress adapters (ADR-0131); leases and phase-resolved restore (ADR-0130); attendance, `UnattendedPolicy`, `ApproverGrant`, batching and `defer` (ADR-0069…0071); the audit-grade `lifecycle.escalation.raised/resolved` class and audit obligations (ADR-0066); cross-run anchors (ADR-0067); goal-scoped budget nodes and `charged_to` (ADR-0039/0040); the caller model for service bindings (ADR-0174); the "an X is a run" pattern with claim leases and views (ADR-0155); attenuating delegation (ADR-0053); `hh-embed/1` as the only way to drive a run (ADR-0176).

It explicitly leaves to neighbours: sub-agent spawning *inside* an activation, topology and merge (**WS-F3**, concurrent — L8 opens and stops *activations*; it never spawns children; §8 pre-registers the naming seam); dispatch prioritization by expected value (**WS-F4** — L8's scheduler order is data with a declared default and no value model); shared-state conflicts between concurrently active work items on one repository (**WS-F5** — L8 gives each work item its own derived workspace, ADR-0138, and declares only the *claim* invariant); the wakeup/occurrence/claim mechanics and inbox runs (**WS-B3** — adopted verbatim; L8 answers OQ-313 as consumer); the approval component, Π, leases and grants (**WS-H7/H1** — adopted; L8 supplies the escalation *routing* and the organizational use of `ApproverGrant`); the audit catalogue (**WS-H6** — adopted; L8 adds obligations, no classes beyond `control.work_item.*`); surfaces that render fleets and approval inboxes (**WS-K1/K2** — L8 fixes the `FleetView` record they project); protocol lowering of work-item state to tracker/MCP-task carriers (**WS-E4/K3**); human directories, on-call rotations, multi-tenant identity and any general workflow engine (**deferred**, ADR-L8-3; non-goals N1/N5).

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence (a failure mode exists / an architecture is implementable)

**F1 — The organizational unit is a *work item* with a desired state held outside the runtime, and the runtime's job is level-triggered reconciliation against it.** Symphony's entire orchestrator is a controller loop: every tick reconciles running and blocked issues against the tracker *before* dispatching (`orchestrator.ex` L256–260; SPEC §7.4 "Reconciliation runs before dispatch on every tick"), re-validates the issue by id immediately before launch (L907–952), stops a run when the tracker moves the issue terminal or non-active (§8.5 Part B; §14.4 "terminal state -> running session is stopped"), and treats an omitted id as "no longer visible rather than inventing a synthetic state" (§11.1). Cursor's agents "subscribe to an event source … and wake when something happens" and self-subscribe to the PRs they create (S-054). Kubernetes names the shape: controllers act on desired vs observed state and a restarted controller re-examines current state (S-426). The failure mode the pattern exists for is visible in Symphony §14.3: with the tracker as the only durable state, "No retry timers are restored … No running sessions are assumed recoverable" — already rejected as Core behaviour by ADR-0130 D5 and ADR-0131. **Implication (§6.1):** a `WorkItem` carries two halves the way a controller object does — `spec` (desired state, read from the source, `authority = external` unless owner-attested) and `status` (observed state, a projection of the ledger). The tracker is never "the control plane" in HarnessHarness; it is a *source of intent and a mirror*, and the reconciler is a run whose decisions are ledgered. Nothing in ADR-0026 is weakened: what the ledger is authoritative *for* is what the runtime did and decided; what the source is authoritative *for* is what the organization wants, and the reconciler records the observed value of that want at each tick.

**F2 — "Blocked on a human" is a distinct state from "stalled", and the distinction is what keeps unattended fleets from thrashing.** Symphony moves a worker that exited while `:turn_input_required`/`:approval_required`/MCP elicitation was pending into a `blocked` map: removed from `running`, kept in `claimed`, excluded from stall restarts, and released only when the tracker moves the issue (L659–666, L747–780, L456–476). Its organizational prompt makes the same distinction at the ticket level: `Human Review` and `Merging` are states in which "waiting on human approval" is the *correct* behaviour, while "Only stop early for a true external blocker (missing required tools, auth, permissions, or secrets)" defines the abnormal block (`WORKFLOW.md`). ADR-0131 D4 already exempts `suspended` runs from liveness takeover and D5 defaults scheduled/goal runs to `unattended`; ADR-0069 Π-12 resolves `ask` under `unattended` to `deny | defer | auto_review`. **Implication (§6.2/§6.3):** the work-item state map declares **human-gate states** (states whose *exit* is a human act) separately from **blocked** (an abnormal condition with a typed reason and an escalation), and stall detection is inactivity-based over `active` activations only. The fleet default for `ask` is `defer` with an escalation chain (ADR-0069 Π-12 `UnattendedPolicy = defer`), because a fleet always has a notification sink by construction (the source); the headless-CLI default `unattended_deny` (ADR-0168) is the right default when there is no one to defer to.

**F3 — Tracker mutations are external effects and must be executed host-side under mediated credentials; the audited pattern already has this shape.** Symphony's "important boundary": "Ticket writes … are typically performed by the coding agent through provider-native tools executed by Symphony with the configured tracker credential"; "the child receives tool results, not a raw token"; adapters "MUST declare authentication-related environment names for removal from local and remote child environments"; each tool must document "whether a tool can mutate tracker state" (SPEC §1, §10.5, §11.5). This is ADR-0058's broker (proxy-injection primary), ADR-0087's capability record with declared effect class, and ADR-0031's risk class (`scope = external`, `compensable` for a state transition or comment, `irreversible` for a merge). Symphony's non-standardization rule — "Do not add generic comment/state/attachment CRUD merely to make providers look alike; those operations lose useful provider semantics" (§11) — is T-LCD-07/-11 stated by a production team: **declare capabilities per adapter, never intersect.** **Implication (§6.2):** the reconciler *reads* through a declared adapter (two read operations, Symphony's minimum); the *agent* writes through ordinary `ToolCapability` entities lifted from the adapter with `source_kind` and `unverified` provenance (ADR-0088), whose effects pass Π like any other external effect. The state map's human-gate rule is then enforceable at Π: a transition that *enters* a human-gate state is an ordinary external effect (allow under a lease); a transition that *skips* a declared human gate is `deny` by a Π narrowing leaf keyed on the capability's `ActionPattern` (ADR-0071).

**F4 — Accountability in production is a single named human owner with explicit, acknowledged handoff — and the current agent systems do not record it.** SRE incident management fixes one incident commander who "holds the high-level state", assigns roles, and must "not leave the call until receiving firm acknowledgment of handoff", with a "living incident document" as the authoritative record (S-WS-L8-03). Symphony's issue carries `assignee_id` and derives `dispatchable` from it (an unrouted issue is not dispatched — `tracker/issue.ex` `routable?`), and its land skill escalates ambiguity by "assign PR to current GH user, mention them, wait" — ownership *is* the routing key, but nothing records who was accountable for which effect. Cursor's write-up "does not contain explicit statements regarding ownership accountability" (S-054, fetched). ADR-0033 mints `principal` "only for the run's human principal"; ADR-0035 says approval endorses one `effect_id`; ADR-0070 D6 gives `ApproverGrant` as "the only route by which a human other than the run's principal decides" and names it "the hook for WS-L8's rotations/on-call"; ADR-0174 makes a `service` binding a `delegate` caller in `unattended` mode. **Implication (§6.3):** every work item has exactly one `owner: PrincipalRef` (a human), the owner *is* the `principal` of every activation of that work item, ownership changes only by a ledgered handoff that the new owner acknowledges, and the accountability record of a work item is `audit_view` restricted to the item's activations plus the owner/grant/escalation events — a projection, not a store. Bainbridge's irony (S-WS-L8-02) is the constraint on the shape: the owner who is escalated to has, by design, not been watching; therefore an escalation must carry the evidence (effect ids, budget state, the last `control.decision`), never a bare notification.

**F5 — Escalation has a record (ratified) but no route (unratified), and the route is a policy over an ordered target chain with per-stage deadlines.** ADR-0066 D5 obligates `lifecycle.escalation.raised` for every `abandoned` effect, every `unknown` open at `finished`, and every hard `control.budget.exceeded`; ADR-0071 D4/D5 escalate on approvals exhaustion and repeated denials; ADR-0132 stops on `MaxHealsExceeded`; Symphony blocks on input required and on "true external blockers". SRE's declaration criteria are *route* criteria ("Do you need to involve a second team?"), and its roles are an ordered set of responsibilities, not a broadcast. ADR-0069 D4 already fixes an ordered, narrowing-only reviewer chain for `ask`; ADR-0071 D6 forbids model-authored text from steering a human decision. **Implication (§6.3):** `EscalationPolicy` is an ordered list of `EscalationTarget{ref ∈ {owner, ApproverGrant, PrincipalRef, sink}, after_ms, on ⊆ EscalationCause}` — a PagerDuty-shaped chain expressed as data — evaluated by the reconciler; each notification is a `message_human` effect through Π (audited, budgeted); resolution is a ledgered act by a `principal`-class or grant-holding human (`acknowledged | amended | reassigned | cancelled | expired`); an `external`-authority reply (a tracker comment, a chat message) can *wake* the item and never resolves it (ADR-0131 D2/ADR-0035).

**F6 — Fleet cost and fleet risk aggregate across principals, and the per-run contracts already make cost aggregation a view while risk aggregation is genuinely open.** Symphony accumulates "aggregate totals in orchestrator state" from absolute thread totals and warns against double-counting deltas (§13.5) — a problem ADR-0039 ("nothing unaccounted"; totals are views) has already dissolved. ADR-0040 gives `BudgetNode{scope: Goal}` with slice/pool containment and ADR-0131 D5 carries the goal node across activations, so per-item, per-owner and per-source totals are `project` views. The *risk* side is different: the Irreversibility Budget preprint identifies a failure class per-effect gates cannot see — "a fleet of individually authorized agents can overdraw its principal's risk under a shared trigger while every local gate stays correct" (S-WS-L8-05, `provisional`) — and states that pricing is unsolved. **Implication (§6.4/§6.6):** fleet observability is a projection (`FleetView`), fleet cost is the L2 view keyed by owner/source/state, and the only fleet-level *risk* mechanism admitted now is a **counter, not a price**: `effects.external_irreversible` per owner pool as a kernel counter dimension with a hard ceiling (root-first exhaustion, ADR-0040) — the irreversibility budget's admission-control *shape* with a unit price of 1, which needs no pricing model. Value-at-risk pricing is deferred (ADR-L8-3) with the preprint as the pointer.

**F7 — Work items spawn work items, and the organizational prompt already encodes an ownership graph with blocking semantics.** Symphony's prompt: "file a separate Linear issue instead of expanding scope … link the current issue as `related`, and use `blockedBy` when the follow-up depends on the current issue"; the normalized issue carries `blocked_by[]` as "best-effort provider metadata" the scheduler does not interpret (§4.1.1; §11.3 "adapters MUST NOT invent blocker semantics"). Kubernetes' owner references give the safe shape: an owner reference per dependent, `blockOwnerDeletion`, foreground cascading deletion (dependents first), orphaning as an explicit policy, cross-namespace references "disallowed by design", and unresolvable owners treated as absent (S-WS-L8-04). **Implication (§6.1):** child work items carry `owner_ref{parent_work_item, block_parent_completion}`; the graph is acyclic within a fleet and never crosses fleets; cancelling a parent cascades `foreground` by default (children first) or `orphan` by declared policy; a parent cannot enter a terminal state while a blocking child is open — enforced by the reconciler, not inferred from provider blocker metadata.

**F8 — The layer's autonomy is a per-function level, not a dial, and that is what makes it a Lab factor.** Parasuraman–Sheridan–Wickens separate automation of information acquisition, analysis, decision selection and action implementation, each at its own level, and warn that automation "imposes new coordination demands" (S-WS-L8-01). Symphony's status map is exactly such a per-function allocation: implementation is automated end-to-end, review is human (`Human Review`), the merge decision is human (`Merging` = "approved by human") while the merge *act* is automated (the land skill). ADR-0016's `ControlBoundary.assignments[authorize | escalate]` and ADR-0069 D6 already record who owned each decision point for the Lab. **Implication (§6.5):** the state map, the escalation policy and the unattended policy are `configuration-level` factors; a fleet-policy comparison is an `ExperimentSpec` with a `MatchSpec` over `approvals.requested`, `time.human_wait_ms` and `spend` (T-LCD-14), and the headline is deterministic (`time_to_handoff`, `escalations_per_item`, `external_effects_per_item`), never a judged "autonomy score".

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

**Against a fleet run and ledgered work items at all (R1).** Symphony runs production fleets with *no* durable orchestrator state — "tracker-driven and filesystem-driven (without a durable orchestrator DB)" (§7.4) — and it is simpler, and the Cursor system apparently does the same on its engine. A ledgered fleet is more machinery. *Why it does not overturn:* Symphony's own §14.3 lists what its choice loses (timers, running sessions, blocked state) and ADR-0130/0131 already rejected those losses; without a fleet ledger there is no record of *why* an item was dispatched, stopped or escalated, which R-2.8.6 (deep audit) forbids; and the pattern's cost is bounded — the fleet run is the ADR-0155 shape already built for experiments, reused with a different event family. The reconciliation *pattern* is adopted; the "no durable state" *choice* is not (same disposition as WS-B3 CF-283).

**Against treating the source as desired state (R2).** Two sources of truth invite the classic split-brain: a human moves the ticket to `Done` while the agent is mid-merge; the ledger says `active`, the source says terminal. *Accepted, and it is why the halves are typed:* the reconciler records the observed source value at each tick (`control.work_item.observed{spec_hash}`), a terminal source state stops the activation through the ordinary `cancel` path (Symphony §14.4 behaviour) and never deletes anything, and a conflict between a human-gate transition the agent performed and a contradicting source move is `blocked{source_conflict}` escalated to the owner — surfaced, never auto-resolved. The source is authoritative only for *intent*; the ledger for *fact*.

**Against a single human owner (R3).** Teams own things; "one owner" is a fiction in many organizations, and pinning `principal` to one person makes team review awkward; Symphony uses `assignee_id` only for routing. *Why it does not overturn:* SRE practice converged on one accountable commander precisely because shared ownership means nobody is reachable (S-WS-L8-03); ADR-0033 mints `principal` for exactly one human per run; team participation is expressed through `ApproverGrant`s issued by the owner (ADR-0070 D6) and through ownership handoff, both ledgered. What is *not* solved — resolving "the on-call for team X" to a person — is deferred to an external directory (ADR-L8-3), and the deferral is the honest reading of R3.

**Against enforcing human-gate states at Π (R4).** A state map keyed on tracker state names is brittle (renames, provider drift) and pushes organizational policy into the security kernel. *Why it does not overturn:* the map is MUST-data in the sealed definition (versioned with the code, Symphony's `WORKFLOW.md` insight), states are compared normalized (Symphony §4.2), a state not in the map is `unknown` ⇒ the transition is `ask` (ADR-0031 `unknown ⇒ irreversible ∧ external` precedence), and the kernel sees only an `ActionPattern` projection of a lifted capability (ADR-0071) — Π gains no organizational vocabulary. The alternative (trusting the prompt to "move status only when the matching quality bar is met") is exactly the model-enforced invariant doc 2 §4 says to put in deterministic software.

**Against `defer` + escalation chain as the fleet default (R5).** ADR-0168 chose `unattended_deny` for headless runs; LangGraph-style deferral re-executes (via H7 R5); a deferred item can sit for days consuming `env.reserved_ms`. *Why it does not overturn:* the fleet, unlike a headless CLI, always has a reachable sink (the source itself) so `defer` has somewhere to go; ADR-0070 D4's `defer` parks the *intent* before `prepared` so nothing re-executes; and the chain's last stage is a bounded stop (`escalation_unresolved`) with the environment released under `HealingPolicy` — the cost is capped by the item's budget node, not by patience. It remains a *default*, varied as a Lab factor.

**Against a fleet-level irreversibility counter now (R6).** The only evidence that fleet-level overdraw matters is a two-week-old preprint (`provisional`), its pricing problem is open, and a unit-priced counter may block benign fleets (every PR merge is `irreversible`). *Accepted, which is why the counter is optional data:* it is a registered `ext.*`-style dimension with a hard ceiling on the owner's pool that a definition may leave unset; the mechanism (root-first exhaustion, ADR-0040) is ratified and costs nothing to expose; pricing is deferred with a named removal test (§6.6). No C4 claim depends on the preprint.

**Against the layer being a Lab object (R7).** Comparing escalation policies requires simulated humans, and user simulators are instrument models (ADR-0047) whose fidelity to real reviewers is unknown; a "fleet benchmark" risks the anecdotal comparisons doc 2 §8 warns about. *Why it does not overturn:* the admitted headline metrics are deterministic ledger counts (time to handoff, escalations per item, external effects, human wait), the human side is stratified as `attendance` and `responder` factors, and any judged component is instrument-charged and labelled — the same discipline as `lab/control-strategy-family-v1` (ADR-0156). What is *not* claimed is that a fleet policy "beats" another without a `MatchSpec` over approvals and spend.

**Negative results.** (N1) No audited system records an accountability record per work item or per owner (Symphony: none; Cursor S-054: none stated). (N2) No audited system budgets `message_human`/escalations; Symphony's approval posture is "implementation-defined" and its example is auto-approve-everything with `approval_policy: never` (`WORKFLOW.md` front matter). (N3) No audited system distinguishes source *intent* from ledger *fact* as typed halves; Symphony overwrites its in-memory issue snapshot on refresh. (N4) No audited system has a cascade policy for follow-up work; Symphony delegates it to provider `blockedBy` metadata it explicitly refuses to interpret. (N5) The OpenAI launch post (S-048) could not be opened (403); nothing here depends on it beyond what the repository states.

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

### 6.1 Work-item and trigger model (data model)

**Position.** A **work item** is the durable organizational object; a `Goal` (ADR-0016) is its semantic core inside the sealed definition; a **fleet** is a run (`run_kind = fleet`, dialect bump of ADR-0183 §C.1 — CF-WS-L8-01) whose ledger holds the work-item records and every reconciler decision; activations are ordinary `agent` runs chained by `continued_from` (ADR-0131 D5) and linked to the fleet run through `causes[]` (no new field). No HIR entity changes: `Goal` keeps `{statement, success_criteria, budget, origin, parent}`; source binding, ownership and state live on the runtime record (T-LCD-06 style; CF-WS-L8-03 notes the `origin` vocabulary).

```
WorkItem{
  work_item_id: allocated id (ADR-0027 kind; time-ordered, opaque),
  fleet_run: RunId,
  goal_ref: VersionedRef<Goal>,                 -- sealed definition entity
  definition_ref: VersionedRef<Assembly>,        -- which Harness Definition executes it
  owner: PrincipalRef,                           -- exactly one human; the principal of every activation
  owner_ref?: {parent: work_item_id, block_parent_completion: bool},   -- ownership graph (child → parent)
  sources: [WorkSourceBinding],                  -- ≥ 1; the first is the origin
  spec: WorkSpec{                                -- DESIRED (observed from sources; authority ≤ external unless owner-attested)
    title: Text, body: Text, priority?: int, labels: [Text], source_state: Text,
    routable: bool, blocked_by: [work_item_id | SourceRef], spec_hash, observed_at, provenance },
  status: WorkStatus{                            -- OBSERVED (projection of the ledger)
    state ∈ WorkItemState, activation?: RunId, activation_no, claim?: LeaseRef,
    blocked?: {reason: BlockReason, since, escalation?: EventRef},
    handoff?: {state, since, awaiting: PrincipalRef | ApproverGrantRef},
    budget: {budget_id (ADR-0040 scope: Goal), consumed, reserved, remaining},
    last_activity, external_effects: [effect_id], open_children: [work_item_id] },
  policy: { state_map_ref, escalation_policy_ref, attendance ∈ {async, unattended}, scheduling{priority_rank, max_activations, max_concurrent_children} },
  provenance }
```

`WorkItemState` (closed, kernel-owned; the *source* state names are surfaces mapped by the state map): `discovered → claimed → active ⇄ suspended → handoff{state} → active | done | cancelled | blocked{reason} → active | cancelled`. `BlockReason ∈ {missing_access, approval_pending_expired, approvals_exhausted, budget_exhausted, environment_lost, source_conflict, ambiguity, max_activations, escalation_unresolved, child_blocking}`.

`WorkSourceBinding{source_ref: VersionedRef<WorkSourceAdapter>, native_id: Text, native_ref?: ext, kind ∈ {tracker_item, pull_request, message_thread, schedule, manual, webhook}, role ∈ {origin, mirror, subscription}, subscription_id?}` — a work item may gain bindings during its life (the PR the agent opens becomes a `subscription` binding; Cursor S-054 self-subscription).

`WorkItemStateMap` (MUST-data of the Harness Definition; ADR-0024 ladder): rows `{source_state: Text (normalized), kernel_state: WorkItemState | handoff, human_gate: bool, entry_allowed_by ∈ {agent, human, either}, exit_allowed_by ∈ {agent, human, either}, terminal: bool}`. A source state absent from the map is `unknown` ⇒ transitions into it are `ask`. Symphony's example map lowers exactly: `Todo → claimed (entry: human)`, `In Progress → active (entry: agent)`, `Human Review → handoff (human_gate; exit: human)`, `Merging → active (entry: human)`, `Rework → active (entry: human)`, `Done/Closed/… → done (terminal; entry: agent ∨ human)`.

**Trigger model.** L8 adds no trigger kind: every trigger is an ADR-0131 `Trigger` (`schedule`, `external{source_ref, filter}`, `manual`, `timer`, `child_terminal`, `permission_decided`). What L8 adds is the **mapping** from an occurrence to a fleet action, declared per source adapter as data: `WorkTriggerRule{on: TriggerFilter, action ∈ {create_work_item{state_map, owner_rule}, wake{work_item}, reprioritize, cancel, annotate, bind_source}}`. `owner_rule ∈ {source_assignee (resolved through the adapter's principal mapping), fixed(PrincipalRef), unowned → blocked{missing_owner}}`; an item with no resolvable human owner is **never dispatched** (Symphony `routable?` generalized: no assignee ⇒ not dispatchable). Poll-based sources (Symphony's two read operations) produce occurrences too: the adapter's `observe(now)` writes `control.wakeup.occurred` with `occurrence_key = H(native_id ∥ source_state ∥ updated_at)` so re-polls dedupe (OQ-313 answer, §6.7); push sources use the source-declared delivery id when present, else `H(canonical(payload − volatile_fields))`, with a replay window `max(2 × poll_interval, 24 h)` bounded by the inbox run's retention.

**Ownership graph.** `owner_ref` forms a forest within one fleet run (cross-fleet references are refused — Kubernetes' cross-namespace rule); a child created by an agent (`file a follow-up issue`) is an external effect that, on `observed{applied}`, causes the reconciler to create the child work item with `owner = parent.owner` unless the source assigns otherwise; `CascadePolicy ∈ {foreground (default: children cancelled first), background, orphan}` on parent cancellation; a parent with an open `block_parent_completion` child cannot enter `done` (reconciler check, never provider `blocked_by` inference).

### 6.2 Control-plane reconciliation contract (operations, invariants, failure modes)

The fleet reconciler is a kernel-adjacent component driven through `hh-embed/1` (ADR-0176 Groups S/W/R; a fleet needing a new verb proposes it `experimental` via ADR-0178, never a private binding). Boundary placement (ADR-0050 §8 clause 4): source adapters and the reconciler policy are out of process; `open_run`, leases, appends and `authorize` are kernel-side; records cross whole (D2).

- `open_fleet(definition_ref, FleetPolicy) → RunId` — opens the fleet run (`run_kind = fleet`; manifest `{fleet_id, state_map_ref, escalation_policy_ref, scheduling_policy, source_bindings[], owner_directory?: none | ref}`); long fleets continue by `continued_from` (ADR-0131 D5, ADR-0155 D1 shape). Failures: `MissingBudget` (a fleet pool budget is mandatory; T-LCD-14), `StateMapInvalid{row}`, `UnownedSource`.
- `observe(source_ref, now) → [Occurrence]` (adapter; out of process) — the ADR-0131 `record_occurrence` writer for poll sources; two required read operations per adapter: `list(states[]) → [WorkSourceRecord]`, `get(native_ids[]) → [WorkSourceRecord]` (omission = "no longer visible", never a synthetic state — Symphony §11.1). Adapter capabilities are a tri-state declaration `{push_delivery_id, poll, native_write_tools, assignee_mapping, blocker_metadata}` probed at bind (T-LCD-07); `unknown` never coerced.
- `reconcile(fleet, now) → [FleetAction]` — pure over the fleet ledger + inbox runs + the activations' checkpoint views (ADR-0130 D1): (1) fold new occurrences into `spec` for every bound item (`control.work_item.observed{work_item_id, spec_hash, source_state, provenance}` only when changed); (2) for every `active` item compare `status` to `spec`: source terminal/non-active/unroutable ⇒ `stop{cancel}`; source moved into a human-gate state by the agent ⇒ `handoff`; source contradicts a ledgered handoff ⇒ `block{source_conflict}`; (3) stall sweep over `active` activations only: `now − last_activity > stall_ms` ⇒ `stop` + retry (ledger-derived timer, `control.retry.scheduled`); `suspended` runs are exempt (ADR-0131 D4); (4) blocked/handoff items: re-check spec; escalation deadlines from `EscalationPolicy` become `timer` subscriptions; (5) retries due (`due(now)`, ADR-0130 D5); (6) dispatch eligible items in `SchedulingPolicy` order while capacity remains. Every action is appended as `control.work_item.*` before it is executed (durable-before-visible).
- `claim(work_item_id, holder) → Lease{scope: resource(work_item_id)}` (ADR-0130 lease scope; ADR-0155 D2 precedent; no new lease kind). **RC-2:** at most one activation per work item at a time; a second claim is `WouldBlock`.
- `dispatch(work_item_id, lease) → RunId` — re-validates the item against the source by id (`get`) immediately before launch (Symphony L907–952); allocates the activation's root `BudgetNode` as a `slice` of the item's goal node (ADR-0040; `InsufficientBudget` refuses before provisioning); derives a workspace per item (ADR-0138 `derive`, preserved across activations under the item's environment lease); opens the activation through `open_session(new | resume)` with manifest facts `{goal_ref, activation_no, continued_from?, attendance, principal = owner, causes[] ∋ fleet dispatch event}`; renders `activation_no`/source context only through `HarnessRule`s whose delivery is ledgered (T-LCD-13; source text enters as `Text{authority: external}`, T-LCD-02).
- `settle(work_item_id, run_id) → WorkOutcome` on the activation's `lifecycle.run.finished`: `completed_activation ∧ source still active` ⇒ continuation retry after a short fixed delay (Symphony 1 s continuation; ADR-0131 `continue_goal`); `stop_reason ∈ {approvals_exhausted, budget_exhausted, environment_lost, escalation_unresolved}` ⇒ `block{reason}` + escalation; `suspended{awaiting_approval}` ⇒ `handoff{approval}` (the item is *not* blocked); abnormal exit ⇒ retry with capped exponential backoff keyed on `error_class` (ADR-0107 shape); `max_activations` reached ⇒ `block{max_activations}`.
- `handoff(work_item_id, state)` / `resume_from_handoff(work_item_id, by: PrincipalRef | grant)` — a handoff is entered by an agent's external effect (state transition) or by the reconciler; it is exited only by a human act observed from the source or through a surface (`respond_permission`, an owner `amend`, or a source transition attributed to a mapped human principal); an agent transition that would *skip* a `human_gate` row is denied at Π by a narrowing leaf on the lifted capability's `ActionPattern` (ADR-0071 D1).
- `transfer_owner(work_item_id, to, basis)` → `control.work_item.owner_changed{from, to, basis ∈ {approval, policy_rule}, acknowledged: false}`; the item is `blocked{missing_owner}` for dispatch until `control.work_item.owner_acknowledged` by `to` (SRE acknowledged handoff); activations already open keep their principal until their next decision point, where the change is delivered as a cue (`Cue.woken` with `follow_up`).
- `cancel(work_item_id, by, cascade ∈ CascadePolicy)`; `annotate(work_item_id, Text{authority: principal | external}, readers)` (OQ-390 input, §6.7); `fleet_view(fleet, at?)` (§6.4); `accountability_record(work_item_id)` (§6.3).

**Invariants.** RC-1 reconcile precedes dispatch on every tick and after every restore (`restore(fleet)` rebuilds the views; no memory-only state — Symphony §14.3 rejected). RC-2 one activation per work item (claim lease). RC-3 `blocked` and `handoff` are never stalled: stall detection reads only `active` activations' `last_activity`. RC-4 every dispatch, stop, block, handoff, escalation, owner change and cancel is a `control.work_item.*` event with `causes[]` naming the occurrence or decision that caused it. RC-5 an unowned item is never dispatched. RC-6 the source is written only by effects through Π; the reconciler itself never writes to a source (it *reads*; `mirror` bindings are written by an explicit `mirror_status` effect under the fleet's own `AgentProcess.hosted = OpaqueProcess` principal-less binding? — no: by a kernel `Procedure` of external effects authorized at `seal` under a `policy_rule`, ADR-0053 D-5 shape; default off). RC-7 capacity is the fleet pool's `fan_out` gauge (ADR-0039) with per-state caps as `SchedulingPolicy` data (Symphony per-state limits). RC-8 `restore(fleet)` after a crash yields the same `[FleetAction]` for the same ledger prefix (ADR-0130 R-4 applied to the fleet).

**Failure modes.** `WouldBlock`, `Fenced`, `InsufficientBudget`, `PoolExhausted`, `SourceUnavailable{source_ref}` (skip dispatch this tick; keep activations — Symphony §11.4), `StaleSpec` (re-validation changed eligibility), `UnownedWorkItem`, `OwnerUnacknowledged`, `StateMapUnknownState{state}`, `CascadeBlocked{children}`, `MaxActivations`.

### 6.3 Ownership, accountability and escalation model

**Ownership.** `owner` is one `PrincipalRef` resolving to a human. Rules: O-1 the owner is the `principal` (ADR-0033) of every activation of the item; no `principal`-class content exists for the item that the owner did not author or endorse. O-2 every `ApproverGrant` used on the item is `delegated_by = owner` (ADR-0070 D6); the owner may delegate `may_decide{domains, max_risk_class}` narrower than their own authority, never wider. O-3 `service`/`agent` callers (ADR-0174) can create and wake items but are `delegate`; an item they create is `blocked{missing_owner}` until a human owner is assigned through the source mapping or a surface. O-4 ownership transfer is a two-event handshake (`owner_changed` → `owner_acknowledged`) and revokes approval leases on the item (policy-fingerprint change, ADR-0071 D2). O-5 the owner is the default first target of every escalation and the only party who may resolve `escalation_unresolved`.

**Accountability record** (a projection; no store): `accountability_record(work_item_id) → {owner_history: [owner_changed/acknowledged], activations: [{run_id, activation_no, principal, attendance, stop_reason}], decisions: [security.permission.decided ∪ granted ∪ lease.* for the item's effects, with decider and grant_ref], external_effects: [{effect_id, capability semantic_id, effective_risk_class, decider, terminal}], escalations: [{raised, targets_notified: [message_human effect_ids], resolved{by, resolution}}], handoffs: [{state, entered_by, exited_by}], budget: ADR-0040 view, audit: audit_view.completeness over the activations (ADR-0068), children: [work_item_id]}` — every element is an `EventRef` or an ADR-0066 audit-grade envelope; content-free (Rule C); `verify` covers it through the activations' chains and the fleet run's cross-run anchors (`causes[]` edges are anchored like `spawned/created` pairs — obligation added to AuditPolicy, CF-WS-L8-04).

**Escalation.** Record = `lifecycle.escalation.raised{subject, severity, addressed_to, cause}` / `resolved{resolved_by, resolution}` (ADR-0066, adopted; `cause` extended by dialect to the L8 `EscalationCause` sum: the ADR-0066 obligations (`abandoned`, `unknown_at_finished`, `budget_hard_exceeded`) plus `approval_pending_deadline`, `approvals_exhausted`, `repeated_denials` (ADR-0071), `blocked{BlockReason}`, `stalled_max_retries`, `heal_exhausted`, `owner_unacknowledged`, `source_conflict`, `veto_failed` (a `veto: true` invariant at `finished`)). Route = `EscalationPolicy{targets: [EscalationTarget{ref ∈ {owner, grant(ApproverGrantRef), principal(PrincipalRef), sink(SinkRef)}, after_ms, on ⊆ EscalationCause, evidence ⊆ {last_decision, effect_ids, budget_state, blocked_reason, source_state}}], final ∈ {block, cancel, stop_activation}, max_open_per_owner}` (MUST-data; PagerDuty-shaped chain; SRE roles as the rationale). Semantics: E-1 raising is kernel-produced (Rule P) and precedes any notification; E-2 each notification is a `message_human` effect through Π (audited; counted under `message_human` and, for `sink` targets with `scope = external`, subject to egress policy); E-3 `after_ms` deadlines are `timer` subscriptions (ledger-derived); E-4 resolution requires a responder whose endorsement would be legitimate for the item (`principal` = owner, or a grant covering the cause's domain) — an `external`-authority reply wakes the item (`Cue.woken`) and is recorded as `annotate`, never as `resolved`; E-5 `resolution ∈ {acknowledged, amended{budget | permission | owner}, reassigned, cancelled, expired}`; `amended` carries the ledgered `amend` event ref (ADR-0040 §6, ADR-0071 D4); E-6 chain exhaustion applies `final` and the item is `blocked{escalation_unresolved}` — never silently dropped, never auto-widened (ADR-0017); E-7 evidence rendered to the human is record-derived (ADR-0071 D6 `Explanation` shape); model-authored text appears only as a labelled `Text{authority: delegate}` leaf; E-8 escalations are rate-bounded per owner (`max_open_per_owner`) so a misbehaving fleet cannot page one person into fatigue (S-053 mechanism; ATR catalogue via H7) — beyond the cap, new causes coalesce onto the open escalation (`coalesced_into`).

**Autonomy allocation as data (S-WS-L8-01).** The state map's `entry_allowed_by/exit_allowed_by` per state, `UnattendedPolicy` (ADR-0069), the reviewer chain and `ApproverGrant`s together *are* the per-function automation levels; `ControlBoundary.assignments[authorize | escalate]` records who owned each decision (ADR-0016/0069 D6). Nothing here is a single autonomy number.

### 6.4 Fleet observability and cost requirements

`fleet_view(fleet, at?) → FleetView{items: [{work_item_id, state, owner, source_state, activation?, activation_no, blocked?, handoff?, escalations_open, budget{consumed, remaining}, last_activity, external_effects_count, children_open}], capacity{max_concurrent, running, by_state, retries_due[], suspended}, escalations: [open, by_owner], totals: {by_owner, by_source, by_state} of TokenVector + Money{confidence} + time.{working_ms, human_wait_ms} + env.{active_ms, reserved_ms}, watermark}` — `project`-pure (ADR-0026) over the fleet run, the items' goal budget nodes and the activations' checkpoint views; `derived_from` watermark and rebuild-equality test as every view. Requirements: V-1 every number in the view is a ledger-computed declaration (ADR-0044; no sampled export — Symphony's delta-vs-absolute problem cannot arise); V-2 `blocked`, `handoff` and `suspended` are rendered distinctly from `active` (never one "running" list); V-3 an item with a hosted activation renders ledger-only fields as `n/a{observability}` (T-LCD-15); V-4 the view is what K1/K2 project (WS-K2 V8 approval inbox joins on `work_item_id` through the pending permission's run); V-5 `refresh` is `reconcile(now)` through Group W and is itself ledgered.

`MetricDeclaration`s (level `work_item` / `fleet`, dimensions autonomy/efficiency/reliability/security; `requires_observability ∋ events`; `applies_to_classes = {native, hosted-with-declared-capabilities}`; `charged_to = subject` unless noted; deterministic detectors only): `time_to_first_handoff_ms`, `human_wait_ms_per_item`, `activations_per_item`, `escalations_per_item`, `escalation_ack_latency_ms{severity}`, `blocked_ratio{reason}`, `owner_acknowledge_latency_ms`, `external_effects_per_item{effective_risk_class}`, `message_human_per_item`, `source_conflict_rate`, `spend_per_item{confidence}`, `fleet_utilisation` (running / capacity), `reconcile_latency_ms` (instrument), `stale_dispatch_rate` (re-validation refusals / dispatches). Veto-adjacent: `unowned_dispatch_count` is a `veto: true` trace predicate (0 or the fleet run is success-with-veto).

Fleet cost: totals are L2 views over the fleet pool (`BudgetNode{scope: Experiment}`-shaped, mode `pool`, with per-item goal slices); `Money` carries `confidence` (ADR-0039); hosted activations report per ADR-0165. **Fleet risk (admitted, optional):** the kernel counter dimension `effects.external_irreversible` (count of `irreversible ∧ scope = external` effects reaching `committed`) is added to the ADR-0039 closed list by dialect bump (CF-WS-L8-05) so a definition may set a hard ceiling on the owner pool; exhaustion converts the marginal effect's decision to `deny{reason: irreversibility_ceiling}` at the reserve step (reserve-before-spend, ADR-0040) and escalates. This is the S-WS-L8-05 admission-control *shape* at unit price; value-at-risk pricing is deferred (§6.6, ADR-L8-3).

### 6.5 The fleet as a Lab object (C4 matched-budget conditionality)

`lab/org-policy-v1` (recipe, not a mandate): factors `state_map` (human-gate placement), `unattended_policy ∈ {deny, defer, auto_review}`, `escalation_policy` (chain shape), `max_activations`; `MatchSpec{dimensions: [tokens.*, approvals.requested, time.human_wait_ms, spend, message_human], mode: matched_cap}`; responders are a declared factor (`attendance`; a simulated responder is an instrument model, `charged_to = instrument`, labelled — ADR-0047/0117); headline metrics deterministic (§6.4); every claim a `ComparisonReport` (ADR-0157). No fleet policy is adopted as a default on the strength of a comparison that lacks this recipe; the defaults above (`defer` + chain; foreground cascade; continuation delay) are conditioned rules with assumption-debt records and this recipe as their removal test (T-LCD-05).

### 6.6 What is C4 and what is deferred (rationale in ADR-L8-3)

**C4, specified here:** `WorkItem`/`WorkSourceBinding`/`WorkItemStateMap`/`EscalationPolicy`/`FleetPolicy` records; `run_kind = fleet`; the `control.work_item.*` event family; the reconciler contract and invariants RC-1…8; the `WorkSourceAdapter` read minimum with tri-state capabilities; ownership rules O-1…5 and the acknowledged handoff; the accountability record; escalation routing E-1…8 with the extended `EscalationCause`; `FleetView` and the metric set; the optional irreversibility counter; the Lab recipe. Reference adapters: a `memory`/file tracker fixture (Symphony's `Tracker.Memory` precedent) and a generic signed-webhook ingress; one real tracker adapter only as a plugin (WS-L5).

**Deferred (each with a removal/entry test):** D-1 **human directory and rotations** (resolving "team X on-call" to a `PrincipalRef`; schedule-based `EscalationTarget`s) — deferred because it is an identity-provider concern outside every ratified contract and inside non-goal territory (N1); entry test: a `PrincipalDirectory` component class contract proposed as an ADR with two out-of-process precedents. D-2 **multi-tenant fleets** (several organizations, per-tenant `readers`, cross-fleet ownership) — deferred: ADR-0174 surface sessions are single-principal at C2 (WS-K2); entry test: a tenant boundary that is a `readers` label partition with no new authority class. D-3 **value-at-risk pricing** of fleet irreversibility — deferred: the only evidence is one `provisional` preprint that calls pricing open; entry test: a pricing rule with an assumption-debt record and a matched-budget comparison against the unit counter. D-4 **dispatch prioritization by expected value** — WS-F4; L8 keeps `SchedulingPolicy.order` as data. D-5 **write-side tracker mirroring by the reconciler** (RC-6 default off) — deferred: it needs a principal-less writer, which ADR-0053 D-5 admits only as a sealed `policy_rule` procedure; entry test: the procedure's audit obligations pass AC-L8-06. D-6 **cross-item shared-state coordination** — WS-F5.

### 6.7 Answers to inherited open questions

- **OQ-313 (occurrence keys / replay window):** push sources with a delivery id ⇒ `occurrence_key = delivery_id` (namespaced by `source_ref`); without ⇒ `H(canonical(payload − volatile_fields))` where `volatile_fields` is declared by the adapter; poll sources ⇒ `H(native_id ∥ normalized(source_state) ∥ updated_at)`; replay window `max(2 × poll_interval, 24 h)`, bounded by the inbox run's retention floor; re-deliveries inside the window are `skipped{duplicate_occurrence}`, outside it they are new occurrences whose `spec_hash` comparison makes them no-ops (level-triggered, so a stale re-delivery cannot regress state).
- **OQ-390 (human annotations as ledger events):** yes, as `control.work_item.annotated{work_item_id, subject: EventRef | RunId | row_id, text_ref: ContentAddress of Text{authority ∈ {principal, external}}, readers}` in the fleet run (plane 5, state); never delivered to a model by D1 unless a `HarnessRule` explicitly lifts it into a `ContextItem` with delivery events (T-LCD-13); rows/reports outside a fleet reuse the class on the subject run (WS-B1 to confirm the class name; CF-WS-L8-04).
- **OQ-182 (narrowing-hint cooling-off):** in a fleet, `NarrowingHint`s are aggregated per owner into the accountability record and surfaced at most once per `EscalationPolicy.hint_interval` (default one per item per activation); a hint is never delivered as an escalation.
- **OQ-017 / OQ-027:** answered by §6.1–§6.4 as a whole (records, route, owner, view); the audit half is ADR-0066/0067 plus the CF-WS-L8-04 anchor obligation.
- **OQ-316 (`steer` at C1):** L8 needs only `follow_up` (owner change, spec change and escalation resolution all wait for the next decision point); no pressure to admit `steer`.

### 6.8 Acceptance-criteria sketch

- **AC-L8-01 Fleet restore equivalence.** Kill the reconciler at each of KP-F1 after `control.work_item.observed`, KP-F2 after `claim` before `dispatch`, KP-F3 after `dispatch` before the activation's `lifecycle.run.created`, KP-F4 after an activation's `finished` before `settle`, KP-F5 after `escalation.raised` before its first `message_human` effect; after `restore(fleet)`, `reconcile` yields the same actions, exactly one activation per item, no duplicate dispatch, no duplicate escalation notification (joins the ADR-0132 battery). (C4/Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-02 Blocked ≠ stalled.** An activation suspended `awaiting_approval` for longer than `stall_ms` is never restarted; an `active` activation with no ledger activity for `stall_ms` is stopped and retried with ledger-derived backoff; a source move to a terminal state stops the activation within one tick and cascades per `CascadePolicy`. (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-03 Human gates at Π.** With a state map declaring `Human Review` as a human gate, an agent effect transitioning the source `In Progress → Done` is `security.permission.decided{deny}` with the narrowing leaf's `rule_ref`; `In Progress → Human Review` is allowed under the item's lease; a state not in the map yields `ask` (or `deny`/`defer` under the unattended policy). (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-04 Owner-as-principal.** Every activation's manifest `principal` equals the item's owner at dispatch; a `service`-binding caller creating an item produces `blocked{missing_owner}` and zero dispatches (`unowned_dispatch_count = 0`, veto); `transfer_owner` without `owner_acknowledged` blocks dispatch and revokes leases. (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-05 Escalation route.** For each `EscalationCause` a fixture produces exactly one `raised`, notifications in chain order at the declared `after_ms` (timers survive a restart, AC-B3-07), one `resolved` by a legitimate responder, and `IllegitimateEndorsement` for a `delegate`/`external` responder's attempt to resolve; chain exhaustion applies `final` and leaves `blocked{escalation_unresolved}`; `max_open_per_owner` coalesces. (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-06 Accountability record completeness.** For a three-activation item with two approvals, one lease, one escalation and one follow-up child, `accountability_record` cites every decision, external effect, escalation and owner event by `EventRef`; removing any one from the ledger is `Tampered` under `verify`; `audit_view.coverage` includes the fleet-anchor obligation (CF-WS-L8-04). (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-07 Ownership graph.** A child with `block_parent_completion` keeps the parent out of `done`; parent cancellation under `foreground` cancels the child first; a cross-fleet `owner_ref` is refused at creation; a cycle is refused. (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-08 Source of truth split.** A conflicting human source move during a ledgered handoff yields `blocked{source_conflict}` + escalation, never a silent overwrite of either half; `spec_hash` history reproduces every observed source state. (Stage 4)
- **AC-L8-09 Adapter capability declaration.** An adapter declaring `push_delivery_id = unknown` is probed; re-delivered payloads dedupe within the replay window by hash; a stale re-delivery outside the window is a no-op by `spec_hash`. (Stage 5)
- **AC-L8-10 Fleet view = ledger.** `fleet_view` totals equal the sum of the items' ADR-0040 views and L2 cost views at the same watermark; hosted activations render `n/a` on ledger-only fields, never 0. (Stage 5)
- **AC-L8-11 Irreversibility ceiling.** With `effects.external_irreversible.hard = N` on an owner pool, the (N+1)-th such effect across two concurrent items is refused at reserve with `irreversibility_ceiling` and escalated; with the dimension unset nothing changes. (Stage 5, optional)
- **AC-L8-12 Lab recipe.** `lab/org-policy-v1` registers and runs at Stage-6 size with a `MatchSpec` over approvals/human-wait/spend; `register` refuses an arm lacking one (T-LCD-14); results rows carry `attendance`/responder strata. (Stage 6)

## 10. Confidence

**High** that the layer is buildable as records, projections and policies over ratified contracts with no new authority path: every operation in §6.2 maps onto an ADR-0130/0131/0155/0176 operation, every record onto an ADR-0016/0040/0066/0070 record, and the reconciler pattern is read in Symphony source at a pinned commit and corroborated by Kubernetes' controller model. **High** for the ownership rules O-1…5: they are consequences of ADR-0033's `principal` minting and ADR-0070 D6, with SRE's acknowledged-handoff practice as the production precedent. **Medium** for the escalation route and the state-map-at-Π enforcement: the pieces are ratified (escalation class, Π narrowing leaves, `ActionPattern`), but no audited system does either, so the integrated behaviour is `no-precedent`; both are C4/Stage 4 with fixtures. **Medium-low** for the defaults (`defer` + chain, foreground cascade, continuation delay): they follow Symphony's and Cursor's practice, but no matched-budget comparison exists anywhere — hence the Lab recipe as their removal test. **Low** for anything fleet-risk-related beyond a unit counter: the sole source is a two-week-old `provisional` preprint, which is why pricing is deferred and the counter is optional.
