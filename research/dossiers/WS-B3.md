## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream decides **what it means for a HarnessHarness run to survive the death of the process, host, worker or environment executing it, and to wake again on an event** — over the run ledger WS-B1 ratified (ADR-0026…0029) and the effect lifecycle WS-B2 ratified (ADR-0030…0032). Concretely it fixes: (i) the **durability contract** — what a checkpoint *is* (a projection of the durable prefix plus the control strategy's `checkpoint_ref`, never a second store), the `restore` procedure and the per-scope **recovery table** that says what happens to every open scope on resume; (ii) the **replay rules** restated from ADR-0028 and made operational (reconstruct; never re-execute a committed effect; never resample the model to rebuild a view; open model calls become failed attempts, not replays); (iii) **lease semantics** — the four lease scopes the runtime uses (writer lease, effect/resource lease, environment lease, wakeup claim), their generation/fencing, expiry, liveness-probe shortening and progress payloads; (iv) the **wakeup event model** — durable subscriptions, durable occurrence records, claim-then-fire delivery with idempotent firing, coalescing, and the closed trigger sum (timers, schedules, approval decisions, child/effect terminals, environment readiness, external sources such as PR/tracker/chat events); (v) **suspended runs and long-lived goals** — the run state machine gains `suspended`, a goal outlives its activations through a continuation chain, and asynchronous approvals (WS-H7) are one trigger kind; (vi) **self-healing environments** — verification of environment handles on resume and reprovisioning as a ledgered, class-`reversible` effect under a declared healing policy; (vii) the **failure-injection acceptance battery** — the kill-point matrix that unifies the batteries WS-B1, B2, F2, E5, G2 and L2 each sketched, plus duplicate-effect, duplicate-wakeup and stale-worker tests.

It explicitly leaves to neighbours: the ledger envelope, ids, `seq`, hash chain, `project()` and the single fenced writer (**WS-B1** — adopted verbatim); the effect state machine, risk classes, idempotency key, `probe`, compensation (**WS-B2** — adopted; this dossier only says *when* recovery invokes them); the control strategy's `checkpoint/restore` operations and the `Cue.resumed` input (**WS-F1** — adopted; B3 supplies the `recovery_decision` that cue carries); the envelope's re-arm at resume, retry policy, deadlines and the stop protocol (**WS-F2** — adopted; B3 supplies the ledger-derived timers the envelope re-arms from and confirms OQ-119 with it); the environment handle model, snapshots and image digests (**WS-B5**; B3 supplies the healing policy and the resume-time verification contract B5 must expose); the helper protocol, `on_kernel_loss`, detached children (**WS-E5** — adopted; OQ-252 answered in §6.5); the approval request/response contract and `attendance` (**WS-H7** — adopted; B3 owns the suspend/resume mechanics H7 references); the sub-agent orchestrator's spawn/merge semantics (**WS-F3**; B3 fixes only that a child run holds its own lease and how a parent resumes over open children); audit-grade obligations on recovery events (**WS-H6** — adopted); ingress surfaces that turn webhooks and chat events into occurrence records (**WS-K1/K2/L8** — B3 fixes the record they must write); fork/branch/counterfactual execution (**WS-B4**); every language, runtime and storage choice (**WS-L1**, ADR-0050).

## 2. Key questions

1. (doc 3 §5, verbatim) Design durable execution over the event store (B1) and effect model (B2): checkpoints, restart from log, leases, event-driven wakeups (schedules, webhooks, PR/tracker events), crash recovery with idempotent replay, self-healing environments (B5), long-lived goals, async approvals (H7).
2. (brief) Deliver: durability contract (checkpoint/restore operations, replay rules, lease semantics, wakeup event model); failure-injection acceptance criteria (kill at tool-cycle boundaries; duplicate-effect tests); ADR.
3. (phase-1 §6.9, binding) B3 resumes from the last durable effect phase and emits `action.effect.unknown` for every `committed` effect on resume — how, exactly, per open scope kind?
4. (added) What do the production systems that run agents for hours or days actually persist and what do they *not* restore after a restart — and which of those gaps are deliberate?
5. (added) Is a "checkpoint" anything more than a ledger watermark once ADR-0026/0028 hold, and if not, what is the cheapest restore path?
6. (added) How does a wakeup get delivered exactly once *enough* — what is the dedup key, who claims it, and what happens to an occurrence that arrives while the run is active, suspended, or gone?
7. (added) What must a self-healing environment never do silently (OQ-045 helper boundary, OQ-090 detached children, OQ-095 hibernation minutes, OQ-252 preserved children)?
8. (added) Which of these obligations are C0 (crash consistency of a single run is already an ADR-0028 acceptance criterion) and which are C1/C2 (suspension, external triggers, goals, healing)?

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence (a failure mode exists / an architecture is implementable)

**F1 — Every durable agent system read here restores by *reading a log*, and none re-drives control code to get there; the ones that checkpoint at code granularity re-execute side effects.** Managed Agents: `wake(sessionId)` + `getSession(id)` "resume from the last event" (S-047). Codex daemon recovery: "using normal resume semantics", `exclude_turns: true`, only "threads successfully persisted through the existing thread store" (`daemon_snapshot.rs`). Cursor: the loop moved onto an event-history engine and reliability went from one to two nines (S-052). Logos: transcript "owned by no process" (S-073, `provisional`). The counter-cases are instructive: LangGraph's `interrupt` "resumes from the start of the node, re-executing all logic" (`types.py` L864) because its checkpoint is per super-step, and Cloudflare fibers state outright that "The original lambda is gone. On recovery, you only have the `name` and `snapshot`" — recovery code must decide what to re-issue (S-470). **Implication:** the checkpoint is the ledger watermark plus the strategy's own serialized state (WS-F1 `checkpoint_ref` on every `control.decision`); restore is `project(run, checkpoint_view, head)`; nothing between the last `control.decision` and the crash is re-executed by code — it is *resolved* per effect phase (ADR-0030) and *re-decided* by the strategy from `Cue.resumed` (WS-F1). This is ADR-0028's "reconstruction" made operational and is the only design consistent with "no repeated action" across kill points.

**F2 — Restart-time gaps are deliberate in the systems that document them, and they cluster on three things: timers, in-flight work, and blocked-on-human state.** Symphony §14.3: "No retry timers are restored from prior process memory. No running sessions are assumed recoverable." — recovery is "fresh polling of active issues" plus "re-dispatching eligible work" and startup terminal-workspace cleanup; the SPEC calls the in-memory scheduler state "intentionally" so. goose clears `currently_running` on every job found running at load (`scheduler.rs` L600–612) — an interrupted scheduled run is forgotten, not resumed, and no record of the missed occurrence exists. Cloudflare keeps interrupted fiber rows and calls `onFiberRecovered`, but bounds them (24 h, 5-min backoff). Symphony is careful about the third gap: a worker that exited while `:turn_input_required`/`:approval_required` is moved to `blocked`, and the stall sweep skips blocked issues (`orchestrator.ex` L600–620) — restarting a run that is waiting on a human is a distinct failure from a stalled one. **Implication:** durable recovery needs (a) timers *derived from the ledger* (a `control.retry.scheduled{not_before}` or a wakeup subscription is the timer; process memory never is), (b) an explicit in-flight resolution per scope (§6.3 recovery table), and (c) a `suspended` run state that stall detection and lease expiry treat differently from `active`.

**F3 — Leases converge on the same four fields and two refinements.** OpenHands (`conversation_lease.py`): `owner_instance_id, generation, expires_at`, renewal at ttl/3, takeover when expired **or** when the owner pid on the same host is dead; `guarded_write` fences every write. prime-agent (`session-lease.ts`): owner `{token, pid, processStartId}`; `isLeaseOwnerAlive` requires the pid to be alive *and* its start-id to match, so pid reuse cannot impersonate a live owner; stale owners are reclaimed by rename; second holders get `SessionAlreadyActiveError`. Symphony: a claim set with a stall timeout and one mutation authority (§7.4) — a lease without a token, safe only because there is one authority. Codex: `WriterLockCoordinator` per thread (via WS-B1). Temporal: the Start-To-Close timeout "forces" retry after a worker crash (S-201). Cursor hibernates VMs between messages (S-052) — the paused-but-alive case WS-B2 R6 warns about. **Implication:** ADR-0030's lease contract (generation fencing, ttl, renewal ≤ ttl/3, non-Byzantine clocks) is confirmed and extended with two mechanisms both precedented twice: *liveness-probe shortening* (takeover before expiry when the holder is provably dead on the same host — OpenHands, prime-agent) and *identity-hardened liveness* (pid + process start-id — prime-agent; OpenHands host check). Both are optimisations of recovery delay, never of safety: the fence remains the generation.

**F4 — Durable wakeups are "subscription + occurrence + claim", and the claim is what prevents double firing.** prime-agent `claimDueInState` writes a `dispatch` record per due job, advances `nextRunAt`, and marks a job already claimed with `lastSkippedAt` instead of dispatching twice; `recoverInterruptedInState` re-queues dispatches the crash interrupted; the daemon supervisor "only wakes non-resident trees; firing and delivery stay worker-owned" and re-arms after restart ("including after a daemon restart", CHANGELOG). goose sets `currently_running` and persists it *before* executing and skips `paused` jobs at fire time. Cloudflare persists schedules in the agent's store, wakes by alarm, and dedupes `startFiber` by `idempotencyKey` (`accepted: false` on a duplicate). Temporal Signals are durable writes that do not depend on a worker being up; Updates are validated before entering history (S-474). Cursor agents "subscribe to an event source … and wake when something happens" and follow-ups "wait for the next tool call" (S-054). prime-agent's heartbeat has the same two delivery modes: `steer` (interrupt the current turn) vs `follow_up` (wait). **Implication:** the model is a durable `WakeupSubscription`, a durable `occurred` record written by whoever observes the event (timer service, ingress adapter, permission responder), and a `fired` event that is a *claim* under the run's writer lease keyed by `(subscription_id, occurrence_key)`; a second claim is `skipped{already_claimed}`; delivery into an active run is a cue at a decision point with a declared mode `{steer, follow_up}`. External occurrences carry `authority = external` (ADR-0033) — a webhook can wake a run, never endorse anything.

**F5 — Long-lived work is modelled as a chain of bounded runs over a durable objective, not as one eternal execution.** Cursor moved from "eternal" workflows to "multiple shorter ones that exit after completing a single task" for versionability (S-052) and exposes `/goal` as the durable objective (S-054). Temporal's Continue-As-New "checkpoint[s] your Workflow's state and start[s] a fresh Workflow" with the same Workflow ID and a new Run ID because histories are capped (51,200 events) and code versions drift; "Entity Workflows … represent durable objects, not just processes" (S-472/04). Symphony re-dispatches a still-active issue after a *normal* worker exit (continuation retry, `handle_agent_down(:normal, …)`) and passes `attempt` to the prompt (§12.3), so the issue — not the session — is the long-lived thing. Anthropic's long-running harness makes the next session "read the git logs and progress files" (S-041), i.e. carried state is a small named artifact set (WS-D3 `resume_set`). **Implication:** a `Goal` (HIR/1 entity) outlives runs; a run is one *activation*; activations chain by reference (`continued_from{run_id, at_seq, head_hash}` — the ADR-0027 fork-by-reference shape with a `kind: continuation` that forbids divergence); the carried state is the `resume_set` plus the budget node, never a re-copied transcript; the wakeup subscriptions of a goal survive the end of an activation. This also bounds ledger growth per run without a second store.

**F6 — Environment loss is handled as an effect the model sees, and reprovisioning is a recipe, not magic.** Managed Agents: "If the container died, the harness caught the failure as a tool-call error and passed it back to Claude … a new container could be reinitialized with a standard recipe: `provision({resources})`" (S-047). Symphony reuses preserved workspaces after restart and cleans terminal ones at startup (§8.6, §14.3); Cursor checkpoints/restores/forks VM images and hibernates between messages (S-052); Anthropic's `init.sh` re-creates a runnable dev environment and runs an end-to-end test (S-041); Cursor's stated next step is agents that "report when secrets are missing, network access is blocked" and "autoinstall" (S-052). WS-E5 gives the helper `hello(resume_session_id)` and `on_kernel_loss ∈ {terminate, preserve_until}`; WS-G2 supplies the level-triggered rule (re-examine current state on restart, S-426). **Implication:** on resume the runtime *verifies* every environment handle (`attached | reattachable | lost`); a lost environment is healed only under a declared `HealingPolicy` whose reprovision is a ledgered effect (`spawn_process`/`exec` domains, reversibility `reversible`, scope `workspace_local`) built from the manifest's `environment_ref` (content-addressed image digest, WS-B5) and the definition's init procedure; uncommitted workspace state that cannot be restored from a snapshot is reported to the model as an observation with `authority = environment`; the model-facing "report missing secrets / re-run init" behaviour is a `HarnessRule` with an assumption-debt record, never kernel code.

**F7 — Asynchronous approval is a suspend/wakeup, and the audited systems that lack it pay for it in blocking.** Cursor: "the cost of blocking is much higher … in the cloud, it could sit there for hours" and uses the PR as the approval point (S-052, via WS-H7); Symphony blocks the issue on `approval_required` and excludes it from stall restarts; MCP `input_required{requestState}` is the target-side form (via H7/E4); LangGraph's `interrupt` is the durable primitive with the re-execution hazard. WS-H7 §6.4 already specifies the async form as `lifecycle.run.suspended{reason: awaiting_approval, permission_id}` + `control.wakeup.scheduled{trigger: permission_decided(permission_id)}` with the writer lease allowed to lapse and resumption under a fresh generation, and proves "Nothing after `intended` exists to replay". **Implication:** B3 owns the generic mechanism H7 instantiates: `suspend(run, reasons[], subscriptions[])` releases the lease deliberately (not by expiry), the run's open scopes are limited to `intended`/`pending` (nothing `committed` may be open across a suspension — AC-B3-06), and `respond` is one occurrence source among several.

**F8 — Detached and orphaned processes need a durable journal independent of the run's process.** prime-agent's `orphan-process-journal.ts` fsyncs `{pid, ownerPid, kernelPid, processStartId, active}` per spawned child "so the host can reap per kernel"; Codex keeps background terminals alive across turn abort (WS-B2 M7, WS-E5 F7); WS-E5 answers OQ-090 (child effects in `committed` owned by the environment handle; `on_kernel_loss`) and raises OQ-252 (preserved children vs B3's recovery window). **Implication:** the ledger's `detached_effect_ids[]` plus the executor's process events (`process{spawned | exited | detached}` in the capture manifest) *are* the journal; on resume the runtime re-attaches to the helper session (`hello(resume_session_id)`) and reconciles each detached child to `observed`, `unknown` or `terminated`; the preserve ttl is bounded by the recovery window (§6.5).

**F9 — Idempotent command handling at the daemon boundary is a separate concern from effect idempotency and is also precedented.** prime-agent's `command-recovery-journal` records `received → result → acknowledged` under a client-minted idempotency key so a retried command after a lost response returns the recorded result; its changelog fixed a bug where a remote message was "delivered twice when the daemon request timed out or the response was lost" by sending "exactly once per call" and surfacing post-send failures rather than resending. This is Stripe's pattern (S-200 via B2) at the WS-L1 §6.5 boundary. **Implication:** `submit`/`request_permission`/wakeup-injection verbs across the kernel boundary carry a caller idempotency key; a duplicate returns the recorded result — an obligation for WS-K4's boundary contract (CF-282), not new kernel machinery.

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

**Against ledger-derived recovery at all (R1).** Symphony runs multi-agent coding orchestration in production with *no* durable orchestrator state — "Restart recovery is tracker-driven and filesystem-driven (without a durable orchestrator DB)" (§7.4) — and Managed Agents lets the model decide retries after a container death (S-047). Both are simpler than what is proposed. *Why it does not overturn:* both delegate durability to an external source of truth (a tracker; a session log) and restrict themselves to reprovisionable, workspace-local effects; Symphony explicitly does not restore timers or in-flight sessions and pushes approval and idempotency semantics to adapters. HarnessHarness's ledger *is* the external source of truth (ADR-0026), and R-2.2.2's compensable/irreversible classes are exactly what those systems do not cover. The reconciliation *pattern* is adopted (level-triggered verification on resume); the "no durable state" *choice* is not.

**Against a `suspended` run state and durable subscriptions (R2).** Cursor's cloud agents apparently achieve event-driven waking on top of a durable-execution engine without a documented suspend state; goose and Cloudflare treat schedules as data outside the run. Adding `suspended`, subscriptions, occurrences and claims is schema and code the Stage-0/1 runtime does not need. *Why it does not overturn:* (a) WS-H7's asynchronous `defer` already requires the suspend/wakeup pair and is C1/Stage 2; (b) without a durable occurrence record, a wakeup that arrives while the runtime is down is lost (goose's forget-on-restart) or double-fired (no claim); (c) the whole mechanism is C1 and adds no field to any C0 entity — the C0 slice is single-run crash recovery only (§6.8). The cost is staged, not avoided.

**Against liveness-probe shortening of leases (R3).** A pid-liveness probe is host-local and can be wrong under pid reuse, containers with separate pid namespaces, or a paused process (SIGSTOP, VM hibernation — Cursor S-052) that is "alive" yet not renewing. *Accepted, and it is why shortening is an optimisation of delay, never of safety:* the generation fence (ADR-0030 inv. 6; WS-B1 `Fenced`) is what prevents a paused holder from writing after takeover; prime-agent's start-id check is adopted to defeat pid reuse; probes are only consulted for holders on the same host (OpenHands rule); cross-host holders wait for expiry. Clock assumptions stay as ADR-0030 states them.

**Against continuation chains for long-lived goals (R4).** A chain of runs complicates identity (results rows keyed by `run_id`), audit (cross-run consistency proofs, WS-H6 §6 rule 4), and cost totals; Symphony and Cursor simply start another session. *Why it does not overturn:* ADR-0027 already gives fork-by-reference with `source_head_hash`, WS-H6 already verifies cross-run anchors, and WS-L2's budget node is scoped to the `Goal` so totals aggregate naturally; a single run whose ledger grows without bound hits exactly the history-size and code-drift problems Temporal documents (S-472/04). The alternative — one run per activation with *no* link — loses the audit trail of the goal, which the sponsor's "deep audit trails" requirement (R-2.8.6) forbids. The chain is restricted to `kind: continuation` (no divergence) so B4's fork semantics are untouched.

**Against reprovisioning environments automatically (R5).** Reprovisioning can silently destroy uncommitted work and can loop (a broken init script re-run forever); Cursor lists self-healing as *future* work; Managed Agents makes the model decide. *Why it does not overturn:* healing runs only under a declared `HealingPolicy` with `max_heals`, only as a ledgered `reversible`, `workspace_local` effect, only after `verify_environment` returns `lost`, and always with an observation to the model listing what was not restorable (`authority = environment`); the default policy for `interactive` attendance is `ask`, for `unattended` it is `reprovision` bounded by `max_heals` then `stop{environment_lost}`. The model-side "diagnose and re-run init" behaviour stays a `HarnessRule` with a debt record (T-LCD-05), so the Lab can delete it.

**Against treating an open model call as failed on resume (R6).** Re-issuing a model call after a crash costs money and may yield a different sample; Cursor instead *rewinds* the client stream and shows the retried step's output. *Accepted:* a model call is a `model_call`-domain effect whose output is not repeat-safe, but its *world* effect is nil and its cost is accounted; the retry is an ordinary `model_attempt` under WS-F2's `RetryPolicy` with `retries` charged (ADR-0039) and the same `model_call_id`; the ledger never held the partial stream (ephemeral deltas, AC-B1-12), so there is nothing to rewind in the record — surfaces rewind (K-track), exactly as Cursor's client does.

**Negative results.** (N1) No audited system records a durable *occurrence* for a wakeup separately from firing it; prime-agent's dispatch record is the closest (claim + skip) — `occurred` as an authority-labelled ledger event is `no-precedent`. (N2) No audited system verifies environment handles on resume against a content-addressed spec; all either reuse a directory (Symphony) or reprovision on demand (Managed Agents). (N3) No audited system distinguishes `suspended` from `idle` in its lease semantics except Symphony's `blocked` set and prime-agent's passivation; neither ties it to effect phases. (N4) Cloudflare's docs do not state delivery semantics for scheduled tasks (S-471) — the "exactly-once wakeup" question is open in the industry; this dossier answers it as at-least-once occurrence + idempotent claim. (N5) Cursor S-051 documents no checkpoint/resume mechanism despite 52-hour runs; the mechanism is in S-052 (engine) not in the harness.

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

### 6.1 Position

1. **A checkpoint is a view, not a store.** `Checkpoint = project(run, checkpoint_view, until_seq)` over the durable prefix (ADR-0026): `{run_id, seq, head_hash, control_state_ref (WS-F1 checkpoint_ref of the last admitted control.decision), effect_summary: [{effect_id, phase, attempt_no}], open_scopes: [ScopeRef], env_handles: [{handle, spec_ref, last_verified_seq}], subscriptions: [subscription_id], budget: {budget_id, consumed, reserved}, resume_set_heads (WS-D3), pending_permissions: [permission_id]}`. It may be materialised as a blob for fast restore and cited from `control.subagent.result.child_head.checkpoint_ref` (WS-H6), but restore from the blob and restore from the raw prefix must yield the same `view_hash` (AC-B3-02). There is no separate checkpoint store, no snapshot of process memory, and no "eternal" run.
2. **Restore is a procedure with an auditable decision, not a replay.** ADR-0028 rules: reconstruct; never re-execute a committed effect; never resample the model to rebuild a view. Every open scope is resolved by the recovery table (§6.3); the strategy is re-entered through `Cue.resumed{last_durable, recovery_decision}` (WS-F1); the envelope re-arms from the ledger (WS-F2 G-RESUME); the decision is recorded once as `lifecycle.run.resumed{from_seq, lease, recovery_decision}` (ADR-0026 table; audit-grade per WS-H6).
3. **Four lease scopes, one fence.** Writer lease (C0, WS-B1), effect/resource lease (C1, ADR-0030 §6), environment lease (C1, with WS-B5/E5), wakeup claim (C1). All share `Lease{scope, holder: HolderIdentity{host, pid?, start_id?, participant_ref}, generation, expires_at, progress?}`; the *generation* fences; expiry bounds recovery delay; a same-host liveness probe may *shorten* takeover; a paused holder is defeated by the fence, never by the clock.
4. **Wakeups are subscription → occurrence → claim → fire.** Subscriptions and occurrences are durable ledger records; firing is a claim under the run's fresh writer lease keyed by `(subscription_id, occurrence_key)`; delivery semantics are *at-least-once occurrence, idempotent fire*; a run that is active receives the occurrence as a cue at a decision point with a declared `delivery_mode ∈ {steer, follow_up}` (never mid-effect — Cursor S-054, prime-agent).
5. **Runs suspend; goals persist.** `RunState = created → active ⇄ suspended → finished` (`recovering` is transient and never durable). A `Goal` outlives its activations; activations chain by `continued_from` (ADR-0027 fork-by-reference with `kind: continuation`); the carried state is the `resume_set` (WS-D3) plus the goal-scoped `BudgetNode` (ADR-0040); subscriptions may be goal-scoped so a finished activation can still be woken.
6. **Environments are verified on resume and healed only by policy.** `verify_environment(handle) → attached | reattachable | lost`; healing is a ledgered `reversible`/`workspace_local` effect under `HealingPolicy`; loss of unrecoverable state is an observation to the model; model-side recovery behaviour is a `HarnessRule`.

### 6.2 Interface-contract sketch (language-neutral; operations, inputs/outputs, invariants, failure modes)

**Durability (C0 slice unless marked C1).**
- `checkpoint(run, until?: Seq) → Checkpoint` — pure over the durable prefix (ADR-0026 `project` invariant); `Checkpoint.view_hash` reproducible. Failures: `UnknownRun`, `SourceIncomplete`.
- `restore(run, holder: HolderIdentity, mode ∈ {takeover, continue}) → RecoveryPlan{lease, from_seq, recovery_decision, actions: [RecoveryAction]}` — procedure: (1) `acquire_writer(run, holder, ttl)` with takeover per §6.4 (failure `WouldBlock(active_holder)` when the current holder is alive and unexpired — Codex "recheck under the same permit" precedent); (2) `head(run)`, `verify(run)` (ADR-0029; failure `Tampered` aborts restore); (3) build the checkpoint view; (4) compute `recovery_decision{last_durable_phase, action ∈ {continue, resolve_unknowns, suspend, stop{reason}}}` from the recovery table; (5) append `lifecycle.run.resumed{from_seq, lease, recovery_decision, holder, cause ∈ {crash, takeover, wakeup, operator, continuation}}`; (6) execute `RecoveryAction`s in order — each is itself ledgered (effect `unknown`/`probe`, `model.call.failed`, `control.timeout.fired`, `verify_environment`, detached reconciliation, budget reservation release/convert per ADR-0040); (7) `envelope.arm` (WS-F2); (8) hand `Cue.resumed` to the strategy (WS-F1). Invariants: **R-1 no re-execution** — no `action.effect.committed` with `attempt_no` unchanged is dispatched again; **R-2 no resampling** — no `model.call.requested` is issued to rebuild a view; **R-3 exactly one `lifecycle.run.resumed` per restore**; **R-4 idempotent restore** — a crash during `restore` and a second `restore` yield the same actions (every action keyed by scope id and durable before the next); **R-5 fenced** — every appended event carries the new `lease_generation`. Failures: `Fenced`, `Tampered`, `ManifestUnresolvable` (definition/profile no longer resolvable — `resolve(mode = execute)` refuses revoked heads, ADR-0037), `DefinitionChanged` (resume verification, ADR-0025 `verify_resume` — add-only changes proceed with `lifecycle.definition.changed`, others refuse).
- `suspend(run, lease, reasons: [SuspendReason], subscriptions: [WakeupSubscription], release_lease: bool = true) → SeqRange` (C1) — appends `lifecycle.run.suspended{reasons, subscription_ids, resume_policy}` then `lifecycle.lease.released{reason: suspended}`. Invariant **S-1**: no effect is in `prepared` or `committed` at suspension (open effects must reach a terminal or `unknown` first — H7 R5 generalised; AC-B3-06); pending permissions and `intended` effects may remain. `SuspendReason ∈ {awaiting_approval{permission_id}, awaiting_event{subscription_id}, awaiting_timer{at}, awaiting_child{child_run_id}, awaiting_effect{effect_id} (long-running target-side effect, MCP tasks), awaiting_environment{handle}, operator_pause, hibernated}`. Failures: `OpenCommittedEffects[effect_ids]`, `Fenced`.
- `continue_goal(goal_ref, from_run, carried: {resume_set_heads, budget_id}, manifest_delta) → RunId` (C1) — opens a new run with `lifecycle.run.created{continued_from{run_id, at_seq, head_hash}, activation_no}`; source run must be `finished{stop_reason ∈ {completed_activation, budget_exhausted, …}}` or `suspended`; goal-scoped subscriptions are re-bound. Invariants: no divergence (`continued_from` is unique per source head; a second continuation from the same head is `AlreadyContinued`); budget node is the goal's (ADR-0040 `scope: Goal`); `verify_resume` applies. Failures: `AlreadyContinued`, `GoalFinished`, `BudgetExhausted`.

**Leases (C0 writer; C1 others).**
- `acquire(scope ∈ {writer(run), effect(effect_id), resource(key), environment(handle), wakeup(subscription_id, occurrence_key)}, holder: HolderIdentity, ttl) → Lease{lease_id, generation, expires_at}`; `renew(lease, progress?: ProgressPayload) → Lease` (generation unchanged; `progress` readable by a successor — Temporal heartbeat-details precedent, ADR-0030 §6); `release(lease, reason)`; `probe_holder(lease) → alive | dead | unknown` (same-host only; `unknown` off-host); `takeover(scope, holder) → Lease` permitted iff `expires_at ≤ now ∨ probe_holder = dead`; generation strictly increases on takeover. Invariants: **L-1** the ledger refuses any append whose `lease_generation` is not the current writer generation (`Fenced`, WS-B1); **L-2** `ttl` per scope kind is a manifest constant with renewal at ≤ ttl/3 (ADR-0030); **L-3** expiry of the writer lease triggers recovery (`restore(mode = takeover)`) by the wakeup service or the next holder; **L-4** expiry of an environment lease never destroys the environment before `verify_environment` runs under the new writer lease; **L-5** reservations held under a lease convert or release at takeover (ADR-0040). Failures: `WouldBlock(active_holder)`, `Fenced`, `HolderUnknown`. Every transition is `lifecycle.lease.acquired/renewed/released/fenced` (WS-B1 table) with `scope` added to the payload (CF-280).

**Wakeups (C1; external sources C1/Stage 4; protocol lowering C2).**
- `subscribe(owner: RunId | GoalRef, trigger: Trigger, policy: WakeupPolicy) → subscription_id` — appends `control.wakeup.scheduled{subscription}` (existing class name kept; payload extended, CF-280). `Trigger` (closed sum, HIR/1 dialect): `timer{at}` · `schedule{expression, timezone, kind ∈ {cron, interval}}` · `permission_decided{permission_id}` · `child_terminal{child_run_id}` · `effect_terminal{effect_id}` · `environment_ready{handle}` · `retry_due{scope_id}` (derived from `control.retry.scheduled{not_before}`, WS-F2) · `external{source_ref: VersionedRef, filter: TriggerFilter}` (PR/CI/tracker/chat events delivered by an ingress adapter; `source_ref` names a registered ingress with its own provenance) · `manual{principal}`. `WakeupPolicy{delivery_mode ∈ {steer, follow_up}, coalesce ∈ {none, latest, all}, max_pending, expires_at?, attendance_required ∈ {interactive, async, unattended}, occurrence_key_fn: OccurrenceKeyRule}`. Failures: `TriggerUnregistered`, `AttendanceUnknown`, `SubscriptionLimit`.
- `record_occurrence(subscription_id, occurrence_key, payload_ref, observed_at, provenance) → SeqRange` — appends `control.wakeup.occurred{subscription_id, occurrence_key, payload_ref, observed_at, provenance{origin ∈ {kernel (timers, effect/child terminals, permission responder), ingress(source_ref)}, authority ∈ {kernel, principal, external}}}` **durable before any run acts** (Temporal Signals; goose persist-before-fire). Written by the timer service, the permission responder (`respond`, WS-H7), the orchestrator (child terminal), the executor (effect terminal) or an ingress adapter; the ledger of the *owner* run (or the goal's current activation) is the destination; if no activation is open, the occurrence is appended to a goal-scoped inbox run (a run with `kind: inbox`, no strategy) so it is never lost. Idempotent on `(subscription_id, occurrence_key)` (second write is `skipped{duplicate_occurrence}`). External payloads are `Text`/blob with `authority = external`; they never endorse and can never widen a permission (ADR-0035).
- `fire(subscription_id, occurrence_key, holder) → fired | skipped{reason ∈ {already_claimed, coalesced, expired, subscription_cancelled, run_finished}}` — the wakeup service acquires a `wakeup` claim lease, then `restore(run, holder, mode = takeover | continue)` (or `continue_goal` for goal-scoped subscriptions), appends `control.wakeup.fired{subscription_id, occurrence_key, occurred_event: EventRef, lease}`; the strategy receives `Cue.woken{trigger, payload_ref}` (a new `Cue` variant proposed to WS-F1, CF-281) at the next decision point per `delivery_mode`. Invariants: **W-1** at most one `fired` per `(subscription_id, occurrence_key)`; **W-2** `occurred` precedes `fired` in the ledger; **W-3** no `fired` while an effect is `committed` (delivery waits for the effect's terminal — Cursor "wait for the next tool call"); **W-4** an occurrence against a `finished` run with no goal subscription is `skipped{run_finished}` and audited, never silently dropped; **W-5** coalescing is recorded (`skipped{coalesced, superseded_by}`). Failures: `Fenced`, `WouldBlock`.
- `cancel_subscription(subscription_id, reason)`; `list_subscriptions(owner)`; `due(now) → [(subscription_id, occurrence_key)]` for timer/schedule triggers — computed from the ledger (schedules are data, WS-A5 MUST-data; the next due time is derived, never stored as the only copy).
- Retry timers: `control.retry.scheduled{scope_id, attempt_no, not_before}` (WS-F2) **is** a `retry_due` subscription; on restore, `due()` recomputes every pending retry from the ledger; a retry whose scope has since terminated is `skipped{scope_closed}`. This closes Symphony's §14.3 gap by construction.

**Environments (C1; verification C0-shaped because it is a read).**
- `verify_environment(run, lease, handle) → attached{helper_session, containment_report} | reattachable{helper_session} | lost{cause ∈ {helper_gone, image_missing, workspace_missing, lease_expired, containment_changed}}` — uses WS-E5 `hello(resume_session_id)` and WS-B5's handle inspection; appends `action.environment.verified{handle, verdict, spec_ref, world_state_ref?}`. Level-triggered (WS-G2): compares current state to the manifest's `environment_ref` and the last `action.world_state.snapshot/patch`.
- `heal(run, lease, handle, policy: HealingPolicy) → healed{new_handle, restored ∈ {full, snapshot{ref}, none}, lost_items_ref} | refused{reason} | ask{permission_id}` — `HealingPolicy{on_lost ∈ {reprovision, snapshot_restore_then_reprovision, ask, fail}, max_heals, verify_after: Ref<Validator>?, preserve_detached: bool}` (MUST-data in the Harness Definition; default by `attendance`: `interactive → ask`, `async | unattended → reprovision`); reprovision is an effect `{domain: spawn_process | exec, reversibility: reversible, scope: workspace_local}` through the ordinary lifecycle (authorized by Π, `committed` before dispatch), producing `action.environment.attached{handle, image_ref}` and `action.environment.healed{from_handle, to_handle, cause, restored, heal_no, verification_verdict}`; `lost_items_ref` is delivered to the model as an observation with `authority = environment`. Failures: `MaxHealsExceeded` (⇒ `stop{environment_lost}` through WS-F2), `ImageUnresolvable`, `PolicyDenied`.
- Detached children on resume: for every `detached_effect_ids[]` entry in `committed`, `reconcile_detached(child_effect_id) → observed | unknown | terminated` via the re-attached helper (`list_detached`, WS-E5); preserved children (`on_kernel_loss = preserve_until(ttl)`) have `ttl ≥ writer_lease.ttl + recovery_grace` (OQ-252 answer, §6.5).

### 6.3 The recovery table (what `restore` does per open scope — normative)

| open scope (ADR-0042 kinds) / phase | recovery action | ledger record | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `effect` in `intended` (no decision) | keep; decision proceeds after resume | none | H7: nothing to replay |
| `effect` `authorized` (not `prepared`) | keep; `prepare` proceeds | none | approval survives resume iff its handle lifetime permits (OQ-134; default `run`) |
| `effect` `prepared` (not `committed`) | drop to `intended`? **No** — record `action.effect.unknown{cause: worker_lost}` only if the class is not `read_only`; for `read_only` simply re-prepare | `action.effect.unknown` (non-`read_only`) | `prepared` acquired resource keys/baselines; releasing them is ledgered by B2 `prepare` re-run |
| `effect` `committed`, no terminal | **`action.effect.unknown{cause: worker_lost, attempt_no}`** then `probe` per ADR-0031 class table; `read_only` ⇒ redispatch; `reversible` ⇒ probe vs `baseline_ref` else revert-and-redo; `compensable` ⇒ probe postcondition, retry same key only on `not_applied`; `irreversible` ⇒ never redispatch, escalate | `unknown` → `probed` → terminal | the phase-1 §6.9 rule; executor `resume`/read-back via helper session (WS-E5 AC-E5-09) |
| `effect` `observed` durable but budget charge missing | re-post charges (`charge` idempotent on `(source_event, dimension)`, ADR-0039) | `control.budget.consumed` | AC-L2-6 |
| `effect` `unknown`/`probed(undeterminable)` before crash | continue the probe policy; `probe_count` from the ledger | as B2 | never reset counters from memory |
| detached child effect `committed` | `reconcile_detached` → `observed | unknown | terminated` | child effect terminal | E5 §6.5 |
| `model_call` open (requested, no completed/failed) | `model.call.failed{error_class: worker_lost}`; retry as a new `model_attempt` under `RetryPolicy` (same `model_call_id`) | `model.call.failed`, `control.retry.scheduled` | partial stream was ephemeral; surfaces rewind (R6) |
| `context_assembly` open | discard; redone by the next decision (pure) | none | ADR-0019/D1 |
| `compaction` open | discard; strategy may re-request | `context.compaction.started` stays unpaired → force-closed `abandoned` by F2 INV-1 | compaction is not an external effect |
| `tool_call` open with no effect intents parsed | re-parse from the durable `model.call.completed.response_ref`; `effect_id` derivation is deterministic (ADR-0027) so no duplicate intent | none or `action.effect.intended` | the reason `effect_id` is derived |
| `permission` `pending` (async) | keep pending; if `deadline` passed ⇒ `refused` (H7 AC-H7-06); re-bind the `permission_decided` subscription | none / `security.permission.decided{timed_out}` | never `unknown` |
| `subagent` open (child running under its own lease) | keep; parent subscribes `child_terminal`; if the child's lease has expired, the child restores independently (its own writer lease) | none | F3 owns merge; parent waits ≤ `TimeoutPolicy[subagent]` |
| `validation` open | re-run (validators are instrument-charged, idempotent, read-only) | new `verification.validator.invoked` | G1 |
| `environment_op` open (attach/snapshot) | `verify_environment`; incomplete snapshot ⇒ `unknown` + re-snapshot | `action.environment.verified` | B5 |
| `wakeup` claimed but not fired | re-fire from the claim (idempotent by key) | `control.wakeup.fired` or `skipped{already_claimed}` | W-1 |
| `turn` open, `control.decision{stop}` already appended | resume the drain (F2 stop protocol) | as F2 | AC-F2-08 |
| any scope past its deadline | force-close per F2 INV-1 | `control.timeout.fired` | OQ-119: default deadline = budget wall-time ceiling + manifest grace (confirms OQ-174) |

The table is the payload schema of `RecoveryAction` and the oracle for AC-B3-01. `recovery_decision.action` summarises it: `continue` (no open non-`read_only` effects), `resolve_unknowns` (≥1 `unknown` emitted), `suspend` (a pending permission or subscription is the only open item), `stop{reason}` (definition unresolvable, `MaxHealsExceeded`, budget exhausted).

### 6.4 Data-model sketch

`RunState ∈ {created, active, suspended, finished}` (derived view over `lifecycle.*`; `recovering` never durable) · `Checkpoint{run_id, seq, head_hash, control_state_ref, effect_summary[], open_scopes[], env_handles[], subscriptions[], budget{…}, resume_set_heads[], pending_permissions[], view_hash}` · `RecoveryPlan{lease, from_seq, recovery_decision{last_durable_phase, action, cause}, actions: [RecoveryAction]}` · `Lease{lease_id, scope: LeaseScope, holder: HolderIdentity{host, pid?, start_id?, participant_ref, instance_id}, generation, acquired_at, expires_at, progress?: ContentAddress}` · `WakeupSubscription{subscription_id, owner: RunId | GoalRef, trigger: Trigger, policy: WakeupPolicy, created_by: EventRef, state ∈ {active, paused, cancelled, exhausted}, next_due?: derived}` · `Occurrence{subscription_id, occurrence_key: Text | ContentAddress (the rule: timers = ISO instant; schedules = scheduled instant; permission = permission_id; child/effect = the terminal EventRef; external = source-declared delivery id, else `H(canonical(payload))`), payload_ref, observed_at, provenance}` · `HealingPolicy{on_lost, max_heals, verify_after?, preserve_detached}` · `EnvironmentVerdict` · `SuspendReason` · manifest additions: `attendance` (WS-H7 term, adopted), `activation_no`, `continued_from?`, `lease_ttl{writer, environment, wakeup_claim}`, `healing_policy_ref`, `envelope_policy_ref` (WS-F2). Event classes: existing `lifecycle.run.resumed`, `lifecycle.lease.*`, `control.wakeup.scheduled/fired`, `control.retry.scheduled` (F2-extended), `action.environment.attached/detached/snapshot`; **new** (proposed to WS-B1 via CF-280): `lifecycle.run.suspended`, `lifecycle.run.continued` (or `lifecycle.run.created.continued_from`), `control.wakeup.occurred`, `control.wakeup.skipped`, `control.wakeup.cancelled`, `action.environment.verified`, `action.environment.healed`. All `durability = ledger`; `lifecycle.run.resumed/suspended`, `control.wakeup.fired`, `action.environment.healed` are audit-grade (WS-H6). Every string-shaped construct is typed or a `Text` leaf (ADR-0015); external occurrence payloads are `Text{authority: external}`.

### 6.5 Answers to inherited open questions

- **OQ-252 (preserved children vs recovery window):** `preserve_until(ttl)` with `ttl ≥ writer_lease.ttl + recovery_grace` (manifest constants; defaults 45 s / 60 s in the same spirit as the OpenHands precedent, stated as constants not code) so a takeover always finds preserved children alive; when no resume arrives before `ttl`, the helper terminates them and the next `restore` records `terminated` for each; the child effects' terminals are written by the resuming writer, never by the helper (executors report, never decide — WS-E5 I-2).
- **OQ-095 (hibernation minutes):** a `suspended{hibernated}` run's environment lease keeps the handle in `env.reserved_ms` only; `env.active_ms` stops at `lifecycle.run.suspended`; a third `env.hibernated_ms` counter is *not* introduced — hibernation is `reserved_ms` with `state = hibernated` on `action.environment.detached/attached` payloads, which the metering formula may weight (WS-L2 owns the formula).
- **OQ-119 / OQ-174 (scope deadline default):** confirmed with WS-F2: the default deadline for `scopes_closed` is the run's hard `time.wall_ms` ceiling plus a manifest `grace_ms`; F2 INV-1 force-closes at resume; H6 reports `scopes_closed = false` only past that.
- **OQ-081 (HLC):** optional at C0; **required** on `control.wakeup.occurred` and on every event of a run with `activation_no > 1` or with children, so cross-run ordering of wakeups and continuations is consistent-snapshot-capable; the clock-skew assumption is ADR-0030's.
- **OQ-090 (detached ownership):** as WS-E5 §6.5 with the ttl rule above; B3 adds only the resume-time reconciliation.
- **OQ-179 (compensation plan before `defer`):** yes — `suspend` invariant S-1 requires no `prepared`/`committed` effect open, so the plan is registered when `prepare` runs *after* resume; nothing is lost by deferring registration.
- **OQ-134 (handle lifetimes across resume/fork):** default `run` scope survives `restore` (same run) and is re-validated at `authorize`; it does **not** survive `continue_goal` or `fork` unless the grant declares `session` scope (H1/H7 decide the declaration).

### 6.6 Acceptance-criteria sketch (the failure-injection battery; one battery serves B1/B2/B3/E5/F2/G2/L2)

**Kill-point matrix (KP).** Injected process death of the runtime at: KP-1 after `action.effect.intended` before `security.permission.decided`; KP-2 after `decided{allow}` before `prepared`; KP-3 after `prepared` before `committed`; KP-4 after `committed` durable before executor dispatch; KP-5 during execution (executor alive, runtime dead); KP-6 after execution before `observed` is durable (outcome lost); KP-7 after `observed` durable before it is visible/charged; KP-8 after visibility before the next `control.decision`; KP-9 mid-batch `append` (torn tail); KP-10 after `lifecycle.run.suspended` before `lease.released`; KP-11 after `control.wakeup.occurred` before `fired`; KP-12 during `heal` (between `committed` reprovision and `attached`); KP-13 during `restore` itself (after `lifecycle.run.resumed`, before all `RecoveryAction`s); KP-14 in a child run while the parent is active; KP-15 executor/helper death with the runtime alive (WS-E5 AC-E5-02 mirror). Each with a **probe tool per risk class** (read-only counter read; reversible file edit with baseline; compensable external counter with `dedup_support ∈ {executor, none}`; irreversible external counter).

- **AC-B3-01 Resolution per table.** For every (KP, class) cell the post-resume ledger matches the §6.3 recovery table exactly: the right `unknown`/`probe`/terminal sequence, one `lifecycle.run.resumed` with the expected `recovery_decision`, and `project(effect_ledger)` equal to the live view (AC-B2-06). (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-B3-02 Checkpoint equivalence.** `checkpoint(run)` from the raw prefix and from a materialised checkpoint blob yield equal `view_hash`; restoring from either yields byte-identical `RecoveryPlan.actions`. (C0/Stage 1)
- **AC-B3-03 Duplicate-effect veto.** Across the whole matrix the external counters read exactly 1 for compensable-with-dedup and irreversible tools, ≤ 1 + probes for reversible, and the class-scoped process metric `duplicate_effect_count` (a `veto: true` invariant, ADR-0047 "duplicate effect") is 0; any 2 fails the battery. (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-B3-04 Stale worker fenced.** A holder paused (signal-stop or hibernate) past ttl, taken over, then resumed: every append it attempts is `Fenced`, its executor `exec` is refused `NotCommitted`/`Fenced` (WS-E5), no effect has two `applied` observations (AC-B2-02 extended to environment and wakeup leases). (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-B3-05 Liveness shortening is safe.** With a dead same-host holder, takeover happens before `expires_at`; with a pid reused by an unrelated process, takeover is refused until expiry (start-id check); off-host holders wait for expiry. (C1/Stage 2)
- **AC-B3-06 Suspension invariant.** `suspend` with any effect in `prepared`/`committed` is refused `OpenCommittedEffects`; a resumed suspended run re-emits no effect phase after `intended` (H7 AC-H7-05 shared). (C1/Stage 2)
- **AC-B3-07 Wakeup idempotence.** Deliver the same occurrence N times (N ≥ 3) through two concurrent wakeup services: exactly one `fired`, N−1 `skipped{already_claimed | duplicate_occurrence}`; KP-11 yields exactly one `fired` after restart; an occurrence for a `finished` run is `skipped{run_finished}` and audited; timers and retries scheduled before a crash fire after restart within `ttl + grace` (Symphony §14.3 gap closed). (C1/Stage 2; external sources Stage 4)
- **AC-B3-08 Delivery at decision points.** A `follow_up` occurrence arriving during a `committed` effect is delivered only after that effect's terminal; a `steer` occurrence is delivered before the next model call; neither interrupts an effect (F2 INV-3 respected). (C1/Stage 2)
- **AC-B3-09 Environment verification and healing.** Destroy the workspace/helper between KP-8 and resume: `verify_environment = lost`; under `on_lost = reprovision` a ledgered reprovision effect runs at most `max_heals` times, a snapshot (if any) is restored, `action.environment.healed` names what was not restored and the model's next context contains that observation with `authority = environment`; under `interactive` attendance an `ask` is raised instead; past `max_heals` the run stops with `stop_reason = environment_lost`. (C1/Stage 2)
- **AC-B3-10 Continuation chain.** A goal with three activations: `continued_from` links verify (H6 cross-run rule 4), the goal's budget node totals equal the sum of activations, a goal-scoped subscription fires after activation 2 finishes and opens activation 3, a second continuation from the same head is `AlreadyContinued`. (C1/Stage 4)
- **AC-B3-11 Resume verification.** Changing the sealed definition between crash and resume: add-only ⇒ `lifecycle.definition.changed` and continue; other ⇒ `DefinitionChanged` refusal (ADR-0025). (C0/Stage 2)
- **AC-B3-12 Recovery time and waste as metrics.** `recovery_latency_ms` (from `lease.acquired` at takeover to `Cue.resumed` delivered), `wasted_calls` (model calls whose result was never consumed because of a crash), `unknown_effects_per_resume`, `heal_count` are process metrics declared per ADR-0045 with `requires_observability ⊇ {events}`, `applies_to_classes = {native}` (hosted rows `n/a{observability}` unless the participant declares `resume`, T-LCD-07/-15). (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-B3-13 Hosted mapping.** A hosted participant declaring `resume ∈ {declared, probed}` maps ACP `session/resume{replayFrom}` to `lifecycle.run.resumed{cause: wakeup | operator}` without adding a field to any native entity (T-LCD-06); one declaring `unknown` renders every recovery metric `n/a`, never 0 (T-LCD-15). (C2/Stage 4)

**T-LCD statement.** Satisfies T-LCD-06 (no IR entity gains a field for hosting; `resume` is a capability-declaration key), T-LCD-07 (`resume`/`interrupt` tri-state, never coerced), T-LCD-08 (`HealingPolicy` and `WakeupPolicy` are parameters of the definition; a control-strategy variant receives `Cue.resumed`/`Cue.woken` through its contract), T-LCD-12 (every operation above is implementable out-of-process; the wakeup service and ingress adapters *are* out-of-process), T-LCD-13 (the model-facing healing/progress rules are `HarnessRule`s whose delivery/activation/following are measured), T-LCD-15 (recovery metrics class-scoped). Not applicable: T-01/-02/-03/-04/-10/-11 (no model-facing surface or lowering is introduced by B3 itself; E4 owns the `control.wakeup.*` lowering); T-14 (no comparison is defined). ADR-0024 ladder: policies are MUST-data (`HealingPolicy`, `WakeupPolicy`, ttl constants); the recovery procedure, lease fencing and the claim service are MUST-code.

## 10. Confidence

**High** for the durability contract's core — checkpoint-as-view, phase-resolved restore, no re-execution/no resampling, generation-fenced leases with expiry and same-host liveness shortening, and the kill-point battery: they follow from ratified ADR-0026/0028/0030 and are corroborated by source read at pinned commits in Codex (daemon recovery through normal resume), OpenHands and prime-agent (leases), Symphony (reconciliation, blocked-vs-stalled) and by Managed Agents/Cursor write-ups; the only preprint (Logos) supplies shape, not numbers. **Medium** for the wakeup model and suspended runs: the subscription/occurrence/claim shape has two source precedents (prime-agent, goose) plus two vendor runtimes (Cloudflare, Temporal), but the durable `occurred` record with authority labels is `no-precedent`, prime-agent is a single-team system barely a month old (`provisional` as a system), and delivery-mode semantics (`steer`) interact with F1/D1 decisions still in flight. **Medium** for continuation chains and environment healing: Temporal's Continue-As-New and Symphony's continuation retry are strong precedents for the chain; healing under policy has only Managed Agents' recipe and Anthropic's init script as precedents and Cursor calls it future work — hence C1, default `ask` for interactive runs. **Low** on every quantitative claim (two nines, 50 M actions, 52-hour runs, Logos counts) — none is load-bearing. Reflexively: the contract assumes the ledger's durable-before-visible append and the executor's write-ahead gating hold; every guarantee above is conditional on those two ratified invariants.
