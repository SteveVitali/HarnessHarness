## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream produces **Ontology v1** for HarnessHarness: every entity, plane, property and relation that Canonical Spec §2 will use, with precise definitions and a formal model. It decides (a) the refined seven-plane model and what "plane" means structurally; (b) the partially-programmable-policy-stack formalism π_system = H[π_M] and the objective; (c) `validity` vs `compliance` as a *measured pair* with an event chain; (d) the `compatibility surface` as a response surface over configurations; (e) the formalization of the two participant classes from ADR-0001 with `comparison granularity` and `observability level`; (f) which concepts are first-class entities and which are derived views; and (g) the two items Phase 0 flagged to WS-A2 — CF-020 (capability declaration vs capability vector) and CF-021 (the `configuration` granularity enum value).

It explicitly leaves to neighbours: the **typed representation** of every entity named here (WS-A3 — terms are marked `typed-by: WS-A3`); the compiler and lowering semantics (WS-A4); component-class contracts and the code-vs-config boundary (WS-A5); the event taxonomy and ID model that will *carry* the artifact-delivery events defined here (WS-B1, WS-I1); the metric registry and scorecard that will *implement* class-scoped and compliance metrics (WS-I2, WS-J4); the budget vector's exact dimensions (WS-L2); provenance/authority label schemes (WS-L3); identity derivation (WS-L4); Hosting ABI depth (WS-J6, OQ-004); and every language/runtime question (WS-L1). Nothing here names a programming language, runtime, package ecosystem or framework; every contract is stated as operations/inputs/outputs/invariants/failure modes.

Binding inputs honoured: `research/synthesis/phase-0.md` §5 (positioning, novelty narrowing, LCD battery, vocabulary, hosting obligations), `registers/lcd-test-battery.md` (T-LCD-09/-13/-15 are the tests this dossier's abstractions must satisfy), ADR-0001…ADR-0011. The product name is **HarnessHarness** (ADR-0011).

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence (a failure mode exists / an architecture is implementable)

**F1 — Every industrial decomposition of a harness is a strict subset of doc 2's seven planes, and the two planes they omit are the same two.** Mapping each decomposition onto planes P1 (observation & context), P2 (action & tools), P3 (control & orchestration), P4 (verification & feedback), P5 (state & durability), P6 (security & governance), P7 (measurement & evolution):

| decomposition | covers | omits |
|---|---|---|
| AIOS kernel (S-030/S-031: scheduler, context, memory, storage, tool, access control) | P3, P1, P1/P5, P5, P2, P6 | **P4, P7** |
| LangChain anatomy (S-045: prompts; tools/skills/MCP; infrastructure; orchestration; hooks/middleware) | P1, P2, P5/P6(partial), P3, P3/P1 | **P4, P7**, P6 (only as "sandbox") |
| Cloudflare runtime/harness split (S-056) | runtime = P5, P3(scheduling), P7(observability only); harness = P1, P2, P3, P5 | **P4, P6**, P7-evolution |
| Harness-Bench definition (S-062: context, tools, state, constraints, permissions, tracing, recovery) | P1, P2, P5, P3/P6, P6, P7(tracing), P5 | **P4**, P7-evolution |

Verification (P4) is absent from every decomposition and measurement/evolution (P7) is present only as telemetry. This is mechanism evidence for two ontology decisions: the seven-plane model is a *superset* classification (nothing in the field contradicts it; it adds what the field under-represents), and P4/P7 must be stated as *planes of the harness* rather than "tooling around it" — otherwise the IR will inherit the industrial blind spot and the novelty claim (a) in ADR-0006 (an IR "spanning all seven planes") is unprovable. AIOS also gives the negative case for treating the kernel as the harness: its "access control" module is named in the paper but absent in source (WS-A1 F6) — a plane that exists only in a diagram.

**F2 — "Plane" must be a partition with a home-plane rule, not a layering; the model is a boundary, not a plane.** Doc 2 §4 says explicitly that seven planes are "a synthesis rather than a claim that the field has standardized". Two things follow from reading the decompositions above in source. First, the same mechanism recurs in different "layers" of different systems (memory is P1 for AIOS's context injector and P5 for OpenHands' event log; compaction is a P1 policy with a P5 event `CondensationSummaryEvent`), so a plane cannot be an architectural layer with a dependency direction. It is a partition of *responsibilities* over the harness parameterization θ: every entity, component class and event has exactly one **home plane** (the plane whose core question it answers) and may reference other planes only through typed edges. Second, doc 3 §2.3 introduces a "Model plane" (gateway, router, Profile Compiler, caching) that doc 2 does not have. In source the corresponding objects sit *at the boundary* between harness and model: AIOS routes model calls through `LLMAdapter` behind a syscall queue; Cloudflare puts "model execution — the call to … provider" inside the harness; Omnigent's `Executor.run_turn(messages, tools, system_prompt)` is the whole model boundary as one operation. The ontology therefore defines the **model boundary** (M | H) and the **environment boundary** (H | E) as first-class *boundaries* with **boundary components** (gateway, router, Profile Compiler; sandbox/environment handle), leaving the plane count at seven and resolving the doc 3 §2.3 naming (CF flagged, §8). The cross-cutting properties (resource economics, provenance, model conditioning) remain *attributes on every entity and event*, never planes.

**F3 — The "moving boundary of control" has a precise object: a partition of decision points.** Doc 2 §9 names it the field's most distinctive research object but leaves it informal. Source gives it a shape. ATIF (S-132 `step.py`) marks a step with `llm_call_count == 0` as "a deterministic (non-LLM) dispatch"; Omnigent's `Executor.handles_tools_internally()` decides whether tool execution is the harness's or the model's loop; OpenHands' `ActionEvent.security_risk` + confirmation policy decides whether a human or code authorizes; AIOS's scheduler decides *when* a model call happens. Each is a **decision point** δ (next action, continue/stop, retrieve/compact, delegate, authorize, verify, retry) assigned to an **owner** ∈ {code, model, human}. The ontology defines the **control boundary** β : Δ → {code, model, human} as a component of θ. This makes "move the boundary only when evaluation shows a benefit" (doc 2 §4) an experiment over a typed factor (WS-F1/F2 vary β; WS-I7 attributes to it) and makes T-LCD-09 factorial designs able to name it.

**F4 — Validity and compliance separate cleanly in one paper and decompose further in another; both are implementable as events.** S-077 gives the algebra: "realized savings = validity × compliance(m, p, a)", with validity decided by the protocol (version stamps and cacheability hints; identical across seven models and three serving paths; zero contract failures in ~9,400 episodes) and compliance decided by the model (first-try application of identical wire bytes ranging from 3.7 % to 100 % across models). S-088 splits compliance into two model-conditioned stages with their own metrics: **activation** (Skill-Load Rate — "the fraction of its trajectories in which it actively loads at least one skill into its context"; computed from trajectories, i.e. deterministic) and **following** (Harness-Following Rate — LLM-judged; plus a phase-level adherence score). Weak-tier models fail at both stages (SLR 0.251 vs ~0.96; HFR 0.142 vs 0.757). Two independent preprints therefore support the same three-event chain the LCD battery already names (T-LCD-13: *delivered / activated / followed*), and existing runtimes already record the first two deterministically: OpenHands `SystemPromptEvent{system_prompt, tools, dynamic_context}` and `MessageEvent.activated_skills` (skills triggered by keyword/path match), AIOS `ContextInjector` "Injected N memories" log lines. **Following** is the only stage without a deterministic precedent; it is deterministic only when the artifact carries an executable check (e.g. a rule whose satisfaction is visible in the ledger) and judged otherwise. The ontology must therefore carry a **detector provenance** `{deterministic, judged}` on `activated` and `followed` so a judged compliance number never masquerades as a protocol-layer validity fact (mechanism: doc 2 §8 warns LLM judges "can be correlated with the same failure modes as the tested agent").

**F5 — The compatibility surface is a response surface in the textbook sense, and the evidence says its interesting terms are interactions on mostly categorical axes.** Response surface methodology (S-WS-A2-01; Box & Wilson 1951) fits a low-order model of a response over factors using designed (factorial, central-composite) experiments, and "the interaction among process variables can be determined by statistical techniques" — exactly doc 2 §1's demand for "interaction effects, not single-axis leaderboards". The evidence about *which* factors matter is consistent across sources: model family/version (S-062 configuration-level reporting; S-088 tier non-monotonicity; S-077 per-model compliance), budget / context window (S-072: a context-policy effect present at 20k tokens vanishes at wide windows — the one clearly *continuous* axis), task distribution and environment (S-135 nine benchmarks × nine models), and harness coordinates (component variant, profile, control boundary). Most axes are categorical (model versions are not ordinal), so the "surface" is a factorial-effects model with interaction terms over categorical factors and a curve only along budget-like axes. The ontology should define the surface abstractly (a mapping from factor space to a *distribution* of the paired harness effect) and leave model form to WS-J4 (§7).

**F6 — The two participant classes are distinguished by the *transparency of H*, not by observability alone; observability is an orthogonal level.** In Harbor, Omnigent and Inspect (WS-A1 F2) the same host can see very different things about a participant depending on mechanism — but in none of them does the host *know the transformation H* the participant applies to the model. For a native participant, H is known by construction: H = compile(h, p) for a Harness Definition h and Model Profile p, so θ is fully exposed and component-level variation is meaningful. For a hosted participant, H is unknown; the lab observes only the ABI projection of its behaviour and can vary only ABI-settable coordinates. This yields the ontology's formal distinction (§6.2): **native ⇔ θ is a Harness Definition in HIR; hosted ⇔ θ is a product version plus ABI-settable coordinates.** Omnigent's `reconcile` and `HarnessCapabilities` (`None` ⇒ `UNKNOWN`), Harbor's frozen `AgentCapabilities`, and ACP's negotiation rule ("omitted … UNSUPPORTED") together show that *declared* and *observed* capability are different objects — which settles CF-020: keep **capability declaration** (claimed record, immutable per run) and **capability vector** (derived `{declared, probed, unknown}`), and record their relation as `capability_vector = reconcile(capability_declaration, probes)`.

**F7 — The ontology needs two levels.** Everything in F1–F4 is about *one harness* (object level). Participants, configurations, arms, runs, metrics, surfaces and conformance records are about *the instrument that compares harnesses* (instrument level). Harbor `JobConfig{agents[], tasks[], n_attempts}` and HAL's runner (WS-A1 §4c) live entirely at the instrument level and know nothing about planes; the AIOS kernel lives entirely at the object level and knows nothing about experiments. Collapsing the two (as the phrase "meta-harness" tempts one to do) is how the product-name collision CF-013 arose. Ontology v1 states the levels explicitly and places every term in exactly one.

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

**Against the seven-plane model as *the* decomposition.** No source uses exactly seven; AIOS uses five kernel services, LangChain five categories, Cloudflare a two-way split, Harness-Bench a seven-item list that differs from doc 2's, and the categorical-architecture preprint (S-WS-A2-03) uses four pillars (memory, skills, protocols, harness). Memory straddles P1 and P5; compaction straddles P1 and P5; permissions straddle P2 and P6. *Why this does not overturn:* the recommendation does not claim the planes are architectural layers or that the field agrees on them; it claims they are a complete classification of harness responsibilities (F1 shows no decomposition names a responsibility outside them) and makes the straddling harmless by the home-plane rule plus typed cross-plane edges. The spec's dependency DAG is by tier (C0–C4), never by plane. The residual risk — that a future mechanism has no natural home plane — is recorded as an open question (§7) with the rule that a new plane requires an ADR, not an eighth column added ad hoc.

**Against π_system = H[π_M] as a formal model.** It is a description, not a computable object: π_M is nonstationary (provider-side model updates change behaviour without a version bump — S-047's "dead weight" story is one instance), stochastic under sampling parameters the harness may not control, and a harness may bind several models (router, subagents, judges) so "the" model policy is a set. The options framework (S-WS-A2-02) formalizes temporal abstraction over a *fixed* base MDP, which agent harnesses violate. *Why this does not overturn:* the formalism's job in the spec is to fix *what is held constant and what is varied* — configuration pins model identity (provider snapshot) and sampling parameters as factors; multi-model binding becomes a model-*set* coordinate (OQ raised, §7); the ontology says "approximately" and never asks the runtime to compute π_system. Its value is that it makes harness edits policy interventions with named decision points (F3), which is what the lab must vary and attribute.

**Against validity/compliance as a clean pair.** (i) In S-088 following is *judged*; judged compliance inherits judge weaknesses and can correlate with the agent's own failures (doc 2 §8). (ii) Validity is not always decidable: a cached recovery suggestion has a version stamp (S-077), but a prose memory "the tests live in `/spec`" has no oracle unless the harness supplies one; doc 2 §5.1's "deterministic at the protocol layer" holds only for artifacts that carry validators or version stamps. (iii) Activation is artifact-kind-specific: a skill load is observable, an instruction inside a system prompt has no activation signal distinct from delivery, so the chain collapses to delivered → followed for some kinds. (iv) S-077's own compliance result flips sign across versions of one family (Sonnet 4.6 88.9 % vs Sonnet 5 11.1 %, attributed to "input-schema conservatism"), so a compliance number is tied to a model *version*, not a family. *Why this does not overturn:* every one of these is absorbed by making the pair *measured* rather than asserted — validity takes a third value `unknown` when no protocol-layer check exists; `activated`/`followed` events carry `detector ∈ {deterministic, judged}` and a confidence; artifact kinds declare `activation_observable`; compliance metrics are class-scoped (`requires_observability` = `events` for deterministic stages, `model_io` for judged stages) and keyed by model version through the configuration. The pair is still worth having because S-077 shows validity *can* be made deterministic where the artifact is designed for it, which is precisely what a memory-lifecycle plane (WS-D4) should require.

**Against the compatibility surface as a response surface.** RSM assumes continuous factors and low-order polynomial response; model family/version, environment image and task family are categorical, and model versions churn faster than a surface can be fitted (S-096: retraining the model broke the harness tuned around it; S-047). A fitted surface could be stale before it is published. *Why this does not overturn:* the ontology defines the surface as a mapping to a *distribution of paired effects* over a mixed factor space, leaves model form to WS-J4, and requires every reported surface to carry its design, sample sizes, confidence intervals and the configuration ids it was fitted from — a *derived view with provenance*, not a truth. Staleness is exactly what assumption-debt records and re-probe cadence (OQ-034, WS-I6) exist to manage; the surface inherits the same expiry discipline (T-LCD-05 applied reflexively).

**Against a strict two-class dichotomy.** Model-boundary interception (Inspect, ClawGym II; OQ-038) yields complete model I/O for a participant whose H is unknown — a "grey box". And a native harness exposed through the Hosting ABI to an external plane (ADR-0005 "act as a participant") looks, from outside, like a hosted participant. *Why this does not overturn:* the class is defined by transparency of H (is θ a Harness Definition in HIR?), observability is an orthogonal level; grey-box is `hosted` with `observability_level ⊇ {model_io}`, and a native harness run through the ABI is still `native` for the lab that owns its definition and `hosted` for a lab that does not. The ontology states this explicitly so that class is never inferred from mechanism.

**Against the two-level split.** The evolution service (C4) edits the harness using the instrument — the object level modifies itself through the instrument level, so the levels are not independent; S-147 ("The Last Harness You'll Ever Build") argues the instrument is the harness. *Why this does not overturn:* ADR-0002 already separates proposal (a P7 component that *calls* the instrument) from deployment (governance), which is only expressible if the levels are distinct; and S-147 is `provisional` and contradicted at matched budget by S-083/S-075/S-091 (CF-004).

**Negative results from the audit.** (i) No audited runtime records a *followed* event; every compliance-following signal in the literature is judged or post-hoc — the third link of T-LCD-13 has no source-code precedent and is the ontology's main unproven obligation on WS-B1/WS-G1. (ii) No audited system represents the control boundary as data; ATIF's `llm_call_count == 0` is the closest and is a trajectory annotation, not a definition-level factor. (iii) Grepping AIOS for any verifier/validator/critic concept in the kernel finds none (`aios/` has no `verif*`/`valid*` module), confirming F1's "P4 absent" for the one system that calls itself an operating system for agents.

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

The complete Ontology v1 text is at `research/registers/ontology-v1-proposal.md` (a drop-in replacement candidate for `ontology.md` §1–§4, keeping every v0/v0.1 term and its status unless superseded with rationale). This section summarises the decisions, contracts, data model, acceptance criteria and staging.

### 6.1 Structural decisions

1. **Two levels.** *Object level*: a harness, its seven planes, its entities, artifacts, components and the policy stack. *Instrument level*: the Harness Lab, participants, configurations, arms, runs, metrics, conformance records, surfaces. Every term in the register carries a `level` field. The reference runtime realises the object level; the Lab realises the instrument level; the Hosting ABI is an instrument-level boundary.
2. **Planes are a partition with a home-plane rule.** P1–P7 as in v0 (names unchanged). Every IR entity (`typed-by: WS-A3`), component class (WS-A5) and event family (WS-B1) declares exactly one `home_plane`; cross-plane relations are typed edges. Planes carry no dependency direction; the build DAG is by tier. Adding a plane requires an ADR.
3. **Two boundaries, not an eighth plane.** The **model boundary** (M | H) and the **environment boundary** (H | E) are first-class; **boundary components** (model gateway, router, Profile Compiler, cache; environment/sandbox handle) have a home *boundary* instead of a home plane. Doc 3 §2.3's "Model plane" is a scope-catalogue grouping of model-boundary components (CF, §8). The three cross-cutting properties are mandatory *attributes* of every entity and event: `provenance{origin, version, authority_class}` (scheme: WS-L3), `resource` (accounting; vector dims: WS-L2), `conditioning{semantic_identity, surface_owner}` (WS-A3/C3).
4. **Control boundary β is a typed element of θ.** Decision points Δ = {next_action, continue_or_stop, retrieve_or_compact, delegate, authorize, verify, retry, escalate} (extensible by ADR); β : Δ → {code, model, human} with per-decision-point guards. β is a factor at the instrument level (comparison granularity `component-level` for native participants) and an observable in trajectories (a step whose owner is `code` has no model call).
5. **First-class vs derived.** First-class (identified, versioned, stored): Harness Definition, Model Profile, component variant, harness artifact (instruction/rule, memory, tool surface, procedure/skill, observation), Budget, Configuration, Arm, Run, ledger Event, Participant descriptor, capability declaration, ConformanceRecord, MetricDeclaration, AssumptionDebtRecord, environment image, task/suite, and *reports* (LcdReport, lowering loss report, fitted-surface report — computed once, then persisted with provenance). Derived views (never stored as truth, always recomputable from first-class objects): π_system, the compatibility surface, capability vector, scorecard values, compliance rates, opacity ratio, comparison granularity (a property of a factor), planes (a classification), cross-cutting properties (attributes).
6. **CF-020 confirmed, CF-021 renamed.** `capability declaration` and `capability vector` stay distinct with the relation `vector = reconcile(declaration, probes)`. The comparison-granularity enum becomes `{component-level, configuration-level, product-level}` (the v0.1 `configuration` value is renamed `configuration-level`; the noun *configuration* keeps its single meaning — a factorial point).

### 6.2 Formal model (for Spec §2.3)

- **Objects.** M — a model snapshot (provider, version, sampling parameters); π_M(a | c) — the stochastic policy it induces over rendered contexts c. E — an environment with state s and an effect semantics. D — a task distribution. B ∈ ℝ^k — a budget vector over the WS-L2 resource dimensions (tokens, calls, wall-clock, environment-minutes, network, fan-out, approvals). θ = (h, p, β, π_pol) — a Harness Definition h ∈ HIR, a Model Profile p, a control boundary β, and policies (permission, budget, retry, stopping) that h references.
- **Plane operators.** One harness step is the composition, around the model boundary, of plane-owned operations: `render` (P1: σ_t ↦ c_t, consuming provenance-labelled context items under budget), `propose` (model boundary: a_t ~ π_M(· | c_t) when β(next_action) = model, else a deterministic choice), `interpret` (P2: parse/validate a_t into a typed action), `authorize` (P6: confer or refuse authority out-of-band from text), `execute` (P2 → environment boundary: effect on E, captured), `verify` (P4: validators/oracles reconcile claims with state), `persist` (P5: append events, checkpoint), `decide` (P3: continue / stop / retry / delegate under the control envelope), with `observe` (P7) recording every step. **π_system = H_{θ,E,B}[π_M]** is the closed-loop policy over environment states induced by iterating this step; it is *partially programmable* because β assigns each decision point to code, model or human. (Lineage, not load-bearing: temporal abstraction over a base policy, S-WS-A2-02.)
- **Objective.** J(θ; M, E, D, B) = E_{τ ∼ π_system}[U(success, quality) − λ_c C(τ) − λ_l L(τ) − λ_r R(τ)] subject to budget (c(τ) ≤ B), safety and permission constraints — unchanged from v0. The **harness effect** is the *paired* quantity Δ(θ, θ₀ | M, E, D, B) = J(θ) − J(θ₀) at matched B (T-LCD-14); never an absolute score.
- **Configuration.** κ = (M, h, p, E, B, seed) (ratified v0.1); the ledger keys results by κ. An **arm** is a set of κ under one hypothesis with matched B.
- **Compatibility surface.** Ψ_θ : F → Dist(Δ), where F is the factor space {model snapshot, task distribution, environment image, budget vector, context window, tool-surface target, control boundary, …} and the value is the distribution of the paired effect. It is a **derived view** estimated from runs by a designed experiment; a *fitted-surface report* records design, factor levels, sample sizes, model form (WS-J4), interaction terms, confidence intervals and the κ ids used. **Portability** of θ = sign-stability of Ψ_θ across the model axis; **conditionality** = the region of F where Δ > 0 with stated confidence; unprobed factor levels are `unknown` (T-LCD-07), never interpolated across categorical axes.
- **Validity vs compliance (measured pair).** For a harness artifact α delivered in c_t to model M: `validity(α) ∈ {valid, invalid, unknown}` is decided by a protocol-layer check that does not consult M (version stamp, validator, oracle; `unknown` when none exists). Compliance is a chain of model-conditioned conditional events: `activated(α)` (the model used/loaded/retrieved α: deterministic for skills, retrievals, tool calls; judged or not observable for prose instructions) and `followed(α)` (behaviour consistent with α: deterministic when α has an executable check, judged otherwise). Realized benefit decomposes as E[Δ] ≈ P(valid) · P(activated | delivered, valid) · P(followed | activated) · E[Δ | followed] (S-077 algebra, S-088 stages). Every `activated`/`followed` event carries `detector ∈ {deterministic, judged}`, `detector_ref` (validator id or judge configuration, with provenance) and `confidence`. A compliance metric is class-scoped: `requires_observability = {events}` for deterministic stages and `{model_io}` for judged stages; unavailable ⇒ `n/a`.
- **Participants.** A participant P = (class ∈ {native, hosted}, hosting_mechanism ∈ {none, session-abi, model-boundary-intercept, container-installed}, capability_declaration, version_identity, observability_level ⊆ {events, model_io, end_state, ledger}, model_binding ∈ {bound(M-set), self-selected}). **Class criterion:** P is *native* iff its θ is a Harness Definition in HIR known to the Lab (H is transparent: H = compile(h, p)); otherwise *hosted* (H is opaque; θ_hosted = product version + ABI-settable coordinates). **Admissible granularities:** native ⊇ {component-level, configuration-level, product-level}; hosted ⊆ {configuration-level (only coordinates whose capability-vector entry is SUPPORTED), product-level}. **Projection invariant:** proj_ABI : native ledger → minimum observable event set is total (a native run can always be presented as a hosted run; ADR-0005), and there is no edge from any HIR entity to the Hosting ABI (T-LCD-06). Class is declared, never inferred from mechanism; observability is a level, never a class.

### 6.3 Interface-contract sketch (language-agnostic)

- `classify_home_plane(entity_or_component_or_event) → plane ∈ {P1..P7} | boundary ∈ {model, environment}` — total on every registered kind; failure `UnclassifiedKind` is a schema error at IR validation (the reflexive LCD check that nothing hides "between planes").
- `deliver(run, artifact_id, surface_ref, position) → artifact.delivered{artifact_id, version, kind, activation_observable, provenance}` — emitted by the context builder for every artifact placed in c_t; invariant: one event per artifact per model call; failure `UndeliverableArtifact` if the artifact lacks identity/provenance.
- `detect_activation(run, artifact_id) → artifact.activated{detector, detector_ref, confidence}` — deterministic detectors are components of P1/P2 (skill load, retrieval injection, tool call naming the artifact); judged detectors are P4 components with their own provenance; invariant: never emitted for artifacts with `activation_observable = false`.
- `detect_following(run, artifact_id, window) → artifact.followed{detector, detector_ref, confidence, evidence_refs}` — deterministic only via a validator the artifact references (`validates` edge); otherwise judged; failure `NoDetector` ⇒ metric `n/a`.
- `validity(artifact_id, at: event_id) → {valid, invalid, unknown, check_ref}` — protocol-layer, model-free; invariant: identical result for the same artifact version and check across all configurations (S-077 property, testable).
- `describe(participant) → ParticipantDescriptor` (fields above) — immutable per run; failure `ClassUndeclared` (class is never inferred).
- `admissible_granularities(participant) → set` — derived from class and capability vector; the experiment engine refuses a factor at a granularity outside the set (`InadmissibleFactor`), reports `n/a` never 0 (T-LCD-15).
- `paired_effect(arm_a, arm_b, metric) → Dist(Δ)` — refuses unequal budget vectors (`UnmatchedBudget`, T-LCD-14).
- `fit_surface(runs[], factors[], metric, model_form) → FittedSurfaceReport` — refuses to interpolate across categorical factor levels not present in `runs` (`UnknownLevel`); output carries κ ids, design, CIs.

### 6.4 Data-model sketch

- `Plane{id ∈ P1..P7, core_question, characteristic_failures[]}`; `Boundary{id ∈ {model, environment}}`; every `IREntityKind`, `ComponentClass`, `EventFamily` has `home: Plane | Boundary`.
- `ControlBoundary{assignments: map<DecisionPoint, {code, model, human}>, guards: map<DecisionPoint, Predicate>}` — element of a Harness Definition (`typed-by: WS-A3`); varied as a `component-level` factor.
- `HarnessArtifact{artifact_id (content-addressed semantic identity, WS-L4), kind ∈ {instruction, rule, memory, tool_surface, procedure, observation}, version, provenance, validity_check_ref?, activation_observable: bool}` (`typed-by: WS-A3`).
- Events (`typed-by: WS-B1`, home P7): `artifact.delivered`, `artifact.activated`, `artifact.followed` as above, each with `participant_class`, `observability_level`, `configuration_id`.
- `ParticipantDescriptor` (fields in §6.2), `CapabilityDeclaration` (claimed; WS-J6), `ConformanceRecord{participant_version, dimension, declared, observed, verdict, probe_run_id}`, `CapabilityVector` (derived).
- `Configuration{id, model_snapshot, harness_definition_ref, profile_ref, environment_image, budget_vector, seed}`; `Arm{id, hypothesis, configurations[], search_budget, eval_budget}`.
- `MetricDeclaration{name, requires_observability ⊆ {events, model_io, end_state, ledger}, applies_to_classes ⊆ {native, hosted}, admissible_granularities, detector_classes_allowed ⊆ {deterministic, judged}}` — the last field is new: a compliance metric declares whether judged detections may contribute.
- `FittedSurfaceReport{metric, factors[], levels, design, model_form, sample_sizes, estimates_with_ci, interaction_terms, configuration_ids[], fitted_at, expiry_condition}`.

### 6.5 Acceptance-criteria sketch (Spec §2 and downstream)

- AC-A2-1: Spec §2 contains the two-level statement, the seven planes with core questions, the two boundaries, the three cross-cutting attributes, and a `home` assignment for every entity kind, component class and event family named anywhere in the spec (a table; a missing row fails readiness).
- AC-A2-2: The formal model appears with the definitions in §6.2; every symbol used elsewhere in the spec (κ, B, β, Δ, Ψ) resolves to §2.
- AC-A2-3 (T-LCD-13): the event taxonomy (WS-B1) contains `artifact.delivered/activated/followed` with `detector` provenance; a compliance metric per profile is computable from ledger events alone for deterministic stages.
- AC-A2-4 (T-LCD-15): every metric in the registry carries `applies_to_classes` and `requires_observability`; a mixed-class comparison renders `n/a` for inadmissible cells; a test fixture with one native and one hosted participant demonstrates it.
- AC-A2-5 (T-LCD-09): a configuration record has all six factors explicit and β is representable as a factor; the canned analysis "does variant V help M₁ more than M₂?" is expressible against the data model.
- AC-A2-6 (T-LCD-07/-06): `admissible_granularities` never coerces `unknown`; the spec DAG shows no edge from HIR entities to the Hosting ABI while `proj_ABI` is specified as total on native ledgers.
- AC-A2-7: the glossary check (ADR-0008 rule 4) passes with the v1 register: no unregistered synonym, no "meta-" sub-concept, "HarnessHarness" only as the proper noun.

### 6.6 Build-stage suggestion

C0 / **Stage 1**: the ontology text (Spec §2), the `home` classification table, the configuration tuple, the participant descriptor, the artifact-delivery event *schema*, `MetricDeclaration` fields (all cheap schema constraints, per ADR-0004/0007). C0 / **Stage 3** (evaluation-first): deterministic `activated` detectors and compliance metrics on the reference harness; `paired_effect` with the budget precondition. C2 / **Stage 3–4**: judged detectors (with WS-G3), fitted-surface reports (WS-J4), hosted-participant descriptors (WS-J6). The control boundary β must be *representable* at Stage 1 and *variable* at Stage 3 (WS-F1).

### 6.7 Dependencies on other workstreams

WS-A3 types every entity marked `typed-by: WS-A3` (HarnessArtifact, ControlBoundary, the plane `home` attribute) and answers OQ-040/041; WS-A5 gives component classes their `home` plane and passes the Model Profile and resource accounting through every contract (T-LCD-08); WS-B1/WS-I1 carry the three artifact events with `participant_class`/`observability_level`; WS-I2/WS-J4 implement class-scoped compliance metrics and the surface fit; WS-L2 fixes the budget-vector dimensions; WS-L3 fixes the provenance/authority scheme the `provenance` attribute references; WS-L4 fixes content-addressed identity for artifacts; WS-G1/G3 own deterministic and judged `followed` detectors; WS-F1/F2 vary β; WS-J6 owns the Hosting ABI and `proj_ABI`; WS-D4 makes validity checks mandatory for memory artifacts.

## 10. Confidence

**High** for the structural decisions (two levels, planes as a partition with home-plane rule, boundaries not planes, first-class vs derived): they rest on source audits at pinned commits and Tier-B production decompositions, and on the observation that every industrial decomposition is a subset of the seven planes. **High** for CF-020/CF-021 dispositions (vocabulary; precedent in Omnigent/Harbor/ACP source). **Medium-high** for the validity/compliance formalization: the algebra and the two compliance stages come from two independent `provisional` preprints, but the *events* have Tier-B source precedents for the first two stages and the C0 content is a schema, not a performance claim; the third stage (`followed`) has no source-code precedent and is the main risk. **Medium** for the compatibility surface: the RSM lineage is Tier A, but the factor space is mostly categorical and the staleness problem is real; hence it is a derived view with expiry, not a stored entity. **Medium** for the control boundary β as a typed factor: it is `novel` with only trajectory-level precedents (ATIF `llm_call_count`, Omnigent `handles_tools_internally`); its representation is deferred to WS-A3 (OQ-WS-A2-04). No C0 recommendation rests on a `provisional` performance number.
