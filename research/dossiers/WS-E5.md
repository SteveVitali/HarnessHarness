## 1. Scope & boundary

This workstream decides **how a proposed tool call becomes an executed, captured, attributed effect** inside an execution environment: (i) the **execution contract** — the ordered operations `resolve → authorize → prepare → commit → execute → capture → observe`, mapped one-to-one onto the ADR-0030 effect lifecycle (`intended → authorized|refused → prepared → committed → observed | unknown …`); (ii) the **executor contract** — the operation-shaped component class `tool_executor` that runs a bound `ToolCapability` version against an environment handle and *reports* (never decides); (iii) the **helper boundary** — the final OQ-045 answer (out-of-process helper binary behind a narrow protocol) and the protocol's minimum verb set; (iv) the **effect capture data model** — every observable side-effect signal (output bytes, filesystem change, spawned process, egress decision, credential use, containment violation, resource usage) carried as a typed capture item stamped with `run_id ⊃ turn_id ⊃ model_call_id ⊃ tool_call_id` and `effect_id/attempt_no`, sealed into a content-addressed capture manifest referenced by `action.effect.observed`; (v) **result normalization and the error taxonomy** — one closed `ErrorClass` sum with a three-layer origin (`tool`, `execution`, `transport`), retryability *derived* from `(ErrorClass, effective_risk_class)`, model-facing text only as `Text{authority = external}`; (vi) **timeouts, cancellation and detached processes** — a deadline hierarchy computed by the kernel, two-phase interruption, class-dependent terminal semantics, detached children as first-class effects (answers OQ-090); (vii) **streaming outputs** — ephemeral deltas, explicit omission records, raw-output offload; (viii) the **credential-mediation obligations** an executor inherits from WS-H3 (placeholders in, redaction-at-source out); (ix) acceptance criteria — *attribution completeness* and *no unmediated effect* as veto-class invariants — and build-stage placement.

Left to neighbours (adopted verbatim, never re-decided): the effect lifecycle, risk class, idempotency key and compensation (WS-B2, ADR-0030/0031/0032); the ledger envelope, ids, offload and fencing (WS-B1, ADR-0026…0029); the `ToolCapability` record incl. `observation_contract.error_classes`, `execution_requirement`, `preconditions`, `scope_bindings`, `resources` and `bind` (WS-E1); the reference monitor's decision function, handle table, Π and TCB boundary (WS-H1); the containment policy record, enforcement points E1–E4, `ContainmentReport`, `enforcement_evidence`, egress mediation and the escape hatch (WS-H4); the credential broker, secret channels, placeholders and the leak battery (WS-H3); approval UX, leases and the reviewer chain (WS-H7); the audit-grade catalogue and producer rule (WS-H6); MCP/ACP transports and the `input_required` paused-effect mapping (WS-E4); the environment handle's lifecycle, image digests, snapshots and `action.environment.*` events (WS-B5 — **its dossier is absent at the time of writing**; this dossier fixes only what an executor needs from a handle and flags CF-212); recovery on resume (WS-B3); retry/backoff policy and budget enforcement (WS-F2); execution-alignment consumption of `unknown`/`abandoned` (WS-G2); every ecosystem choice (ADR-0050 — referenced only for host-ecosystem requirements and sandbox primitives, by id and layer).

## 4. Findings

### 4a. Mechanism evidence

**F1 — Every audited harness runs tool execution as an ordered pipeline with the decision made *outside* the executor, but none records the pipeline as durable phases.** Codex's `ToolOrchestrator::run` is literally "approval → select sandbox → attempt → retry with an escalated sandbox strategy on denial" (S-107 `orchestrator.rs` L3–7): the requirement is computed (`ExecApprovalRequirement ∈ {Skip{bypass_sandbox}, NeedsApproval{reason}, Forbidden{reason}}`), the sandbox is selected from a permission profile, the attempt runs under a `SandboxAttempt` record, and a `SandboxErr::Denied` triggers a *second attempt* with a fresh approval unless cached. OpenHands' `ParallelToolExecutor._run_safe` checks the cancel token, resolves lock keys from `DeclaredResources`, runs, and returns synthetic cancellation results (S-110 `parallel_executor.py` L275–310). goose's `handle_approval_tool_requests` yields an `ActionRequired` message, awaits confirmation, then dispatches (S-112 `tool_execution.rs` L149–244). What none of them persists is the phase boundary *before* dispatch — which ADR-0030 has already decided (`committed` write-ahead). **Implication:** the execution contract is the ADR-0030 lifecycle with three E5-owned operations inserted where the audited pipelines do work the lifecycle does not name: `resolve` (before `authorized`: canonical arguments via `SurfaceArgMap`, executor binding, deterministic assessment inputs), `execute` (between `committed` and `observed`: the helper round trip) and `capture` (concurrent with `execute`: the observed-effect stream). §6.1.

**F2 — Executors report facts they cannot vouch for, and the mature protocol says so in its types.** Codex's `ExecResponse.sandbox_type` is optional "because current peers report `None` when the process was not sandboxed" and *absent* when the peer did not report (S-107 `protocol.rs` L343–361); `ExecMetadata` is "attribution for executor telemetry, not authorization" (L271–279); `enforce_managed_network` without `managed_network` details means "the executor must continue to fail closed" (L309–317); `is_likely_sandbox_denied` is a heuristic over output (S-107 `exec.rs` L797). sandbox-runtime documents that on Linux "bubblewrap doesn't provide built-in violation reporting" (S-288 README §Violation Detection). Landlock logs denials only "if audit is enabled" (S-293). **Implication:** the kernel must separate `source ∈ {executor_reported, helper_observed, mediator_observed, kernel_derived}` on every capture item and treat completeness as measured (`complete | partial{reason} | unknown`), never assumed; WS-H4's `enforcement_evidence` tri-state is the same discipline one level down. §6.3, AC-E5-01.

**F3 — Attribution of a side effect to a tool call is a *token* problem, not a logging problem.** Codex passes `tool_call_id` into the executor as telemetry metadata (F2) and its network proxy keys decisions on an execution-scoped proxy with a cancellation token (`SandboxAttempt.network_denial_cancellation_token`, `network_proxy`, S-107 `sandboxing.rs` L403–404, `orchestrator.rs` L94–99); WS-H4 requires every `security.egress.decided` to carry an `effect_id` and denies "a connection without an attribution token, or with a token for another environment" (WS-H4 AC-H4-06). sandbox-runtime routes all traffic through host-side proxies reached over a bind-mounted unix socket or a fixed loopback port — the only channel out of the environment (S-288 README §Network Isolation). **Implication:** the kernel mints one opaque **attribution token** per `(run_id, effect_id, attempt_no)` at `prepare`, the helper delivers it to the child (environment slot or socket credential), and every observer (egress mediator, credential broker, helper fs layer) stamps the `effect_id` it resolves from the token — unresolvable ⇒ refuse (network, credential) or `unattributed` violation (filesystem, process). §6.3. (WS-H4's sidecar defines the token as "minted by the executor"; this dossier makes the kernel the minter — CF-213.)

**F4 — Three distinct failure origins are conflated in every audited result type, and the protocol that gets it right does so by construction.** MCP: "errors that originate from the tool SHOULD be reported inside the result with `isError`… any errors in *finding* the tool… should be reported as an MCP error response" (S-153 L1825–1836); WS-E4 found no audited client keeps that distinction in its ledger. Codex distinguishes `ToolError::{Rejected(String), Codex(CodexErr)}` and `SandboxErr::{Denied, Timeout, Signal}` (S-107 `sandboxing.rs` L357–361; `orchestrator.rs` L534–544) — execution failures are typed, but `Rejected` is a string and the model-facing text is free-form ("command failed; retry without sandbox?" L546–550). browser-use collapses everything into `RuntimeError(f'Error executing action …')` (S-122 `registry/service.py` L396–418). goose returns a fixed string constant for a declined tool (S-112 L135–137). OpenHands alone has a closed, frozen failure contract (`FailureKind`, `retryable`, `user_action`) with code-based classification *before* text heuristics (S-110 `error_classification.py`). **Implication:** the error taxonomy has three layers with one closed `ErrorClass` sum: `tool` (inside the observation; `isError`), `execution` (timeout, signal, cancelled, containment denied, output cap, environment lost), `transport` (helper/protocol failure). The model always receives a typed class plus a `Text{authority = external}` payload; `retryable` is derived by the kernel from `(ErrorClass, effective_risk_class)`, never asserted by the executor. §6.4.

**F5 — Cancellation is two-phase and class-blind in production; the class-aware part must be added.** Codex on cancel: `terminate_process_group` (TERM), 50 ms grace, then `kill_process_group`; on timeout: kill the group immediately, synthetic exit 124; then wait ≤ 2 s for output pipes because "grandchildren that inherited stdout/stderr… would cause `read()` to block indefinitely" (S-107 `exec.rs` L84–95, L996–1050). OpenHands `interrupt()` sends Ctrl-C from another thread and the "interrupted command finishes quickly rather than blocking until its original timeout" (S-110 `parallel_executor.py` L215–221). MCP's `notifications/cancelled` "MAY arrive after the request has already finished" and only says the result "will be unused" (S-153 L616–651); ACP's `$/cancel_request` is optional and may return partial results (S-035 cancellation.mdx). None of these consults the effect's reversibility. ADR-0030 already makes cancellation a `unknown{cause: cancelled}` cause; ADR-0031 forbids blind redispatch for irreversible effects. **Implication:** interruption is `interrupt(mode ∈ {graceful, kill})` with a per-class policy: `read_only` — kill, `observed(not_applied)`; `reversible` — graceful → kill → `observed(partial)` with baseline diff (probe by diff); `compensable`/`irreversible` — graceful only if the executor declares `interrupt = supported`, otherwise `unknown` → `probe`. Approval-wait cancellation is a *refusal* (WS-H7 AC-H7-06), never `unknown`. §6.5.

**F6 — Timeouts are a hierarchy of caps, and the safe direction is "shorter than the environment's own death".** Codex: a 10 s default with a model-supplied `timeout_ms` and `yield_time_ms` (S-107 `exec.rs` L60; `process_manager.rs` L605–615); OpenHands refuses a foreground command whose timeout exceeds 90 % of the runtime's idle-cleanup timeout, with a typed rejection telling the model to background the command (S-110 `timeout_policy.py`); ADR-0040 fires hard budget ceilings only at decision points and requires open effects to be closed first. **Implication:** the kernel computes `deadline = min(capability default, model-requested, budget `time.wall_ms` remaining, environment `idle_cap × ratio`, turn deadline)` at `prepare`, records it on `action.effect.prepared`, and the *helper* enforces it locally so a dead kernel never leaves a runaway process; a request whose floor exceeds the environment cap is refused `PreconditionViolated{timeout_exceeds_environment}` before dispatch. §6.5.

**F7 — Processes that outlive a tool call are a first-class object in the mature system and a hazard in the others.** Codex's unified exec stores the process "before the initial yield wait so interrupting the turn cannot drop the last Arc and terminate the background process", assigns a client-chosen `process_id`, returns partial output plus "Process running with session ID", exposes `list_processes`/`terminate_process`, and `terminate_all_processes` on session end (S-107 `process_manager.rs` L560–600, L1682–1735; `context.rs` L491–515). OpenHands' terminal is a tmux pool where a not-finished previous command is interrupted before reuse (S-110 `impl.py` L238–270). Codex's io-drain comment (F5) is the negative case: a grandchild nobody owns. **Implication (OQ-090):** a detached child is an effect of domain `spawn_process` with its own `effect_id` (`parent_effect_id` = the spawning effect), in state `committed` while it runs, owned by the environment handle (terminated on `detach`/run terminal), whose later output arrives as capture items under *its* `effect_id`; the spawning effect closes `observed(partial)` with `detached_effect_ids[]`. No separate lease at C0 (inherits the writer lease); resource-scoped leases are B2's C1 extension. §6.5.

**F8 — Streaming and "what the model sees" are already separated from "what is retained" in production, but retention is capped silently.** Codex keeps `raw_output` and a separate `log_output` budget ("the telemetry budget must not inherit the model's output-token limit"), marks omitted bytes with an explicit marker, caps retained bytes (`EXEC_OUTPUT_MAX_BYTES`) and live deltas (`MAX_EXEC_OUTPUT_DELTAS_PER_CALL`) (S-107 `context.rs` L363–374, L448–489; `exec.rs` L75–83); OpenHands saves full output to `full_output_save_dir`; goose redirects large responses to an owner-only file. WS-B1 makes streaming deltas `ephemeral` and offloads payloads above 64 KiB (ADR-0026/0029). **Implication:** output chunks are ephemeral events with a per-execution `seq`; the terminal observation carries the model-facing view (profile-owned truncation) and a `raw_output_ref` to the offloaded full bytes up to a *declared* retention cap; an omission is a typed record `truncation{omitted_bytes, policy_ref, original_size}` so completeness is never silent. §6.6.

**F9 — Concurrency safety is declared, tri-state, and world-change invalidates queued siblings.** OpenHands `DeclaredResources{declared=false}` ⇒ serialize the whole tool (S-110 `tool.py` L250–281; WS-E1 adopted the tri-state as `resources`); browser-use aborts the rest of a multi-action batch after a `terminates_sequence` action because the page changed (S-122 `views.py` L23–24). **Implication:** the dispatcher serializes effects with overlapping `resource_keys` and whole-capability serialization when `resources.declared = false`; a capture item `world_changed{scope}` from any effect re-validates `state_requires`/`path_within` preconditions of queued sibling proposals from the same model call and refuses stale ones with `PreconditionViolated{stale: effect_id}` (E1's precondition kinds, no new mechanism). §6.2.

**F10 — The helper boundary is a real protocol in the one system that ships it, and its shape answers OQ-045.** Codex `exec-server` is "a small JSON-RPC server for spawning and controlling subprocesses", the `codex` binary "owns hidden helper dispatch for sandboxed filesystem operations and `codex-linux-sandbox`", the server supports `resume_session_id`, local/remote/forward transports, and executor-side sandbox application ("Portable sandbox intent. Concrete wrapper argv is resolved by the exec-server") (S-107 `exec-server/README.md` L1–40; `protocol.rs` L283–321); `linux-sandbox` is a separate binary whose `main` is one line (S-107 `linux-sandbox/src/main.rs`). sandbox-runtime is a separate process wrapping the command (S-288). OpenHands executes in a Docker/Apptainer/remote workspace over an API (S-110 `openhands-workspace/`). Cost evidence: gVisor's user-space kernel and Firecracker's microVM both quantify the overhead of putting a boundary under the process (S-393/-03). **Implication:** OQ-045 final = out-of-process helper behind a narrow protocol (matches ADR-0049's provisional answer; no ADR-0050 revalidation trigger fires); the protocol's verb set is fixed in §6.7 and is the *same* boundary WS-H4's `ContainmentReport` and egress round trip use (one declaration per seam). Process handles are kernel-chosen `execution_id`s ("protocol key, not an OS pid"); the helper's session is resumable so `probe` after a kernel crash is a helper `read`, not a re-execution.

**F11 — Secret handling at the executor is two obligations, both precedented.** OpenHands masks every terminal observation through the conversation's secret registry before returning it (S-110 `impl.py` L360–384); browser-use substitutes placeholders only in the `input` action and only for the current URL's domain (S-122 `registry/service.py` L350–360); Codex `ExecEnvPolicy{inherit, exclude, include_only}` builds the child environment from an allow-list (S-107 `protocol.rs` L323–331). WS-H3 fixes the mechanism (placeholders in, `redact` as a kernel deriver before append). **Implication:** the executor receives only `CredentialBinding.placeholder`s through the helper; the kernel's `capture → observe` path runs WS-H3 `redact` on every capture item *before* it becomes a ledger event or a model-visible observation — the executor cannot skip it because it never appends. §6.3.

### 4b. Performance evidence (benchmark lifts) — each tagged `provisional` if 2026 single-team/preprint

- 84 % fewer permission prompts with filesystem + network sandboxing (S-290, Anthropic internal, Tier B, product-specific) — a hypothesis for AC-H4-11, not a design input here.
- gVisor: system-call and file-open overheads measured up to order-of-magnitude on microbenchmarks (S-394, HotCloud 2019, Tier A, older substrate); Firecracker: microVM boot ~125 ms, memory overhead < 5 MiB (S-393, NSDI 2020). Used only to justify measuring helper overhead (M-points) and a persistent helper session rather than per-call spawn.
- "Verified tool calls" duplicate reduction (S-204, `provisional`, simulated) — not load-bearing; the probe mechanism rests on ADR-0030.
- No audited system reports a measured attribution-completeness rate; this dossier makes it a class-scoped metric rather than citing a number.

## 5. Disconfirming evidence & negative results

- **Against "every side effect attributable".** Grandchild processes that inherit descriptors (S-107 `exec.rs` L84–95), unix-socket and domain-fronting channels (S-288 §Security Limitations), bubblewrap's missing violation reporting, and Landlock's audit-gated logging mean some effects are *unobservable* by any kernel-side component. *Why it does not overturn:* the recommendation is not "attribution is complete" but "attribution completeness is a measured, class-scoped veto metric with declared residuals": a run whose environment lacks an observer for a channel reports `completeness = partial{residual_channel}` (WS-H4's `residual_channels`), never `complete`; an *observed* signal without an `effect_id` is a violation event. The honest metric is the deliverable.
- **Against an out-of-process helper (OQ-045).** In-process syscall filtering has the lowest per-call overhead; the AIOS scaffold shows polyglot cost is real (ADR-0049 (c)); gVisor/Firecracker quantify boundary overhead (S-393/-03). *Why it does not overturn:* every product-grade harness read in source drives its sandbox from outside the loop's process (F10); WS-H1's TCB rule 7 and T-LCD-12 already assume it; the helper session is persistent (Codex `resume_session_id`), so the cost is per-session attach plus a per-call message, measured at M-points (AC-E5-11). Recorded as a C7/C12 input per ADR-0049, not a gate.
- **Against a closed error taxonomy.** MCP's rationale for `isError` is that the model must *see* the error to self-correct; browser-use attaches structured memory to errors; a code alone loses that. *Why it does not overturn:* the class is paired with a `Text{authority = external}` payload (T-LCD-02) rendered by the profile; the closed sum governs kernel decisions (retryability, lifecycle transition), the text governs model recovery. Surjectivity (every executor failure has a class) is E1's E5 obligation, tested (AC-E5-06).
- **Against class-aware interruption.** Reversibility of arbitrary commands is heuristic (ADR-0031 (c)); a "graceful" interrupt of a compensable effect may still leave it half-applied. *Why it does not overturn:* the class only chooses between *kill now* and *ask/probe first*; the `unknown → probe` path (ADR-0030) is the safety net; the executor's `interrupt` declaration is tri-state and `unknown` is treated as `unsupported` (T-LCD-07).
- **Against durable write-ahead plus helper round trip on every call.** Read-heavy coding workloads issue hundreds of fast reads. *Why it does not overturn:* `read_only` effects skip the barrier (ADR-0030 decision 2) and may batch through the helper's fs verbs; the cost is a Stage 3 measurement (AC-E5-11), and the Stage 0 baseline may run a single in-process executor with `enforcement_evidence = unknown` recorded (fail-closed for writes unless `warn_and_degrade` is declared — WS-H4 AC-H4-08).
- **Against kernel-derived retryability.** Executors (and OpenHands' contract) know whether *their* failure is transient. *Why it does not overturn:* the executor's `retryable_hint` is admitted as a raise-only input (it may make a failure *less* retryable, never more) — the same monotone rule ADR-0031 applies to risk hints.
- **Against typed capture for browser/GUI environments.** Browser-use's action space has no filesystem baseline or exit status; "world changed" is the only observable. *Why it does not overturn:* the capture item set is a closed sum with `world_changed{scope}` and `structured_result` as the minimum every executor can emit; per-environment-class richness is declared (`ExecutorDeclaration.capture_kinds`), and the Lab stratifies on it (T-LCD-15).
- **Negative results from the audit.** (i) No audited harness persists the *request* sent to its executor with the sandbox facts it relied on (Codex persists `ExecCommandBegin/End` only transiently — WS-H6 F5). (ii) No audited harness distinguishes tool `isError` from execution failure from transport failure in its durable record (WS-E4 negative (iii) confirmed here for Codex `ToolError::Rejected(String)`). (iii) No audited harness measures attribution completeness. (iv) Every audited output cap is silent about the retained-bytes cap in the durable record (Codex marks omission in the model text only).

## 6. Recommendation for the spec

### 6.1 The execution contract (C0 / Stage 1) — mapped onto ADR-0030

The brief's five verbs `prepare → authorize → execute → capture → observe` are refined into seven kernel-owned stages so that the order is consistent with ADR-0030 (authorization precedes B2's `prepared`). One tool call (`tool_call_id`) yields ≥ 1 effects; the stages run per effect, with `resolve` shared per call.

| E5 stage | ADR-0030 phase entered | who | operation (language-neutral) | records |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **resolve** | `intended` (already appended by the runtime on `action.tool.proposed`) | kernel dispatcher | `resolve(tool_call_id) → [EffectProposal]`: apply `SurfaceArgMap` (ADR-0022 E2) → canonical params; look up the pinned `ToolCapability` version (`resolve(mode = execute)`, WS-E1); select the `ExecutorBinding` for the run's environment (`bind`, WS-E1); run the **deterministic assessors** the executor registers (path canonicalisation after link resolution against the handle's writable roots; command classification by the closed grammar; host classification) and record their inputs by content address; derive `declared: EffectClass` per effect and `resource_keys` from `scope_bindings`/`resources`; compute `deadline` (§6.5). Failures: `UnmappedArgument`, `PreconditionViolated{kind}`, `CapabilityUnresolvable`, `BindFailure{…}` (E1) — each ends as `action.effect.refused{reason, decider = kernel}` and a typed refusal observation. | `assessment_inputs_ref`; `effect_id` allocation per ordinal (B1 rule) |
| **authorize** | `authorized` \| `refused` | reference monitor (WS-H1 `authorize`, checks 0–8) with H4's `admits()` precondition | E5 supplies the `Proposal` fields (`params_canonical`, `declared`, `args_provenance`, `self_report?`) and consumes `KernelDecision{effective_risk_class, allow \| ask \| deny, handle_ids[], cache_key?}`; `ask` is H7's. | **execute** | (between `committed` and terminal) | executor via the helper boundary | `execute(ExecutionRequest) → execution_id` where `ExecutionRequest = {effect_id, attempt_no, execution_id (kernel-chosen), commit_token \| read_only_marker, capability_version, canonical_args, env_handle, containment_policy_ref, attribution_token, credential_bindings[] (placeholders), deadline, grace_ms, drain_ms, output_policy, resource_reservation_ref, idempotency_key, dedup_hint}`. Global invariants (kernel-enforced; errors never warnings):
- **I-1 No unmediated effect.** For every non-`read_only` effect: `security.permission.decided{allow}` precedes `action.effect.committed` precedes `action.tool.started` in `seq` (H1 complete mediation + ADR-0030 barrier), *and* the helper has refused every request lacking a valid `CommitToken`. Static check on the ledger (`audit_view` obligation, H6 Rule O) + helper conformance test.
- **I-2 No ambient channel.** The environment has `net.mode ∈ {none, mediated}` (H4), credential placeholders only (H3 SV-4), and no kernel identity material; the attribution token is *not* a credential (possession proves nothing to any external party).
- **I-3 Executors report, never decide.** No executor operation returns a permission, a risk class that *lowers*, an authority label, or a retryability that *raises*; the kernel derives all four. (H1 §6.5: executors are outside the TCB.)
- **I-4 One observation per attempt.** Exactly one terminal (`observed` or `unknown`) per `(effect_id, attempt_no)`; a late helper report after `unknown` is recorded as `action.effect.probed{verdict, evidence_refs}`, never as a second observation (MCP "result will be unused" precedent).
- **I-5 Completeness is explicit.** `action.effect.observed.completeness ∈ {complete, partial{reasons ⊆ {truncated, unobserved_channel(kind), drain_timeout, executor_unreported}}, unknown}`; never absent.
- **I-6 Deadline dominance.** Every effect's `deadline` ≤ the environment's own liveness bound; the helper enforces it locally and reports `timeout` even if the kernel is gone.

### 6.2 The executor contract (component class `tool_executor`, ADR-0023 slot; T-LCD-08/-12)

Operations (out-of-process by construction; the canonical encoding on every boundary):
- `declare() → ExecutorDeclaration{executor_id, version_identity, environment_class ∈ {kernel_internal, sandbox_helper, environment_handle, remote_server, provider_hosted} (WS-E1), isolation_min, capture_kinds ⊆ CaptureKind, interrupt ∈ {supported, unsupported, unknown}, partial_application: bool, streaming: bool, dedup_support ∈ {none, executor, target}, dedup_window?, probe_support ∈ {read_back, postcondition, none}, detached_processes: bool, meters ⊆ KernelDimension, on_kernel_loss ∈ {terminate, preserve_until(ttl)}, assessors: [AssessorRef]}` — tri-state fields are never coerced (T-LCD-07). `bind` (WS-E1) validates `dedup_support_required ≤ dedup_support` and `isolation_min`.
- `prepare(effect_id, capability_version, canonical_args, env_handle) → {baseline_ref?, resource_keys[], executor_state_ref?} | BaselineUnavailable | EnvironmentUnavailable` — executor-side preparation only (the kernel owns the ledger records).
- `execute(ExecutionRequest) → execution_id | NotCommitted | AttributionMismatch | EnvironmentUnavailable | InvalidRequest` — non-blocking accept.
- `capture(execution_id, after_seq) → Stream<CaptureItem>` — inclusive cursor; ordered `seq`; ends with `terminal`.
- `interrupt(execution_id, mode ∈ {graceful, kill}) → {acknowledged, already_exited, unsupported}`.
- `write_input(execution_id, bytes, write_id) → {accepted, closed, unknown}` — for interactive executors (Codex `write_id` dedup precedent); every write is an `output_chunk{stream: stdin}` capture item.
- `probe(effect_id, attempt_no, idempotency_key) → ProbeVerdict{applied | not_applied | undeterminable, evidence_refs[]}` — implements ADR-0030 `probe` for the capability's declared `probe_support`.
- `snapshot(env_handle) → SnapshotRef` / `diff(env_handle, baseline_ref) → FsChangeSet` — where `capture_kinds ∋ fs_change` (B4/B5 own the mechanism).
- `list_detached(env_handle) → [{effect_id, execution_id, state}]`, `terminate_detached(execution_id)` — §6.5.
- `close(env_handle)` — releases executor resources; the helper terminates every child unless `on_kernel_loss = preserve_until`.
Inputs every contract receives per T-LCD-08: `(ModelProfile, ResourceAccount)` — the executor uses the profile only to select a **result renderer** surface (E1 surface field); effects and captured facts are profile-independent (ADR-0022 E1–E3).

Concurrency (F9): the kernel dispatcher runs effects of one model call in parallel iff their `resource_keys` are disjoint and `resources.declared = true`; `declared = false` ⇒ serialize the capability; a `world_changed` item re-validates queued siblings' static and `state_requires` preconditions and refuses stale ones (`PreconditionViolated{stale_after: effect_id}`).

### 6.3 Effect capture data model (C0 / Stage 1; per-kind richness by executor class)

`CaptureItem = {run_id, turn_id, model_call_id, tool_call_id, effect_id, attempt_no, execution_id, seq, ts_mono, source ∈ {executor_reported, helper_observed, mediator_observed, kernel_derived}, kind, payload, provenance: ProvenanceRecord}` with `kind` a closed sum:
- `output_chunk{stream ∈ {stdout, stderr, pty, stdin, structured}, bytes_ref | bytes, byte_offset}` — **ephemeral** (`durability = ephemeral`, B1); the concatenation up to `output_policy.retain_bytes_cap` is offloaded as `raw_output_ref` at `observe`; the count of dropped bytes is `truncation.omitted_bytes`.
- `progress{token?, message: Text(external), fraction?}` — ephemeral (MCP `notifications/progress`, L3).
- `fs_change{path_canonical, change ∈ {created, modified, deleted, renamed{from}, mode_changed}, before_ref?, after_ref?, inside_writable_roots: bool}` — durable-by-manifest; produced by `diff(baseline_ref)` for `reversible` effects (`kernel_derived`) or by the helper fs layer (`helper_observed`).
- `process{event ∈ {spawned, exited{exit_status}, signalled{signal}, detached}, child_execution_id, argv_hash, cwd_canonical}` — durable-by-manifest; a `detached` event allocates a child `effect_id` (§6.5).
- `egress{decision_ref: EventRef (security.egress.decided), host_norm, port, protocol, bytes_out?, bytes_in?}` — `mediator_observed` (H4 supplies the event; E5 links it by `effect_id` from the attribution token).
- `credential_use{used_ref: EventRef (security.credential.used)}` — `mediator_observed` (H3).
- `containment_violation{violated_ref: EventRef}` — `helper_observed` (H4 `security.containment.violated`).
- `resource_usage{dimension ∈ KernelDimension ∪ registered ext, amount, measured_at: M-point}` — `time.wall_ms`, `env.active_ms`, `network.calls` always; `cpu_ms`, bytes as registered `ext` (CF-137 pending; OQ-118).
- `world_changed{scope ∈ {fs(paths), page(url), external(target)}, evidence_ref}` — the minimum every executor class can emit (browser-use `terminates_sequence` generalised).
- `structured_result{schema_ref, value_ref}` — the `output_schema`-conformant result (MCP `structuredContent`).
- `terminal{status ∈ {ok, error(ErrorClass)}, exit_status?, executor_outcome_hint ∈ {applied, not_applied, partial, unknown}, retryable_hint?: false, detail: Text(external)}` — `executor_reported`; the kernel decides the outcome (§6.4).

`EffectCaptureManifest{effect_id, attempt_no, execution_id, items: [CaptureItem − output_chunk], raw_output_ref?, truncation?: {omitted_bytes, original_size, policy_ref}, completeness, observers_present: set<CaptureKind>, residual_channels_ref (H4)}` — a content-addressed blob (`capture_manifest_ref` on `action.effect.observed`), hashed under `idp/1`; ephemeral chunks are excluded so two runs with identical effects hash identically regardless of chunking.

**Attribution token.** `mint_attribution(run_id, effect_id, attempt_no, env_handle) → AttributionToken` — opaque (≥ 128 bits of entropy), single-use per attempt, stored only as `token_hash` in the ledger, delivered by the helper into the child's environment slot and/or the bridged channel's peer credential, expiring at the effect's terminal event. `resolve_attribution(token) → {effect_id, attempt_no} | Unknown`. The egress mediator (H4) and credential broker (H3) *refuse* on `Unknown`; the helper fs/process layer records `unattributed`. Kernel-minted (F3; CF-213 against WS-H4's "executor-minted" wording — one minter, the kernel, so hosted `container-installed` mechanisms simply have none and report `n/a{observability}`).

### 6.4 Result normalization and the error taxonomy (C0 / Stage 1)

`Observation` (the HIR `Observation` payload, B1 §6.3) as produced by `observe`:
`{effect_id, attempt_no, tool_call_id, outcome ∈ {applied, not_applied, partial}, status ∈ {ok, error{class: ErrorClass, origin ∈ {tool, execution, transport}, detail_ref: Text(external), retryable: bool (kernel-derived)}}, exit_status?, content: [ContentBlock] (model-facing view, profile-rendered, truncation-aware), structured?: value_ref, raw_output_ref?, truncation?, artifacts: [ContentAddress], detached_effect_ids[], postcondition_results[], cost: {per dimension}, timing: {dispatched_at, first_output_at?, terminal_at, drain_ms}, completeness, provenance{origin = tool, authority: derived per WS-E1 §6.2, taint, readers}}`.

**`ErrorClass` (closed; extends WS-E1's list by dialect bump, CF-215):** `invalid_arguments, precondition_violated{kind}, not_found, conflict, timeout, environment_unavailable, permission_refused{reason}, rate_limited, partial_application, executor_error, cancelled{by ∈ {principal, budget, lease, run_end, protocol}}, containment_denied{kind}, signalled{signal}, output_cap_exceeded, protocol_error, unattributed`. Origin rule: `tool` ⇐ the tool's own result (`isError`, non-zero exit with output, validation inside the tool); `execution` ⇐ `timeout, cancelled, containment_denied, signalled, output_cap_exceeded, environment_unavailable` reported by the helper/kernel; `transport` ⇐ `protocol_error, executor_error` (helper unreachable, malformed report). Mapping to the lifecycle (kernel rule, not executor choice):

| origin × class | `read_only` | `reversible` (baseline) | `compensable` / `irreversible` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tool` error | `observed(not_applied)` | `observed(not_applied \| partial)` by `diff(baseline)` | `observed(not_applied)` if the executor's hint is `not_applied` and `probe_support ≠ none` confirms; else `unknown` → probe |
| `execution` timeout / signalled / cancelled | `observed(not_applied)` | `observed(partial)` with `fs_change[]` from diff | `unknown{cause}` → probe (ADR-0030); never redispatched for `irreversible` |
| `execution` containment_denied | `observed(not_applied)` + `security.containment.violated` | same | same — and the model's next proposal may *propose* an amendment (H4 §6.7) |
| `transport` | `observed(not_applied)` if no dispatch is proven; else `unknown{cause: executor_error}` | `unknown` | `unknown` |

`retryable = f(class, origin, effective_risk_class)`: true for `tool`-origin `rate_limited`, `conflict`, `timeout` on `read_only`/`reversible`; `precondition_violated{stale}` after re-resolution; false for `permission_refused`, `containment_denied`, `cancelled{by ∈ {principal, budget}}`, and for every class on `irreversible`; an executor `retryable_hint` may only set it to false. F2 consumes it for backoff; H7's `retry_of` re-review applies when the class rose.

Model-facing rendering: the profile's `error_format` surface renders `(class, origin, detail)`; the reason enum is typed (H1 output rule); the detail is a `Text{authority = external}` leaf (T-LCD-02) after H3 `redact`. For MCP-lowered surfaces, `status.error` lowers to `isError: true` with the class in `_meta` (declared loss `hint_only` where the target has no slot — T-LCD-11).

### 6.5 Timeouts, cancellation, detached processes (C0 / Stage 1–2)

- **Deadline hierarchy** (computed at `prepare`, recorded on `prepared`): `deadline = min(capability.cost_model?.time.wall_ms.p95 × k \| executor default, model_requested?, budget.remaining(time.wall_ms), env.idle_cap × idle_ratio (default 0.9), turn.deadline?)`; `grace_ms` (default 50 ms… configurable) between graceful and kill; `drain_ms` (default 2 000 ms) to collect output from descendants after kill. All three are **MUST-data** parameters; a model-conditioned default is a conditioned rule with a debt record (T-LCD-05). A request whose *floor* (capability-declared minimum) exceeds any cap is refused `precondition_violated{timeout_exceeds_environment}` with the typed hint "background it" (OpenHands precedent).
- **Enforcement locus.** The helper enforces `deadline` locally (I-6) and reports `terminal{status: error(timeout)}`; the kernel independently marks `unknown{cause: timeout}` if no terminal arrives by `deadline + grace_ms + drain_ms + protocol_slack`. A timeout on a `read_only`/`reversible` effect is an *observation* (partial output kept); on `compensable`/`irreversible` `external` effects it is `unknown` (the S-204 post-dispatch failure model, `provisional` as a name only).
- **Cancellation sources and semantics.** `cancel(effect_id | run, by)` from: principal (turn cancel; ACP `session/cancel`; MCP `notifications/cancelled` inbound when hosting), hard budget at a decision point (ADR-0040: never mid-effect — the run ends *after* open effects close), writer-lease fencing (B1), run terminal, executor loss. Procedure: `interrupt(graceful)` → wait `grace_ms` → `interrupt(kill)` → drain `drain_ms` → terminal per §6.4 row "cancelled". For `compensable`/`irreversible` with `interrupt = unsupported \| unknown` the kernel does **not** kill; it waits until `deadline` then marks `unknown` (killing could leave an external effect half-applied with no evidence). Cancellation while awaiting approval is `action.effect.refused{reason: cancelled}` (H7 AC-H7-06). The model always receives a typed `cancelled` observation with partial output (Codex `AbortedToolOutput`, `success: None` — mapped to `outcome = not_applied \| partial`, never a silent success).
- **Detached processes (answers OQ-090).** A process the executor reports as `process{detached}` becomes a child effect `{domain: spawn_process, parent_effect_id, effect_id, state: committed}` under the run's writer lease, owned by the environment handle: listed by `list_detached`, terminable by the principal or the kernel, terminated at `detach(env_handle)`/run terminal (`terminate_all_processes` precedent), with an explicit `observed(not_applied)` or `observed(applied){exit_status}` when it exits. Its later output arrives as `output_chunk` items under the child `effect_id` and is readable by the model through the capability's surface (`read(execution_id)` — Codex `write_stdin`/poll shape). The spawning effect closes `observed(partial)` with `detached_effect_ids[]`. No child leases at C0; resource-scoped leases with progress payloads are ADR-0030's C1 extension (B3 resumes them). `on_kernel_loss` decides what the helper does when the kernel dies: `terminate` (Stage 1 default) or `preserve_until(ttl)` (C1, for B3 resume with `resume_session_id`).
- **Dedup store (answers OQ-087).** C0: the `(idempotency_key → Observation)` map is a ledger projection `project(run, effects_by_key)` — a repeat `commit` with a key that already has `observed(applied)` returns the stored observation without dispatch (kernel-side dedup, retention = run retention). C1/Stage 2: executor-side stores for `dedup_support = executor` with a declared `dedup_window`; when the window lapses mid-retry the kernel falls back to `probe` (never redispatch). `target` forwarding is E4's Stage 4.

### 6.6 Streaming outputs (C0 / Stage 1)

Chunks are ephemeral `output_chunk` items on `subscribe` (surfaces, K-track), never in `read` (AC-B1-12); rate-capped per execution (`output_policy.max_deltas`, Codex 10 000 precedent) without affecting retention. Retention: `output_policy{retain_bytes_cap (default = 1 MiB, MUST-data), model_view: profile-owned truncation (head/tail; token budget), offload_above (ADR-0029 threshold)}`; the raw bytes up to the cap are one blob (`raw_output_ref`), the model-facing view is rendered by the profile's `result_renderer`, and the omission is a typed `truncation` record on the observation and the manifest (F8). Structured streaming (`progress`, MCP `progressToken`) is L3 ephemeral (ADR-0044). Long-running commands use the detached-child pattern (§6.5) rather than a blocking stream: the initial effect yields after `yield_ms` with `observed(partial)` + child.

### 6.7 The helper boundary — OQ-045 final answer and protocol minimum (C0 schema Stage 1; binary Stage 2)

**Decision:** the sandbox helper is a **separate helper binary behind a narrow protocol**, confirming ADR-0049's provisional answer; no ADR-0050 revalidation trigger fires. The kernel *drives* isolation primitives through it; the ADR-0050 helper ecosystem can host seccomp/Landlock/Seatbelt/job objects in-thread (ADR-0050 §8 (c) reference only). Requirements the boundary contract must satisfy (stated as properties; WS-K4/L5 own the §6.5 boundary grammar): one persistent session per environment handle with `resume(session_id)`; request/response plus ordered notifications; byte-exact chunks; the canonical encoding for every record that is hashed; version handshake with capability declaration (Codex `EnvironmentCapabilities` precedent; T-LCD-07).

Minimum verbs (one declaration per seam — WS-H4's `ContainmentReport` and egress round trip ride the same protocol; CF-134/-05 apply to B5): `hello(client, resume_session_id?) → {session_id, helper_identity, capabilities}`; `attach(env_handle, ContainmentPolicy by content address + inline) → ContainmentReport` (H4 §6.3); `exec(ExecutionRequest) → execution_id | NotCommitted | AttributionMismatch`; `read(execution_id, after_seq, max_bytes, wait_ms) → {chunks[], next_seq, exited, exit_status?, failure?: ErrorClass, sandbox_denied?: bool}` and the equivalent notifications; `write_input`; `signal(execution_id, interrupt | terminate | kill)`; `terminate(execution_id) → {running}`; `fs.{read, write, canonicalize, metadata, read_dir, remove}` under the same sandbox context (for `read_only`/`fs_write` capabilities that need no process); `snapshot`/`diff` where supported; `egress.decision_request → allow | deny | ask` (helper → kernel; fail closed on no answer — H4); `list_detached`, `detach(env_handle)`. Every helper report carries `enforcement_evidence` per field group and a lowering-loss list for what the backend cannot enforce or observe (H4). The Stage-0 spike (ADR-0049 S1 "one sandboxed tool call through a helper-binary boundary") is the acceptance check for this section.

### 6.8 Credential mediation obligations (C0 / Stage 1; with WS-H3)

Executors receive `CredentialBinding.placeholder`s in `ExecutionRequest.credential_bindings` and never a value (SV-4); the helper builds the child environment from the definition's allow-list (Codex `ExecEnvPolicy` shape) with the kernel's non-inheritable list removed; the kernel's capture path runs `redact` (H3, a `kernel` deriver) on every `CaptureItem` before it is appended, streamed or rendered (SV-2/SV-3) — placement in the kernel rather than the executor is deliberate so that an untrusted executor cannot skip it; a `canary` hit anywhere in a capture stream yields `security.secret.leak_detected` and the `secret_leak` veto. Egress-carried credentials are injected by H4's mediator only for requests whose attribution token resolves to an effect holding a `secret_access` grant covering that destination (SV-5/SV-8) — the token links the two mediators without giving the environment either credential.

### 6.9 Acceptance-criteria sketch

- **AC-E5-01 Attribution completeness (veto).** For every run in the Stage 3 suite: every `security.egress.decided`, `security.credential.used`, `security.containment.violated`, `fs_change` inside writable roots and `process{spawned}` resolves to an `effect_id` whose `action.effect.committed` (or `read_only` `intended`) precedes it in `seq`; a seeded unattributed connection (no token / another environment's token) is denied and recorded `unattributed`; `attribution_completeness{applies_to: native, requires_observability: ledger, veto: true}` = 1 for environments whose `residual_channels = ∅`, and `partial{…}` is reported (never 1) otherwise. (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-E5-02 No unmediated effect.** (a) Static: no `action.tool.started` without a preceding `committed` (non-`read_only`) and `decided{allow}`; (b) conformance: a helper receiving `exec` with a forged/expired/absent `CommitToken` refuses `NotCommitted` and the environment shows no change; (c) fault injection: kill the kernel between `committed` and `exec`, and between `exec` and `observed`; on resume every such effect is `unknown` then resolved by `probe` via `resume(session_id)`; no duplicate `applied`. (C0/Stage 1 static; Stage 3 executable — shares kill points with AC-B1-2/AC-B2-01)
- **AC-E5-03 Error surjectivity and origin.** Every failure an executor can produce (fixture list per executor class: tool `isError`, non-zero exit, timeout, SIGKILL, sandbox denial, helper crash, malformed report, output cap) maps to exactly one `(ErrorClass, origin)`; the model-facing text is a `Text{authority = external}` leaf; `retryable` equals the §6.4 function; an executor `retryable_hint = true` never raises it. (C0/Stage 1)
- **AC-E5-04 Timeout and cancel per class.** A `read_only` timeout → `observed(not_applied)` with partial output; a `reversible` timeout → `observed(partial)` with a non-empty `fs_change[]` equal to `diff(baseline)`; a `compensable` external timeout → `unknown → probe`; an `irreversible` cancel with `interrupt = unsupported` is never killed before `deadline` and never redispatched; a cancel during approval is `refused`. Grandchildren holding pipes do not stall `observe` beyond `drain_ms`. (C0/Stage 1–3)
- **AC-E5-05 Streaming and retention.** Chunks appear on `subscribe` and never in `read`; the raw blob equals the first `retain_bytes_cap` bytes; `truncation.omitted_bytes` equals the dropped count; two runs producing identical effects with different chunking have identical `capture_manifest_ref`. (C0/Stage 1)
- **AC-E5-06 Detached children.** A backgrounded process yields a child effect in `committed` with `parent_effect_id`; its exit yields `observed`; run end terminates it (`on_kernel_loss = terminate`) or preserves it (C1) and B3 resume re-attaches; the model sees `detached_effect_ids[]` and can read the child's output. (C0/Stage 2; C1 preserve)
- **AC-E5-07 Deadline dominance.** With the environment idle cap at T, a request with floor > 0.9 T is refused before dispatch with the typed hint; with the kernel killed, the helper still terminates the process at `deadline`. (C0/Stage 2)
- **AC-E5-08 Redaction placement.** LT-01/LT-03 (H3) pass with the executor replaced by a hostile variant that returns the placeholder's value verbatim: the capture path masks it and raises `leak_detected`. (C0/Stage 1)
- **AC-E5-09 Dedup and probe.** Repeating `commit` with an existing key returns the stored observation without dispatch; lapsed executor window ⇒ `probe`, never redispatch; `probe` via `resume` reproduces `exit_status` of a completed exec. (C0/Stage 1 ledger tier; C1 executor tier)
- **AC-E5-10 World-change invalidation.** Two sibling proposals from one model call where the first emits `world_changed{fs(paths)}` overlapping the second's `path_within` precondition: the second is refused `PreconditionViolated{stale_after}` and re-proposable. (C0/Stage 2)
- **AC-E5-11 Helper overhead measured.** M-points around `exec`/`read` report `harness_overhead.execution_ms` per effect; the Stage 3 suite publishes distributions per executor class and isolation class (T-LCD-14: same budget across arms `in-process baseline` vs `helper`). (C0/Stage 3)
- **AC-E5-12 Executor declarations.** `bind` refuses `dedup_support_required > dedup_support`, `isolation_min > handle.isolation_class`; `interrupt = unknown` is treated as `unsupported` and reported as `unknown` in the catalog (T-LCD-07). (C0/Stage 1)
- **AC-E5-13 Hosted participants.** A `container-installed` participant's tool activity maps to the lifecycle via the ACP five-status mapping (ADR-0030 (d)); attribution and capture metrics render `n/a{observability}`; no native contract changes (T-LCD-06/-15). (C1/Stage 4)

## 10. Confidence

**High** for the stage order, "executors report, never decide", the out-of-process helper (OQ-045), the write-ahead-gated `execute` with a commit token, two-phase interruption, explicit truncation and the three-origin error taxonomy: each rests on Tier-A principles (complete mediation, sagas/leases/idempotence) and on source read at pinned commits in four production harnesses plus one shipped sandbox runtime that independently converge (Codex is the closest single precedent for the helper protocol and cancellation; OpenHands for interruption and the closed failure contract). **Medium** for the capture-item sum and the completeness metric: attribution via a kernel-minted token is precedented in parts (Codex `ExecMetadata`, H4's egress attribution, sandbox-runtime's single channel) but the integrated manifest and the measured completeness are novel and their observer coverage per backend is unmeasured until Stage 3. **Medium** for the deadline hierarchy defaults and detached-process ownership rules (precedented by Codex/OpenHands; class-aware behaviour is this dossier's addition and its approval/probe volume on shell-heavy suites is a Stage 3 measurement). **Low** only for any performance number: helper overhead figures come from container-runtime literature on other substrates and the 84 % prompt reduction is a product statistic; neither is load-bearing.
